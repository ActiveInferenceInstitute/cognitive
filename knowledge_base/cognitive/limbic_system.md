# Limbic System

## Overview
The limbic system is a collective name for interconnected structures on the medial surface of the cerebral hemispheres and beneath them, treated as a substrate of emotion, motivation and some forms of memory. Broca described a "limbic lobe" of cortex encircling the brainstem in the nineteenth century. Papez (1937) proposed a circuit linking the hippocampus, mammillary bodies, anterior thalamus and cingulate cortex as a mechanism of emotion, and later work added the amygdala, septal area and orbitofrontal cortex under the label limbic system.

## Components and functions
Structures commonly included are:
- the [[amygdala]], associated with threat detection and emotional learning;
- the [[hippocampus]], essential for forming new episodic and spatial memories;
- the cingulate gyrus, including the [[anterior_cingulate]];
- the hypothalamus, which controls autonomic and endocrine output and drives such as feeding;
- the septal nuclei, mammillary bodies and anterior thalamic nuclei.

These regions are heavily interconnected with the brainstem, the insula and prefrontal cortex, and they influence autonomic, hormonal and behavioural responses to events that matter for survival and reproduction.

## A contested category
Many neuroscientists regard the limbic system as a historical convenience rather than a natural unit. Criteria for membership differ between authors, some included regions have little to do with emotion, and key emotional processes involve areas outside the group, such as the insula and ventromedial prefrontal cortex. LeDoux (2000) argued that there is no single emotion system and that different emotions rely on different circuits, such as the circuit for defensive responses that centres on the amygdala (see [[emotion_circuits]]). The term persists in teaching and clinical language as shorthand for the subcortical and medial networks that link bodily state, affect and memory.

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is indirect. Active Inference does not use the limbic system as a theoretical category, but accounts of affect in terms of interoceptive and homeostatic inference attribute a central role to many of its components, including the cingulate, hypothalamus and amygdala as regulators of bodily and motivational priors, with valence linked to the rate of change of free energy in some proposals. These are theoretical proposals under development, and the mapping of formal variables onto limbic structures is a hypothesis. See [[emotional_processing]].
