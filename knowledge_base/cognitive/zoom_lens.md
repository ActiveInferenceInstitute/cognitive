# Zoom Lens

## Overview
The zoom lens model describes visual spatial attention as a region of enhanced processing whose size can be adjusted. A narrow focus yields high processing efficiency inside a small area, while a broad focus covers more of the visual field but with lower resolution, as if a fixed amount of processing resource were spread over a larger area. It extends the earlier spotlight metaphor, in which attention is a beam of fixed size that moves across the field (see [[spotlight]]), by adding a variable size (Eriksen and St. James, 1986).

## Evidence
- **Flanker interference.** In the flanker task, observers respond to a target while irrelevant items flank it. Incompatible flankers slow the response, and the size of this interference depends on how close the flankers are to the target and on how widely attention is distributed. Eriksen and Yeh (1985) found that a reliable cue to the target location reduced interference from nearby items.
- **Size-dependent efficiency.** Eriksen and St. James (1986) varied the size of a precue and reported that performance declined as the cued region increased in size, which they interpreted as a reduction in the resources available per unit area.
- **Neural correlates.** Imaging studies have reported that the spatial extent of enhanced cortical activity in retinotopic visual areas follows the size of the attended region.

## Competing accounts
Other findings suggest that attention can be divided across separate locations, which a single lens of continuous extent would not allow (Awh and Pashler, 2000), and that it can select whole objects regardless of the space they occupy (see [[visual_attention]]). The zoom lens is therefore regarded as one description of spatial selection, and not as a complete theory of attention.

## Relation to Active Inference
Feldman and Friston (2010) described attention as the optimisation of precision, the expected reliability of sensory prediction errors, so that attended channels have more influence on belief updating (see [[precision_weighting]]). A zoom lens then corresponds to a spatial profile of precision that is narrow or broad, and the tradeoff between extent and resolution is consistent with a limited total precision to be allocated. This reading restates the metaphor in the vocabulary of the theory and does not by itself make new predictions.
