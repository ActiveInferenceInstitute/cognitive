# Extinction

## Overview
Extinction, in the neuropsychological sense, is a failure to report a stimulus on the side opposite a brain lesion when another stimulus is presented at the same time on the intact side, even though the same stimulus is detected normally when presented alone. It was described for touch and later for vision and hearing. In conditioning, the same word names the weakening of a learned response when the predictive stimulus is no longer followed by its outcome, which is covered under [[fear_conditioning]].

## Features
Testing uses double simultaneous stimulation: the clinician touches both hands, or flashes lights on both sides, and the patient reports only the ipsilesional stimulus. Extinction is most often seen after damage to the right parietal lobe or the temporoparietal junction, and may be present in patients with only mild or recovered neglect. It therefore dissociates from [[spatial_neglect]], though the two commonly co-occur (Driver and Vuilleumier, 2001). Extinction can occur within a single modality or across modalities, and is affected by grouping: stimuli that form a perceptual unit are less likely to be extinguished. Some extinguished stimuli can still produce implicit effects, such as priming or neural responses in visual cortex, which indicates that processing is partly preserved while awareness is lost.

## Competition accounts
A leading explanation is that stimuli compete for neural representation and attention, and that the lesion biases the competition against the contralesional item. This follows the biased competition framework developed for normal visual attention, in which top-down and bottom-up signals resolve competition between simultaneous stimuli (Desimone and Duncan, 1995). Imbalance between the hemispheres in attention networks provides a related view of neglect and extinction (Corbetta and Shulman, 2011).

## Relation to Active Inference
Predictive formulations recast attention as the optimisation of precision, so that competition becomes competition between the precision-weighted prediction errors from different locations. Damage that lowers the precision or prior weighting of one side would reproduce extinction, and simulations of Active Inference agents with disconnections reproduce neglect-like behaviour (see [[spatial_neglect]]). The connection is an active theoretical proposal with simulation support and few direct empirical tests specific to extinction.
