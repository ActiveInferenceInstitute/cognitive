# Nelson and Narens Metamemory Framework

## Overview
The framework proposed by Thomas Nelson and Louis Narens (1990) is the standard organising scheme for research on metamemory, which is knowledge about and regulation of one's own memory. It is a general account of [[metacognition]] built on two interacting levels. The object level carries out cognitive activities such as learning and remembering. The meta level contains a model of the object level, and it receives information from it and sends instructions to it.

## Monitoring and control
- **Monitoring** is information flowing from the object level to the meta level. It informs the meta-level model about the state of the object level, for instance how well an item has been learned.
- **Control** is flow from the meta level to the object level. It changes ongoing processing, for example by initiating, continuing or terminating an activity.

Nelson and Narens analysed these flows across three stages of memory: acquisition, retention and retrieval. Examples of monitoring are ease-of-learning judgments before study, judgments of learning during or after study (see [[judgment_of_learning]]), feeling-of-knowing judgments for items that cannot currently be recalled (see [[feeling_of_knowing]]) and confidence in retrieved answers (see [[confidence_judgment]]). Examples of control are selection of items and allocation of study time, choice of strategy, deciding when to stop searching memory and deciding whether to report an answer.

## Significance
The framework turned metamemory from a collection of phenomena into a set of testable relations between monitoring and control. Studies using it have shown that learners often allocate study time according to their judgments of learning, and that judgments can be inaccurate when they are based on fluency or familiarity instead of retrievability. The scheme has been applied to education, ageing and clinical populations, and extended to the study of metacognition in non-human animals. Flavell (1979) provides the earlier conceptual background for the field.

## Relation to Active Inference
A conceptual parallel exists. Monitoring resembles estimating the reliability or precision of first-order beliefs, and control resembles selecting mental actions, such as directing attention, by means of beliefs at a higher level of a hierarchical model. Sandved-Smith and colleagues (2021) formalised meta-awareness and attentional control with deep parametric Active Inference models along these lines. The Nelson and Narens framework itself was not derived from such models, and the correspondence is an interpretation.
