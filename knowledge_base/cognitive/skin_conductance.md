# Skin Conductance

## Overview
Skin conductance, also called electrodermal activity, is the ease with which a weak electric current passes between two electrodes placed on the skin, usually on the fingers or palm. Conductance rises when the eccrine sweat glands fill with sweat, because salty fluid conducts better than dry skin. These glands are controlled by the sympathetic branch of the autonomic nervous system, with no significant parasympathetic input, so skin conductance gives a relatively clean readout of sympathetic arousal. It is cheap, non-invasive and widely used.

## Measurement and components
Recordings are usually analysed in two parts.
- **Tonic level**: the slowly changing baseline, often reported as skin conductance level, which shifts with general arousal, alertness and context.
- **Phasic responses**: discrete peaks, called skin conductance responses, which begin one to three seconds after a stimulus. A response elicited by a specific stimulus is separated from non-specific fluctuations that occur without an identifiable trigger.

Typical eliciting events include novel, intense, emotionally significant or task-relevant stimuli, and the response diminishes with repetition (see [[habituation]]). Because the response occurs without any distinct emotion label, it indexes the intensity of arousal and not its valence: pleasant and unpleasant stimuli can produce similar responses. Its interpretation also depends on temperature, humidity, electrode placement and individual differences. Brain regions linked to the response include the amygdala, ventromedial prefrontal cortex and anterior cingulate (Critchley, 2002), and it has been used in studies of fear conditioning, orienting, decision-making and, controversially, in lie detection. See [[emotional_processing]].

## Relation to Active Inference
Skin conductance is a measurement tool and not part of Active Inference theory, so the relation is as an empirical readout. Predictive-processing accounts of emotion, such as interoceptive inference, hold that bodily arousal reflects inference about internal states and about the precision of predictions (see [[interoception]]). Within that framing, skin conductance responses have been used as proxies for surprise, violated expectation or heightened uncertainty in experiments on learning and decision-making. The mapping from conductance to a particular model quantity is indirect and contested, and conclusions should be drawn with caution. See [[prediction_error]].
