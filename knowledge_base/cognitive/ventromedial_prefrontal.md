# Ventromedial Prefrontal Cortex

## Overview
The ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC) is the lower part of the medial surface of the frontal lobe, extending around the rostral genu of the corpus callosum and merging with medial [[orbitofrontal]] cortex. Its boundaries are defined differently across studies, and it covers parts of several cytoarchitectonic areas, so statements about "vmPFC" are not always about the same tissue. It has dense connections with the amygdala, hypothalamus, hippocampus, ventral striatum and brainstem regions that regulate bodily state.

## Functions
- **Value computation.** Functional imaging consistently shows vmPFC activity that scales with the subjective value of options, across goods of different kinds, which is read as evidence for a common value signal used in choice (see [[value_computation]]).
- **Emotion regulation.** The region contributes to modulating the amygdala, and it is engaged in the retrieval of extinction memory and in reappraisal (see [[emotion_regulation]]).
- **Decision-making.** Patients with vmPFC or adjacent orbitofrontal damage often retain intelligence and explicit knowledge but make poor everyday and financial decisions and perform poorly on tasks such as the Iowa Gambling Task. Damasio's somatic marker hypothesis proposed that bodily affective signals bias choice and that the vmPFC helps bring them to bear (Bechara, Damasio and Damasio, 2000; Damasio, 1994).
- **Self-related and schema-based processing.** The region is part of the default mode network and is active during self-referential thought and memory-based inference.

The nineteenth-century case of Phineas Gage, whose frontal injury was followed by changes in conduct, is often cited as an early example, although the extent of his brain damage and his later recovery are still debated.

## Relation to Active Inference
In predictive processing accounts, visceromotor regions of the medial prefrontal cortex are proposed as sources of top-down predictions about the state of the body, which makes emotion a matter of interoceptive inference (Barrett and Simmons, 2015). Preferences over outcomes are part of the generative model in Active Inference, which fits the vmPFC's value signals, and impairments after damage could be read as a loss of precise prior preferences. These mappings are interpretations of lesion and imaging data and are not uniquely supported.
