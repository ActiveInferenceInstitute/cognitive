# Auditory Agnosia

## Overview
Auditory agnosia is a rare neurological condition in which a person cannot recognise the meaning or identity of sounds although basic hearing, assessed by audiometry, is adequate. Like other agnosias, it is defined by exclusion: the failure should not be explained by deafness, general cognitive decline or aphasia (see [[agnosia]]). It is usually associated with bilateral or, less often, unilateral damage to the temporal lobes involving the [[auditory_cortex]] and surrounding areas, commonly from stroke.

## Forms
- **Verbal auditory agnosia (pure word deafness).** Speech is not understood, although reading, writing and speaking are relatively spared and nonspeech sounds may be recognised. This pattern is separated from [[wernicke_aphasia]], in which language is impaired in all modalities.
- **Nonverbal auditory agnosia.** Environmental sounds such as a doorbell or a barking dog cannot be identified, while speech may be intact.
- **Amusia.** Music perception is impaired in receptive forms, with loss of melody recognition.
- **Mixed and apperceptive versus associative types.** Analogous to the visual case, some patients have difficulty in forming a percept from the acoustic features, for instance discriminating pitch or temporal patterns, while others perceive adequately but cannot link the sound to its meaning (see [[visual_agnosia]]). Classification of individual patients is often difficult, and descriptions overlap.

## Interpretation
Dissociations between speech and nonspeech sounds support models in which different auditory object categories rely on partly separate processing pathways beyond primary cortex (Polster and Rose, 1998). Some cases of word deafness are explained by a deficit in rapid temporal processing, while others appear to reflect disconnection of auditory input from language areas, and the debate is unresolved.

## Relation to Active Inference
There is no established Active Inference account of auditory agnosia. Predictive-coding models of the auditory system treat perception as hierarchical inference about the causes of sounds, with deviance responses such as the mismatch negativity read as prediction errors (see [[predictive_coding]] and [[auditory_streaming]]). By analogy, agnosia could be described as damage to the levels that assign sounds to object categories. This is a hypothetical description, not a tested model.
