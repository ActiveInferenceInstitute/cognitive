# Recollection

## Overview
Recollection is the retrieval of specific details about a previous episode, such as where it happened, what accompanied it and what was thought or felt at the time. It is usually contrasted with familiarity, the sense that an item has been encountered before without access to any contextual detail. Tulving (1985) linked recollection to autonoetic awareness, the experience of mentally re-living an event.

## Dual-process accounts
Dual-process models of recognition memory propose that a judgment of prior occurrence can rest on two kinds of evidence (Mandler, 1980; Yonelinas, 2002). Familiarity is fast, graded and item-based. Recollection is slower, threshold-like in many formulations, and returns associated information. Behavioural methods used to separate them include remember/know judgments, source-memory tests, process-dissociation procedures and receiver operating characteristic curves, whose shape is interpreted in terms of the two components. Single-process signal-detection models offer an alternative reading of the same data, and the degree to which the two processes are truly separate remains debated.

## Neural basis
Recollection is associated with the hippocampus and with a wider network that includes parahippocampal cortex, retrosplenial cortex and parietal regions. Familiarity has been linked more closely to perirhinal cortex, although the strength of this dissociation is contested. In electrophysiological studies the two are often associated with different event-related components, a late parietal effect for recollection and an earlier frontal effect for familiarity.

## Relation to Active Inference
Recollection is not stored playback. Contemporary accounts treat it as constructive: a partial cue triggers pattern completion in the hippocampus and the missing detail is filled in by a generative model of scenes and events (see [[scene_construction]]). In Active Inference terms, retrieval is inference about hidden causes given a cue, with the remembered episode as the posterior over those causes. This reading is a conceptual alignment with predictive-processing views of the hippocampus, not a settled derivation, and specific quantitative models of recollection remain a research topic.
