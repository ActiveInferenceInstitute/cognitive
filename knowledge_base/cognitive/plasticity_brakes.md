# Plasticity Brakes

## Overview
Cortical circuits are highly plastic during critical periods in early life and much less so in adulthood. The term "brakes" refers to molecular and structural factors that appear as critical periods close and actively restrict further plasticity, so that experience-dependent change is harder to elicit afterwards. The concept reframes the end of a critical period as an active process, not merely a passive loss of capacity (see [[critical_periods]]).

## Identified factors
In the visual system, where the best evidence exists, the maturation of inhibitory circuits is a trigger: critical-period onset depends on a threshold of GABAergic inhibition, particularly from parvalbumin-expressing interneurons (Hensch, 2005). Structural brakes include perineuronal nets, extracellular matrix structures that condense around these interneurons, and myelin-associated signalling through the Nogo receptor, whose absence allows ocular dominance plasticity to persist in adult mice (McGee et al., 2005). Degrading the matrix in adult rat visual cortex reactivated ocular dominance plasticity (Pizzorusso et al., 2002). Other proposed brakes include the prototoxin Lynx1 and epigenetic changes that reduce transcription of plasticity-related genes, as reviewed by Bavelier et al. (2010).

## Caveats
The evidence is mostly from rodents and from the visual system, and the brakes may differ among sensory, motor and higher-order areas, which open and close their plasticity at different ages. Releasing a brake can increase plasticity without ensuring that the resulting change is adaptive, and in some experiments removal is combined with a behavioural intervention to guide change. The translation to humans remains limited, with few clinical interventions supported by strong controlled evidence. It is also unclear how brakes relate to the behavioural observation that adults can still learn many skills, including by perceptual training.

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is an analogy. A brake that reduces plasticity resembles a shift from high learning rates, where parameters of the generative model are readily revised, to strongly constraining priors. This is a heuristic reading and not an established model, although precision and learning rates are part of the framework.
