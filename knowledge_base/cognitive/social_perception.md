# Social Perception

## Overview
Social perception is the extraction of socially relevant information from sensory input: who someone is, what they feel, where they are looking, what action they are performing, and what they may intend. It sits between low-level vision or audition and the inferential processes of [[theory_of_mind]], and in practice the boundary is blurred because even simple percepts are shaped by expectation.

## Sources and findings
- **Faces and gaze.** Facial expression, gaze direction, and head orientation are read quickly and support [[joint_attention]]. Face identity and changeable aspects such as expression are partly dissociable.
- **Bodies and movement.** Point-light displays show that motion alone conveys action, gender, and emotion ([[biological_motion]]).
- **Voice.** Prosody and timbre carry affect and speaker identity.
- **Thin slices.** Brief exposures of behaviour predict later judgments of traits and relationships, though accuracy varies by trait and judgment, and first impressions of traits such as trustworthiness are often unreliable against actual behaviour.
- **Neural substrates.** The superior temporal sulcus region responds to eye, mouth, hand, and whole-body movement, the fusiform face area to faces, and temporoparietal and medial prefrontal regions to inferences about mental states ([[superior_temporal]], [[temporoparietal]], [[medial_prefrontal]]).

## Role in Active Inference
Action observation can be treated as inference about the hidden causes (goals, intentions) of seen movement, using the observer's own generative model of action. Kilner, Friston, and Frith formulated the mirror-neuron system in these terms: prediction errors at lower levels of a hierarchy are explained away by inferring intentions at higher levels. This is a theoretical proposal consistent with some data; it remains debated whether mirror-like responses play this inferential role. A related line models communication and joint action as agents inferring each other's generative models ([[multi_agent_active_inference]]).
