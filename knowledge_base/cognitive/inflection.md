# Inflection

## Overview
Inflection is the modification of a word's form to express grammatical categories such as tense, number, person, gender and case, without creating a new lexical item. English walk, walks, walked and walking are forms of one word, whereas deriving walker from walk is derivation, which forms a new word and is treated under [[word_formation]]. Languages differ widely in how much they inflect. English has few inflectional endings; Latin and Russian mark case and gender extensively; Turkish strings several suffixes onto a stem, each with one meaning.

## Regular and irregular forms
Most English verbs form the past tense by adding -ed, but a closed class of frequent verbs, such as go and went, is irregular. Children learning English commonly pass through a phase of overregularisation (producing "goed"), after earlier correct use of some irregular forms. This pattern set up one of the best known debates in cognitive science. Dual-route accounts hold that regular forms are produced by a symbolic rule and irregular forms are retrieved from associative memory (Pinker & Prince, 1988; Pinker, 1999). Single-mechanism connectionist accounts, beginning with Rumelhart and McClelland's past-tense network, propose that one pattern-associating system handles both. Neuroimaging, aphasia and priming evidence has been cited by both sides, and Pinker and Ullman (2002) review the case for a declarative-memory versus procedural distinction. The question is not settled, and models of both kinds have been refined in response to the other.

## Processing
Whether complex forms are stored whole or composed from stems and affixes during recognition and production is also debated. Evidence from frequency effects suggests that high-frequency regular forms may be stored, so that storage and composition probably coexist (see [[lexical_access]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
There is no established derivation linking inflection to Active Inference. Language as inference over hierarchical generative models is a general proposal in the vault's themes, but the rules-versus-memory question has not been resolved in those terms.
