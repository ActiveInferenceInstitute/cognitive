# Autobiographical Memory

## Overview
Autobiographical memory is memory for the events, circumstances and facts of one's own life. It draws on [[episodic_memory]] for specific experiences and on [[semantic_memory]] for general knowledge about oneself, such as places lived or periods of life. Tulving linked recollection of past events to autonoetic consciousness, the awareness of oneself as the experiencer in the past.

## Structure and properties
Conway and Pleydell-Pearce (2000) described a self-memory system in which autobiographical memories are constructed on demand, under the control of goals associated with the working self, from a hierarchy of knowledge: lifetime periods, general events, and event-specific details. Retrieval can be generative (effortful, top-down search) or direct (cue-triggered). Several regularities are well documented:
- memories are reconstructive and can be distorted or fabricated, as Bartlett (1932) argued from schema-based recall;
- the same neural machinery that supports remembering also supports imagining future events, which led Schacter and Addis (2007) to propose that memory is constructive for adaptive reasons;
- recall engages a distributed network including hippocampus, medial prefrontal and posterior midline cortex, and lateral temporal and parietal regions (see [[medial_prefrontal]]);
- emotionally significant events are retained differently from neutral ones, and consolidation changes their content over time (see [[memory_consolidation]], [[systems_consolidation]]).

## Role in Active Inference
Constructive recall fits naturally with a generative-model view in which remembering means inferring a plausible past state and sensory scene from a partial cue by running the model forwards or backwards, with prior expectations filling gaps. Imagining the future is the same operation applied to predicted states. This is an interpretation that links existing findings to the framework; specific hippocampal and cortical implementations of such inference are under active investigation, and the formal treatment of long-term personal narratives (beyond short-horizon planning) is limited. Self-related priors in the model also link autobiographical memory to [[self_awareness]].
