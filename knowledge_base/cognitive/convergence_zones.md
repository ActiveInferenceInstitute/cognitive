# Convergence Zones

## Overview
Convergence zones, or convergence-divergence zones, are a proposal about how the brain stores and retrieves multimodal memories. In Antonio Damasio's 1989 framework, the features of an event or object are first represented in separate early sensory and motor cortices. Neural ensembles in higher regions receive converging inputs from these areas and project back to them. The ensembles do not hold the content of the memory. They store the combinatorial patterns that can reactivate the feature representations together.

## Mechanism
- **Convergence.** Feedforward projections from several sensory modalities, and from motor and affective systems, meet in ensembles at successive levels, so higher zones record which lower-level features co-occurred.
- **Divergence.** Feedback projections from a zone reach the same earlier regions, so activating the zone can re-evoke the distributed pattern of features that constitutes recall. Damasio called this time-locked multiregional retroactivation.
- **Hierarchy.** Lower-order zones bind simple combinations, higher-order zones bind more abstract or multimodal ones, and temporal and parietal association regions are suggested sites. The hippocampal system is usually described as binding episodes during acquisition and consolidation, while neocortical zones carry long-term stored patterns.

## Related work
Meyer and Damasio (2009) restated the framework with later evidence for reactivation of sensory cortices during recall. A separate but compatible idea is the semantic hub account of Patterson, Nestor and Rogers, which places an amodal hub in the anterior temporal lobes, supported by semantic dementia. Neuroimaging studies of recall that find sensory-cortex reactivation are often cited in support, though how specific the reactivated patterns are remains debated.

## Relation to Active Inference
The architecture resembles a hierarchical generative model: higher-level states generate predictions that descend to sensory regions, and the reactivation of early cortex corresponds to a top-down prediction. Remembering can accordingly be read as generating sensory consequences from a high-level cause. This is a conceptual correspondence made by later authors. Damasio's proposal predates Active Inference and does not use its formalism.
