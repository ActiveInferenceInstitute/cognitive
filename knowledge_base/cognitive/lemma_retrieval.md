# Lemma Retrieval

## Overview
In theories of speech production, a lemma is an abstract lexical entry that links a concept to the syntactic properties of a word, such as its grammatical category and, in some languages, its gender, without specifying how the word sounds. Lemma retrieval is the selection of this entry for a concept the speaker wishes to express. In Levelt's influential account, it is followed by retrieval of the word form, or lexeme, and its phonological encoding. The two-step view is a form of [[lexical_access]] in production.

## Stages of word production
1. **Conceptual preparation.** A concept is selected, activating a set of related lexical candidates.
2. **Lemma selection.** The lemma matching the intended concept is chosen from competing entries, and its syntactic features become available for building the sentence.
3. **Form retrieval.** The lexeme with its morphological and phonological content is accessed.
4. **Phonological and phonetic encoding.** A syllabified articulatory plan is built and passed to the motor system.

## Evidence
Support for separating lemma and form comes from tip-of-the-tongue states, in which speakers sometimes retrieve a word's grammatical gender without its sounds, from speech errors that substitute semantically related words, and from priming and picture-word interference experiments. Alternatives such as interactive models allow information to flow back and forth between stages (see [[spreading_activation]]), and the necessity of a separate lemma level remains debated. Meta-analyses by Indefrey and Levelt (2004) associated lemma retrieval with the left middle temporal region (see [[middle_temporal]]), though localisation of cognitive stages is approximate.

## Relation to Active Inference
Speech production has been modelled as inference over a hierarchy in which a conceptual level generates predictions for lexical, phonological and motor levels (Friston and colleagues, 2020). In that setting, selecting a lemma corresponds to inferring an intermediate state that conditions lower-level predictions. This is a possible reading of Levelt's stages and not a result derived from them.
