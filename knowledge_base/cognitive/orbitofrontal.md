# Orbitofrontal Cortex

## Overview
The orbitofrontal cortex (OFC) is the region of prefrontal cortex above the orbits, on the ventral surface of the frontal lobe. It receives taste, smell, visual, and somatosensory inputs together with signals from the amygdala, hypothalamus, and striatum, and projects to areas that control autonomic and motivated behaviour. It is a core node in the valuation circuitry for choice and for linking sensory cues to expected outcomes. The page covers the human and primate OFC; the rodent homologue is still debated.

## Functions
- **Value representation**: neurons in primate OFC encode the subjective value of offered goods, on a common scale that permits comparison across different goods (Padoa-Schioppa and Assad, 2006). Activity tracks both chosen value and offer value, and has been interpreted as a substrate of economic choice.
- **Outcome expectancy and devaluation**: OFC is needed to adjust behaviour when the value of an outcome changes, for example after satiety or devaluation, indicating it represents specific expected outcomes and not only generic reward.
- **Reversal learning**: lesions impair flexibly switching responding when reward contingencies reverse.
- **Task-space representation**: Wilson and colleagues (2014) proposed that OFC encodes a cognitive map of the current task state, including unobservable information relevant for predicting outcomes.
- **Emotion and social behaviour**: damage to ventromedial prefrontal regions, including OFC, impairs decision making in the Iowa Gambling Task and is associated with changes in social conduct (Bechara et al., 1994). The somatic marker hypothesis interprets this as loss of bodily signals that bias choice.

Distinctions between lateral OFC, medial OFC, and neighbouring ventromedial prefrontal cortex ([[medial_prefrontal]]) are important, since functional claims differ by sub-region and species.

## Role in Active Inference
Active Inference offers two complementary readings. First, the state-representation view of OFC fits the framework's emphasis on beliefs about hidden states: the OFC would hold the posterior over task-relevant states from which outcomes and their preferred status are predicted. Second, expected outcomes and their preference (the C vector in discrete models, see [[C_matrix]]) provide the quantities that valuation signals appear to express. Reversal learning corresponds to updating beliefs about contingencies when predictions fail. These identifications are hypotheses; most OFC evidence is also explained by reinforcement-learning models, and Active Inference accounts of specific OFC findings are limited.
