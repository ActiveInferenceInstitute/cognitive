# Near Transfer

## Overview
Transfer refers to the use of knowledge or skill acquired in one setting in another. Near transfer is transfer to a context that is similar to the one in which learning occurred, for example solving a new problem of the same type, using a skill with a different but closely related tool, or performing a task in a slightly changed format. It contrasts with [[far_transfer]], which applies learning to dissimilar domains. The distinction is a matter of degree, and has been formalised in taxonomies that describe similarity along several dimensions such as content, physical context, timing and social setting (Barnett and Ceci, 2002).

## Theoretical background
Thorndike and Woodworth (1901) proposed that improvement in one function benefits another only to the extent that the two share identical elements, which predicts frequent near transfer and little far transfer. Later cognitive theories describe transfer as dependent on recognising structural similarity between situations, including through abstraction of schemas (see [[schemas]]). Learners often fail to notice the relevance of earlier knowledge when surface features differ, so transfer is easier when examples are varied and the underlying principle is made explicit.

## Evidence from training
Studies of working memory and other cognitive training programmes often report improvement on trained tasks and on similar untrained tasks, which is near transfer. Meta-analyses typically find that improvements do not extend reliably to measures of general intelligence or academic achievement (Melby-Lervåg, Redick and Hulme, 2016). A review of chess, music and working memory training reached a similar conclusion for far transfer (Sala and Gobet, 2017). The results therefore support the identical elements picture, although some critics question the quality of individual studies and the choice of outcome measures.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is a loose analogy. Transfer can be described as reuse of a learned generative model, or of its structure, in a new setting, in which near transfer needs little revision of the model and far transfer requires more. Work on structure learning and model reduction in Active Inference addresses how models are generalised, but it has not been applied to the educational transfer literature in a tested way. In machine learning the corresponding topic is [[transfer_learning]].
