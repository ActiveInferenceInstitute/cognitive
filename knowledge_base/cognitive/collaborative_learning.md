# Collaborative Learning

## Overview
Collaborative learning refers to situations in which two or more people learn, or attempt to learn, something together, usually by working on a shared task or problem. The term covers peer tutoring, small-group problem solving, discussion and computer-supported joint work. Dillenbourg (1999) notes that the phrase is broad and that its value lies less in the label than in the interactions that occur, such as explaining, disagreeing and negotiating meaning.

## Theoretical accounts
- **Sociocultural.** Vygotsky (1978) argued that higher mental functions arise first between people and are later internalised. His zone of proximal development describes what a learner can do with help but not yet alone.
- **Sociocognitive.** Following Piaget, disagreement between peers is thought to create cognitive conflict that prompts reorganisation of understanding.
- **Shared cognition.** Group members build a common ground of knowledge, which supports coordination but can also entrench shared errors.

## Conditions and findings
Effects of group work are not automatic. Research on cooperative learning identifies positive interdependence among members and individual accountability as conditions that help, alongside well-structured tasks and skills in giving and receiving explanations (Johnson & Johnson, 2009). Outcomes depend on task design, group composition and the quality of interaction, and poorly structured groups can show social loafing or unequal participation. The literature uses "collaborative" and "cooperative" with overlapping and sometimes contested meanings.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is an analogy. Multi-agent formulations of Active Inference treat agents as exchanging information to align shared generative models, and communicative exchange can be described as reducing uncertainty about another agent's beliefs. This is an active research area, but there is no established derivation specific to classroom collaboration, and educational effects remain an empirical matter.
