# Wernicke's Area

## Overview
Wernicke's area is a region of the left posterior superior temporal gyrus, roughly corresponding to Brodmann area 22, that classical neurology identified as the centre for understanding spoken language. Carl Wernicke (1874) described patients with fluent but disordered speech and poor comprehension following lesions in this territory. Together with Broca's area and the arcuate fasciculus connecting them, it formed the basis of the Wernicke-Lichtheim-Geschwind model of language (Geschwind, 1970).

## Clinical features and revision
- **Wernicke's aphasia.** Speech is fluent, with normal rhythm and grammatical-sounding structure, but contains substitutions of words or sounds (paraphasias), occasional invented words, and little meaningful content. Comprehension of speech is markedly impaired, and patients often lack awareness of their errors.
- **Boundaries.** Authors disagree about which cortex belongs to the area. Definitions vary from the posterior superior temporal gyrus alone to wider zones including the planum temporale, supramarginal gyrus and angular gyrus.
- **Modern models.** Hickok and Poeppel (2007) proposed a dual-stream model in which posterior superior temporal regions perform acoustic and phonological analysis, a ventral stream through the middle temporal gyrus maps sound to meaning, and a dorsal stream maps sound to articulation. On this account, lasting comprehension deficits reflect damage to a distributed network, and the classical picture of a single comprehension centre is too simple.

## Relation to Active Inference
Language comprehension can be formulated as inference about the causes of a speech signal, using a hierarchical generative model that spans acoustic, lexical and semantic levels. Friston and colleagues (2020) developed such a model for linguistic communication, simulating how beliefs about words and sentences are updated and how an agent chooses utterances to resolve uncertainty. Posterior temporal regions are plausibly engaged at the lower levels of such a hierarchy, but there is no established mapping of model components onto this cortical area. The link is a modelling programme that offers a formal language for the older anatomical findings.
