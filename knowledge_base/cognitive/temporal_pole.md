# Temporal Pole

## Overview
The temporal pole is the most anterior part of the temporal lobe, corresponding roughly to Brodmann area 38. It has connections with the amygdala, orbitofrontal cortex and insula through the uncinate fasciculus, as well as with sensory association cortex in the temporal lobe. This position gives it access to both highly processed perceptual information and to emotional and motivational signals, and it is often grouped with neighbouring anterior temporal cortex when function is discussed.

## Semantic hub
The best-supported function of the anterior temporal lobe is the representation of conceptual knowledge. In semantic dementia, also called the semantic variant of primary progressive aphasia, progressive atrophy centred on the anterior temporal lobes, usually more marked on the left, produces a loss of knowledge about objects, people and words across all modalities. Patients may still be fluent, and their perception, working memory and everyday problem solving can be relatively preserved, but they lose the meaning of words and objects. This pattern led to the hub-and-spoke model, in which modality-specific "spokes" in sensory and motor regions are linked by an amodal hub in the anterior temporal cortex that supports generalisation across items with different surface features (Patterson et al., 2007; Lambon Ralph et al., 2017). Imaging and stimulation studies in healthy participants support a role in semantic tasks, although the signal is difficult to measure with some functional imaging methods (see [[semantic_memory]]).

## Social and emotional functions
Olson and colleagues (2007) reviewed evidence from lesions and imaging that the temporal pole also contributes to social and emotional processing, including recognition of familiar people, understanding of social concepts and emotional memory. They suggested that it helps to couple perceptual inputs with visceral emotional responses. Whether these roles reflect a distinct function or the same conceptual machinery applied to social knowledge is unresolved (see [[social_cognition]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
There is no established Active Inference model of the temporal pole. A speculative link is that an amodal hub corresponds to a high level of a hierarchical generative model, where abstract categories constrain predictions at lower sensory levels. This is an analogy that suggests testable ideas, such as how conceptual priors shape perception, and should not be read as a demonstrated account of the region.
