# Serial Bottleneck

## Overview
A serial bottleneck is a stage of processing that can handle only one item or operation at a time, so that other tasks or stimuli must wait even though earlier and later stages work in parallel. The idea has a long history in attention research, beginning with Broadbent's (1958) filter model, in which a limited-capacity channel follows parallel sensory analysis. In consciousness research it is used for the proposal that only one content can be consciously processed at a time.

## Evidence
- **Psychological refractory period.** When two speeded tasks are presented in quick succession, the response to the second is delayed, and the delay shrinks as the interval between stimuli grows. Welford (1952) interpreted this as a single-channel limit. Pashler (1994) concluded that perceptual and motor stages can overlap, while a central stage, such as response selection, cannot.
- **Attentional blink.** Detection of a second target is impaired when it follows a first target by roughly 200 to 500 ms in a rapid stream of items, consistent with a limited resource that is occupied by the first (see [[attentional_blink]]).
- **Neural signatures.** Imaging and electrophysiology show that late, widespread activation, linked to access to a global workspace, is delayed under dual-task conditions.

The strength of the limit is disputed. Extensive practice can greatly reduce dual-task costs, and some tasks appear to share capacity rather than queue, so many researchers prefer graded-capacity or multiple-resource accounts.

## Relation to consciousness
In the global neuronal workspace framework, subliminal processing is parallel and unconscious, whereas conscious access is a limited, serial broadcast to many systems (Dehaene & Naccache, 2001). This implies a bottleneck on conscious content. Other theories do not require a strict serial limit.

## Relation to Active Inference
Active Inference does not itself require a serial bottleneck. It does imply that an agent selects one policy at a time, so a single posterior over policies yields one action stream, and that attention is the allocation of limited precision. These are formal features of the framework, but the identification of the observed psychological bottleneck with either is interpretive.
