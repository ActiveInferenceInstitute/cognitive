# Sensorimotor Development

## Overview
Sensorimotor development is the early growth of perception, movement and their coordination, through which infants come to know the world by acting on it. In Piaget's theory it defines the first stage of cognitive development, from birth to about two years, in which intelligence is expressed through actions and their sensory consequences before the child has symbols or language (Piaget, 1952).

## Piaget's substages
Piaget divided the stage into six substages: simple reflexes; primary circular reactions, where an infant repeats an action that happened by chance on its own body; secondary circular reactions, repeating actions for their effects on objects; coordination of secondary schemes, combining actions to reach a goal; tertiary circular reactions, experimenting with variations; and the beginning of representation, with mental combinations and deferred imitation. A key achievement is object permanence, the understanding that objects continue to exist when out of sight, which Piaget inferred from the age at which infants search for hidden objects.

## Later developments
Later research with looking-time methods suggests that infants may show some knowledge of object properties earlier than Piaget's search tasks indicated, although how to interpret such results is debated. Dynamic-systems approaches emphasise that skills such as reaching and walking emerge from the interaction of body, task and environment, not from a fixed maturational sequence (Thelen & Smith, 1994). Embodied views stress learning of sensorimotor contingencies, the lawful relations between movements and the changes they produce in sensation.

## Relation to Active Inference
Infant exploratory action, such as repeated reaching and mouthing, has been interpreted as the acquisition of a generative model of how actions produce sensations, in which actions are selected partly to reduce uncertainty about the model. This is an active area of theoretical and robotics work rather than a settled developmental account, and it is not a replacement for Piaget's empirical descriptions.
