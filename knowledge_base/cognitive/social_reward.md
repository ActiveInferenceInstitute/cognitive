# Social Reward

## Overview
Social reward refers to outcomes of interaction that people and animals value: approval, a good reputation, affiliation, being treated fairly, successful cooperation, and the company of others. The central empirical claim is that these outcomes are processed by largely the same valuation circuitry as primary and monetary rewards, so that social value can be compared against, and traded for, other goods.

## Evidence and mechanisms
- **Shared circuitry.** Imaging studies report ventral striatal and ventromedial prefrontal responses to positive social feedback, including a good reputation, that overlap with responses to money ([[ventral_striatum]], [[value_computation]]).
- **Learning.** Social outcomes drive prediction-error learning like other rewards, so partner behaviour shapes future choices ([[reward_processing]], [[value_learning]]). Learning from others adds a layer: values can be taken over from observed choices ([[social_learning]]).
- **Cooperation and fairness.** Mutual cooperation and the punishment of norm violators activate reward-related regions in economic-game studies, which is one proposed motivation for cooperation beyond material payoff ([[cooperation]], [[social_norms]]).
- **Neuromodulators.** Dopamine is implicated in valuation and learning. Oxytocin is often linked to social bonding; effects of intranasal oxytocin on human social behaviour are small and inconsistent across studies, so claims about it should be held loosely ([[oxytocin]], [[dopamine_system]]).
- **Individual differences.** Sensitivity to social reward varies and is studied in relation to social anxiety and autism, but causal conclusions are limited ([[social_anxiety]], [[autism]]).

## Role in Active Inference
Active Inference replaces a scalar reward with prior preferences over outcomes: an agent is drawn toward observations it expects to encounter as part of its characteristic states. Social reward then corresponds to prior preferences defined over social outcomes, such as expected approval, which enter policy selection through expected free energy ([[expected_free_energy]], [[pragmatic_value]]). This is a representational choice that lets social valuation be modelled; it does not by itself explain why such outcomes are preferred.
