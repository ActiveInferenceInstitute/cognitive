# Set Shifting

## Overview
Set shifting is the ability to change from one mental set, such as a rule, strategy or attentional focus, to another when circumstances require it. It is one of the three executive functions identified by Miyake and colleagues (2000), alongside updating working memory and inhibition, and is often called shifting or cognitive flexibility. Difficulty with it appears as perseveration, the continued use of a rule that no longer applies.

## Assessment
- **Wisconsin Card Sorting Test.** Cards are sorted by colour, shape or number, and the sorting rule changes without warning, so the participant must infer the change from feedback (Berg, 1948). Perseverative errors are the usual index of impaired shifting.
- **Task-switching paradigms.** Participants alternate between two tasks, and the cost of a switch relative to a repetition is measured; Monsell (2003) reviewed these costs.
- **Attentional set-shifting in animals.** Rodent and primate tasks require changing the relevant stimulus dimension, such as from odour to texture.
- **Reversal learning.** A previously rewarded option becomes unrewarded, which tests flexibility at the level of values.

Switch costs persist even with advance notice, and have been explained by reconfiguration of the task set, interference from the previous set (task-set inertia) and retrieval of associations.

## Neural basis
Lesion and imaging studies implicate lateral prefrontal cortex, the anterior cingulate, parietal cortex and the basal ganglia, and the neuromodulators dopamine and noradrenaline. Dissociations within the prefrontal cortex have been reported, with orbitofrontal damage affecting reversal and dorsolateral damage affecting changes in the relevant dimension. Deficits are described in many conditions, including frontal damage, Parkinson's disease, and autism and obsessive-compulsive disorder, though in these groups results are heterogeneous.

## Relation to Active Inference
Shifting can be interpreted as inference about a hidden context variable that determines which rule applies. When prediction errors accumulate under the current context, the posterior over contexts changes and the policy set changes with it. This is a reading shared by hierarchical Bayesian models of the card-sorting and reversal tasks, not a specific result of Active Inference.
