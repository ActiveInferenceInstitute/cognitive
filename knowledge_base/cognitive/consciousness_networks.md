# Consciousness Networks

## Overview
Consciousness networks are the distributed brain systems whose activity or interaction correlates with, or is hypothesised to underlie, conscious experience. Research separates the level of consciousness (wakefulness, sleep, anaesthesia, disorders of consciousness) from its contents (what one is aware of), and different networks are implicated for each. Which networks are necessary and which are merely correlates is contested.

## Networks and findings
- **Frontoparietal networks**: sustained, widespread activation in prefrontal and parietal cortex accompanies reportable perception, which is central to workspace theories (Dehaene and Naccache, 2001; see [[global_workspace]]). Critics note that prefrontal activity may reflect report and task demands rather than experience itself.
- **Posterior cortical "hot zone"**: Koch and colleagues (2016) argued that the neural correlates of consciousness lie mainly in posterior sensory cortex, consistent with integrated information theory (see [[integrated_information]]).
- **Default-mode network**: medial prefrontal, posterior cingulate and lateral parietal regions more active at rest (Raichle et al., 2001), associated with self-referential thought, mind-wandering and autobiographical memory (see [[medial_prefrontal]], [[autobiographical_memory]]).
- **Salience network**: anterior insula and anterior cingulate, implicated in detecting salient events and in switching between networks (Seeley et al., 2007; see [[anterior_cingulate]], [[interoception]]).
- **Thalamocortical systems**: thalamic nuclei and their cortical loops support arousal and cortical communication, and are affected in anaesthesia and in disorders of consciousness.

Measures of integration and differentiation of network activity, derived from perturbation or spontaneous dynamics, differ between conscious and unconscious states, although their interpretation is still debated (see [[connectivity]]).

## Role in Active Inference
Active Inference accounts propose that conscious content depends on the inference carried out by deep, temporally extended generative models, with precision-weighted hierarchical message passing as the relevant process, and that interoceptive and self-related inference underlie the sense of self (see [[global_workspace]], [[self_awareness]], [[interoception]]). Proposals relate specific networks to model components, such as salience networks to precision control and the default-mode network to high-level self-models. These ideas are active research hypotheses with partial empirical support; none has been decisively distinguished from the competing network theories, and the free-energy principle does not by itself predict which networks are required.
