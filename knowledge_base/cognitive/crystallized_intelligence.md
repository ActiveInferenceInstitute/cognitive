# Crystallized Intelligence

## Overview
Crystallized intelligence is the store of knowledge and skills that a person has acquired through education, culture and experience, together with the ability to use it in reasoning. Raymond Cattell introduced the term and, with John Horn, contrasted it with fluid intelligence. In the Cattell-Horn-Carroll model it corresponds to a broad ability often called comprehension-knowledge, covering vocabulary, general information and verbal comprehension.

## Measurement
Crystallized intelligence is assessed with tests of vocabulary, general knowledge, verbal analogies and reading comprehension. Because performance depends on what has been learned, scores reflect schooling, language and cultural exposure, and tests normed in one population may be less suitable in another. In practice, vocabulary measures are often used to estimate a person's prior level of ability, because they change less with age or mild brain injury than speeded reasoning tasks.

## Lifespan course
Cross-sectional and longitudinal studies generally show that crystallized abilities continue to increase well into middle age and are retained into later life, in contrast to fluid abilities, which decline earlier. Part of this difference arises because knowledge accumulates and is rehearsed, while speed-dependent processing slows. The size of the age differences depends on the sample and design, and cohort effects complicate comparisons across generations. Fluid and crystallized abilities are positively correlated, since fluid reasoning aids the acquisition of knowledge, an idea sometimes expressed as investment theory.

## Relation to Active Inference
Active Inference has no standard account of crystallized intelligence. A natural analogy is that crystallized knowledge corresponds to the content of a generative model already learned, including its learned priors and likelihood mappings, which supports fast inference in familiar domains. The capacity to extend such a model to new domains would then be closer to fluid reasoning. This mapping is an interpretation that has not been tested against psychometric data.
