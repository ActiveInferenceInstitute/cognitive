# Error Monitoring

## Overview
Error monitoring is the detection of mistakes in one's own performance, together with the adjustments that follow. It is part of the wider capacity for performance monitoring and supports cognitive control by signalling when behaviour has departed from a goal. Behavioural signs include post-error slowing, a lengthening of response times after an error, and corrective responses that may occur before external feedback is given.

## Neural markers
The best-known marker is the error-related negativity (ERN or Ne), a negative deflection in the event-related potential that begins around the time of an incorrect speeded response and is largest at frontocentral scalp sites (Gehring et al., 1993). It is often followed by a later positivity associated with conscious recognition of the error. Source localisation and imaging studies place generators in the medial frontal cortex, especially the dorsal anterior and midcingulate regions (see [[cingulate_cortex]]). Related signals follow negative feedback. The size of the ERN varies with factors such as motivation and anxiety.

## Theories
Three families of theory are prominent. The first is the mismatch account, in which the system compares the intended or correct response with the actual response. The second is the conflict-monitoring account, in which co-activation of competing responses is detected and triggers control (Botvinick et al., 2001). The third is the reinforcement-learning account, which proposes that the ERN reflects a negative dopaminergic prediction error sent to the medial frontal cortex when events are worse than expected (Holroyd and Coles, 2002). The accounts make partly overlapping predictions and are still being compared.

## Relation to Active Inference
In an Active Inference reading, an error is an outcome that is surprising under the policy that was being pursued, and the associated signal is a prediction error about outcomes. The resulting increase in uncertainty about the current policy motivates belief updating, and may increase exploratory or corrective behaviour. This is broadly consistent with the reinforcement-learning and predicted-outcome theories, which also treat the signal as a prediction error. The mapping is largely conceptual, with few direct quantitative tests, and it does not by itself decide between the competing accounts above.
