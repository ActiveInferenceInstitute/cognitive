# Adaptive Expertise

## Overview
Adaptive expertise is a form of expertise in which a person performs routine tasks efficiently and also changes their methods when a problem differs from those previously met. The term was introduced by Hatano and Inagaki (1986), who contrasted it with routine expertise: a routine expert is fast and accurate on familiar problem types but may fail when conditions change, whereas an adaptive expert understands why procedures work and can modify or invent them.

## Characteristics
- **Conceptual understanding.** Adaptive experts tend to hold causal or structural models of a domain, not only memorised procedures. This lets them judge which parts of a method depend on which conditions.
- **Efficiency and innovation.** Later work described two dimensions, efficiency and innovation, and argued that adaptive expertise requires both, with a balance between them.
- **Metacognition.** Monitoring one's own understanding, noticing when a routine no longer fits, and being willing to leave a comfortable approach are commonly described as central (see [[metacognition]]).
- **Domain breadth.** The concept has been applied in teaching, medicine, engineering and the study of workplace learning, and the details of measurement vary across these fields.

## Development
Studies of instruction suggest that adaptive expertise is promoted by variation, by explaining and comparing cases, and by opportunities to handle problems that cannot be solved routinely, whereas heavy repetition of a single problem type builds routine fluency (see [[deliberate_practice]] and [[skill_acquisition]]). Transfer to new situations is the practical test, and it is often hard to obtain (see [[near_transfer]] and [[far_transfer]]). The empirical base is mostly from education and professional training, and there is no single agreed operational definition.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is conceptual. Routine expertise resembles a well-tuned generative model with strongly precise habitual policies (see [[automaticity]]). Adaptive expertise resembles a model whose structure captures the causes of outcomes, so that it can be re-used or revised when the context changes. This parallels the Active Inference treatment of structure learning and model comparison, in which agents choose between model structures by their evidence (see [[structure_learning]] and [[model_comparison]]). No study has tested adaptive expertise in these terms.
