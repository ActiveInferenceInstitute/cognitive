# Expressive Suppression

## Overview
Expressive suppression is the effortful inhibition of ongoing emotion-expressive behaviour, such as facial movement, gesture and vocal tone, while an emotion is being felt. In Gross's process model of emotion regulation (1998) it is a response-focused strategy: it acts late, after the emotional response has already been generated, and changes how the emotion appears to others and not how it arises. This contrasts with [[cognitive_reappraisal]], an antecedent-focused strategy that alters the interpretation of a situation before the emotional response fully develops.

## Findings
Laboratory studies, usually using film clips or autobiographical recall, report several consistent patterns:
- Suppression reduces expressive behaviour, but typically does not reduce the experience of negative emotion to the same extent.
- Some studies find increased sympathetic physiological responses in the suppressing person.
- It consumes cognitive resources. Richards and Gross (2000) found poorer memory for information presented during suppression, which they attributed to the self-monitoring that suppression requires.
- It can affect social interaction. Partners of suppressors have been reported to feel less rapport.

Gross and John (2003) developed a questionnaire that separates habitual suppression from habitual reappraisal. Higher habitual suppression was associated with less positive and more negative emotional functioning, and with poorer well-being and social relations in their samples. The findings are correlational, and later work stresses that effects depend on culture, context and the demands of the situation, so suppression is not uniformly harmful.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is an analogy. In an Active Inference reading, expressive output is an action that also changes the signals that others receive and, through interoceptive and proprioceptive feedback, the agent's own evidence about its emotional state. Suppression could then be described as selecting actions that override a prior expectation of expression, which requires effortful control (see [[inhibitory_control]]). Specific computational models of suppression are not established.
