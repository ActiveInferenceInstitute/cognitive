# Global Workspace Theory

## Overview
Global workspace theory (GWT) proposes that consciousness corresponds to the broadcast of information to many specialised, otherwise independent processors. Bernard Baars introduced it in 1988 as a cognitive architecture; Dehaene, Changeux, and Naccache developed a neural version, the global neuronal workspace (GNW). It is a theory of access consciousness: what is conscious is what becomes globally available for report, reasoning, memory, and action control.

## Mechanism
In Baars's architecture many unconscious specialists operate in parallel with limited communication. A limited-capacity workspace, analogous to a theatre stage lit by attention, lets one coalition of content at a time be broadcast to all, enabling flexible, novel combinations that no specialist could achieve alone. The neural proposal places the workspace in a network of long-range cortical neurons, with strong representation in prefrontal and parietal cortex. A stimulus becomes conscious when it triggers "ignition": a sudden, self-sustained, nonlinear reverberation across this network. Empirically cited signatures include late (about 300 ms or later) event-related components such as the P3b, widespread gamma-band synchronisation and long-distance coupling ([[neural_synchrony]]), and the all-or-none character of visibility in masking and attentional-blink experiments ([[attentional_blink]]).

Critics argue the theory conflates access with phenomenal experience, that frontal activity may reflect report rather than consciousness, and that ignition may be a consequence rather than a correlate. A preregistered adversarial collaboration comparing GNW and integrated information theory ([[integrated_information]]) reported results that challenged predictions of both, which shows that the theories remain unsettled.

## Role in Active Inference
The two frameworks are largely complementary and are being combined. Whyte and Smith proposed a predictive global neuronal workspace: in a hierarchical generative model, ignition is cast as the selection of a posterior belief (via a form of attention-mediated policy selection) that is then made available to the whole model and used to guide action, particularly through high-level, abstract beliefs. This provides a formal active-inference model of visual consciousness. It is a proposal that reuses the Active Inference machinery rather than a derivation from the free-energy principle itself, and it inherits the open questions of GNW. Related workspace ideas also inform artificial systems; see [[artificial_consciousness]].
