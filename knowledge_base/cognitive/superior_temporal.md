# Superior Temporal Cortex

## Overview
"Superior temporal" in the linked pages refers to the superior temporal region of the lateral temporal lobe, which has two parts with different emphases: the superior temporal gyrus (STG), containing auditory cortex and posterior regions important for speech, and the superior temporal sulcus (STS), a sulcus between STG and the middle temporal gyrus involved in social and multisensory perception. Both are referred to as "superior temporal" in the source pages, and the sense is chosen from context.

## Functions
- **STG, auditory and language.** Primary and association auditory cortex lie on the upper surface of the STG. Posterior STG, including Wernicke's area, is associated with phonological analysis and the comprehension of speech ([[speech_perception]], [[language_processing]]).
- **STS, social perception.** Posterior STS responds to biological motion, gaze shifts, and mouth, hand, and body movement ([[biological_motion]], [[social_perception]]). Reviews describe it as central to perceiving the actions and intentions of others.
- **STS, multisensory.** The STS integrates auditory and visual signals, as in audiovisual speech where seen lip movements alter heard syllables ([[multisensory_integration]]).
- **Voice.** Voice-selective regions along the upper bank of the STS respond preferentially to human vocal sounds.
- **Connections.** The STS connects to temporoparietal, inferior frontal, and amygdala regions, which links perception with mentalising and emotion ([[temporoparietal]]).
- **Heterogeneity.** The STS contains functionally distinct subregions, so a single label across studies glosses over differences.

## Role in Active Inference
Superior temporal regions are used as locations for hierarchical models of auditory and speech signals. Kiebel, Daunizeau, and Friston described how a hierarchy of timescales in the brain can represent sequences such as birdsong or speech, with slower higher-level states setting the context for faster ones; such models are often mapped onto temporal cortex. Mapping specific equations to STS or STG sub-areas is an interpretive step rather than a measured result. Predictive accounts of audiovisual integration use the same hierarchy.
