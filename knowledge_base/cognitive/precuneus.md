# Precuneus

## Overview
The precuneus is the portion of the medial parietal cortex that lies behind the paracentral lobule and in front of the parieto-occipital sulcus, on the inner surface of each hemisphere. It is densely interconnected with the posterior cingulate cortex, the medial prefrontal cortex and the lateral parietal cortex, and it shows high metabolic activity during quiet wakefulness. Because it is buried in the longitudinal fissure, it was long hard to study in isolation.

## Functional organisation
- **Subdivisions.** Cavanna and Trimble (2006) described an anterior part linked to sensorimotor and body-centred processing, a central part linked to associative cognitive functions, and a posterior part linked to visual areas.
- **Cognitive correlates.** Imaging studies relate the region to visuospatial imagery, retrieval of episodic memories, taking a first-person or alternative perspective, and thinking about the self. Many of these findings come from correlational neuroimaging, so the region is better described as contributing to such functions than as uniquely responsible for them.
- **Default mode network.** Together with posterior cingulate and medial prefrontal cortex, the precuneus is a node of the default mode network, which is more active during rest and internally directed thought than during demanding external tasks (Raichle et al., 2001).
- **Consciousness.** Reduced precuneus activity and connectivity are reported in states of reduced awareness such as deep sleep and anaesthesia, although the interpretation remains debated.

## Relation to Active Inference
There is no derivation specific to the precuneus. The connection is interpretive: the default mode network has been proposed to sit at the top of cortical hierarchies, encoding high-level, relatively abstract beliefs about the self and its context, with Carhart-Harris and Friston (2010) offering a free-energy account of this idea. If that proposal holds, the precuneus would contribute to such beliefs, but this link is an analogy supported by indirect evidence, not an established result.
