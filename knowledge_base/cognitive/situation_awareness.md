# Situation Awareness

## Overview
Situation awareness is the understanding of what is happening in a dynamic environment well enough to act appropriately. In the influential definition of Endsley (1995), it consists of three levels: perception of the relevant elements in the environment, comprehension of their meaning in relation to goals, and projection of their status into the near future. The concept arose in aviation and is now used in medicine, driving, emergency response, military operations and industrial control.

## Model and measurement
Endsley's model treats situation awareness as a state of knowledge, distinct from the processes used to attain it, and as dependent on working memory, attention and long-term mental models that allow rapid pattern matching. Failures are classified by level: failure to perceive information, failure to understand it, and failure to anticipate what follows. The model has been criticised by authors who regard situation awareness as an emergent property of an interaction between people and tools, rather than a mental state of an individual, so definitions remain contested.

Measurement approaches include:
- **Freeze-probe methods**, such as the Situation Awareness Global Assessment Technique, in which a simulation is paused and the operator is asked about the current situation.
- **Real-time probes**, which ask questions while the task continues.
- **Subjective ratings** and observer ratings of the operator's behaviour.

Each has limitations, including interference with the task and reliance on self-report.

## Relation to Active Inference
The three levels line up loosely with perception, inference about causes, and prediction in a generative model. The third level, projection, corresponds to forward simulation of how the situation will evolve under possible actions, which is also how policies are evaluated in Active Inference. This is a conceptual alignment; Active Inference has not been established as a framework for measuring or modelling situation awareness in applied settings, though predictive-processing accounts of expertise are compatible with it.
