# Goal Maintenance

## Overview
Goal maintenance is the keeping of a goal or task rule in an active state over time so that it continues to guide perception, memory retrieval and action despite distraction and competing responses. It is a core executive function and is often considered a specialised use of working memory.

## Mechanisms and evidence
Miller and Cohen (2001) proposed that prefrontal cortex maintains patterns of activity that represent goals and the rules for achieving them, and that these patterns bias processing in other regions toward task-relevant pathways. Failures of maintenance appear as goal neglect: Duncan and colleagues (1996) found that people with lower fluid intelligence, and patients with frontal damage, may understand instructions yet fail to act on part of them, particularly in tasks with several rules.

Braver's dual mechanisms framework (2012) distinguishes two modes of control:
- **Proactive control.** The goal is actively maintained in advance, preparing the system for expected demands; it is effective but costs effort and resources.
- **Reactive control.** The goal is retrieved late, when interference is detected, which is cheaper but less effective.

Individuals and situations differ in which mode is used, and ageing, stress and some clinical conditions shift the balance.

Dopamine is implicated in regulating when the goal representation is updated or protected. Computational models of prefrontal and basal ganglia circuits use gating, in which a dopaminergic signal opens a gate to update contents and closes it to maintain them (O'Reilly and Frank, 2006). Maintenance interacts with emotion, since affective states can interrupt or support ongoing goals.

## Role in Active Inference
In Active Inference, goals are expressed as prior preferences over outcomes, and policies are scored by expected free energy relative to those preferences. Maintaining a goal corresponds to keeping prior preferences and beliefs about the task context stable across time so that they continue to shape policy selection. Dopamine has been proposed to encode the precision of beliefs about policies, which would determine how strongly the maintained goal determines behaviour (Friston et al., 2013). In hierarchical models, a higher level holds the slowly changing goal context while lower levels execute actions. These mappings are interpretive proposals, and the neural implementation of preferences is not established.
