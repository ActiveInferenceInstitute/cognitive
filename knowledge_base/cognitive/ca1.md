# CA1

## Overview
CA1 is a subfield of the hippocampus proper, positioned between CA2 and the subiculum. It is the principal output stage of the classical trisynaptic circuit and projects to the subiculum and to the deep layers of the [[entorhinal_cortex]], through which hippocampal information reaches the neocortex. Its pyramidal cells are central to studies of spatial coding, synaptic plasticity and memory (Andersen et al., 2007).

## Connectivity
- **Schaffer collaterals.** Axons from [[ca3]] pyramidal cells form the main input to CA1 apical dendrites in stratum radiatum.
- **Direct entorhinal input.** Layer III of the entorhinal cortex projects directly to distal dendrites of CA1 in stratum lacunosum-moleculare, so CA1 receives both a processed signal through the trisynaptic route and a more direct signal about current cortical input (Amaral and Witter, 1989).
- **Local inhibition.** A varied population of interneurons shapes timing, theta and gamma rhythms and the gating of the two input streams.

## Functions
CA1 contains place cells, neurons that fire when an animal occupies a particular location (O'Keefe and Dostrovsky, 1971). Place fields in CA1 form soon after exposure to a new environment and change with experience. Synapses from CA3 to CA1 show long-term potentiation, a model system for learning-related plasticity (see [[ltp]] and [[synaptic_plasticity]]). Lesion and imaging studies relate CA1 to the retrieval and temporal organisation of memories. CA1 cells also form sequences that bridge time gaps between events, and proposals describe the area as a place where memory is compared with current input, or where events are integrated over time (see [[episodic_memory]]). CA1 is highly sensitive to ischaemia and to damage in conditions such as Alzheimer's disease (see [[alzheimers_disease]] and [[amnesia]]). The precise computational roles of CA1 are debated.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is a conceptual proposal. Friston and Buzsaki (2016) discussed how hippocampal sequences could implement a generative model of "what" and "when", with sequential dynamics providing predictions about the unfolding of events. In this scheme, a comparison of predictions from CA3 and the direct entorhinal input could be read as detecting mismatch (see [[prediction_error]] and [[generative_model]]). Such mappings of Active Inference constructs onto hippocampal anatomy are hypotheses, and they compete with other theories of hippocampal function.
