# Augmentative Communication

## Overview
Augmentative communication, usually called augmentative and alternative communication (AAC), covers the tools, symbols, strategies and techniques used to supplement or replace spoken language when speech is absent or insufficient. Augmentative means adding to speech that exists, and alternative means substituting for it. AAC is used by people with a range of conditions, including cerebral palsy, motor neurone disease, autism, aphasia after stroke and developmental or acquired speech impairments.

## Forms and techniques
- **Unaided** systems use the body alone: gestures, facial expression, vocalisation and manual signs (see [[gesture]]).
- **Aided** systems use external materials: picture boards, communication books, letter boards, and speech-generating devices that produce synthesised or recorded speech from selected symbols or typed text.
- **Selection** can be direct, by touch, pointing or eye gaze, or indirect, by scanning through options and activating a switch when the desired item is reached.
- **Symbol and language representation** ranges from photographs and line drawings to orthography, with vocabulary organised in grids, categories or situational displays.

Interventions are generally planned around the person's abilities, partners and environments, and communication partner training is a standard component (Beukelman & Light, 2020). Reviews of intervention studies have reported no evidence that AAC use impedes natural speech production. Technology has changed the field, with mainstream tablets and eye-tracking replacing many dedicated devices (Light & McNaughton, 2012).

## Relation to Active Inference
There is no direct link to Active Inference. A loose connection is that word and phrase prediction in AAC software reduces keystrokes by anticipating likely continuations, which resembles the predictive processing view of language in broad outline only. Brain-computer interfaces that decode attempted speech belong to the neighbouring topic of [[brain_machine_interfaces]].
