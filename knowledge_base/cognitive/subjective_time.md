# Subjective Time

## Overview
Subjective time is time as experienced: how long an interval seems to last, in what order events appear to occur, and how quickly time seems to pass. It differs from physical clock time in systematic ways. An interval filled with attention-demanding events may seem short while it is happening yet long in retrospect, and a wait during which attention is directed at the passage of time often seems prolonged. Researchers separate prospective timing, in which a person knows in advance that duration will be judged, from retrospective timing, in which the judgement is made from memory afterwards. The two rely on different resources and can dissociate.

## Accounts of timing
- **Pacemaker-accumulator models.** In scalar expectancy theory a pacemaker emits pulses, an attention-dependent switch gates them, and an accumulator counts them; the count is compared with remembered durations (Gibbon, Church and Meck, 1984). A defining empirical regularity is the scalar property: variability of timing estimates grows roughly in proportion to the interval being timed.
- **Distributed or state-dependent models.** Timing is read out from the evolving state of neural populations, without a single dedicated clock. Evidence for neurons whose activity is tied to elapsed time is reviewed in [[time_cells]].
- **Interoceptive and event-based proposals.** Wittmann (2013) proposed that the insula integrates bodily signals over time and that duration is derived from accumulated salient changes. The proposal is influential but not settled.

Timing judgements are modulated by attention, arousal, emotion and stimulus properties such as size, intensity and repetition. Pharmacological work in animals and clinical observations in basal ganglia disorders implicate dopaminergic circuits in interval timing (see [[dopamine_system]]). Perceived order and simultaneity rest on a separate, shorter-range temporal integration (see [[temporal_integration]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is exploratory. A discrete-time generative model indexes inference by steps rather than by experienced duration, so subjective duration is not an output of the standard formalism. Some authors suggest that the felt rate of time depends on how much change is inferred, or on the precision of interoceptive predictions, which would tie timing to [[predictive_processing]]. These are research hypotheses, and no accepted derivation connects subjective duration to expected free energy. The phenomenological side of the topic is treated in [[time_consciousness]].
