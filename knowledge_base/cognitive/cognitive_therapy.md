# Cognitive Therapy

## Overview
Cognitive therapy is a form of psychotherapy developed by Aaron Beck (1976) on the premise that emotional states and behaviour are strongly influenced by how situations are appraised. It focuses on identifying and testing habitual interpretations and underlying beliefs. Combined with behavioural methods, it forms cognitive behavioural therapy (see [[behavioral_therapy]]). This page covers the concepts and associated computational hypotheses; it offers no treatment guidance.

## Core elements
- **Cognitive model**: situations trigger automatic thoughts, which shape emotion and behaviour, and are themselves generated by deeper assumptions and core beliefs (schemas) about self, world and future.
- **Cognitive distortions**: systematic patterns such as all-or-nothing thinking, overgeneralisation and catastrophising.
- **Cognitive restructuring**: recording thoughts, examining evidence for and against them, and generating alternative appraisals (see [[emotion_regulation]]).
- **Behavioural experiments**: planned activities that test a belief against observed outcomes, treating beliefs as hypotheses.

Evidence for the efficacy of cognitive and cognitive-behavioural therapies comes from clinical trials, and mechanisms of change, whether through belief change, skills or other pathways, remain debated.

## Role in Active Inference
The cognitive model resembles a hierarchical generative model in which high-level priors about the self and world shape the interpretation of events (see [[hierarchical_models]], [[prior_beliefs]]). In the computational psychiatry literature, some symptoms are hypothesised to involve priors or precisions that are overly strong or poorly updated, so that expected evidence fails to revise beliefs (see [[computational_psychiatry]], Adams, Huys and Roiser, 2016). On this reading, behavioural experiments supply observations that generate prediction errors, and restructuring corresponds to revising beliefs and their precisions (see [[belief_updating]], [[prediction_error]]). These are hypotheses and interpretive parallels. The mapping between specific therapeutic operations and model parameters is not established, the same model features can be attached to many conditions, and such models do not currently guide care.
