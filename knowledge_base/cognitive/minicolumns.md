# Minicolumns

## Overview
A minicolumn, sometimes called a microcolumn, is a thin vertical chain of neurons running through the layers of the cerebral cortex, perpendicular to the surface. Counts commonly quoted are of the order of 80 to 100 neurons per minicolumn, with a width of a few tens of micrometres, although estimates vary with area and species. Mountcastle proposed that minicolumns are the basic modules from which larger [[macrocolumns]] are assembled, and that cortex gains its areal differences largely by varying the number and connections of a common unit (Mountcastle, 1997).

## Anatomical basis
- **Cell arrangement.** Cell bodies in cortical layers II to VI often line up in narrow radial stacks, visible in stained sections and in the vertical bundling of pyramidal cell apical dendrites.
- **Connections.** Inputs from the thalamus and from cortico-cortical pathways are thought to distribute mainly along these vertical lines, with inhibitory interneurons restricting lateral spread between neighbouring columns.
- **Development.** One proposal is that minicolumns arise from radial migration of neurons born in the same proliferative zone along radial glial fibres.

## The minicolumn hypothesis
Buxhoeveden and Casanova (2002) reviewed the evidence for treating the minicolumn as a fundamental unit of cortical processing and a possible site of change in some neurodevelopmental conditions. Reports of altered minicolumn spacing in such conditions are of interest but are not firmly established. Critics note that minicolumns are difficult to define consistently, that their boundaries are not sharp, and that their physiological role is less clear than their appearance suggests (Horton and Adams, 2005).

## Relation to Active Inference
Theories of predictive coding assign prediction and prediction-error functions to specific populations in the layers of the canonical cortical microcircuit (Bastos and colleagues, 2012; see [[predictive_coding]]), and minicolumn-sized units are a candidate level for repeated copies of such a circuit. This is a modelling hypothesis, not an empirical demonstration that minicolumns implement it.
