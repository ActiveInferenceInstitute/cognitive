# Dorsal Stream

## Overview
In current models of speech and language, the dorsal stream is a pathway that maps acoustic speech signals onto articulatory representations, linking auditory areas of the temporal lobe with parietal and frontal motor regions. It forms one half of the dual-stream model proposed by Hickok and Poeppel (2004, 2007), in which the [[ventral_stream]] maps sound to meaning. The term is borrowed from vision, where Ungerleider and Mishkin (1982) described a dorsal pathway for spatial location and Goodale and Milner (1992) argued that it supports visually guided action.

## Anatomy and function
After spectrotemporal analysis in auditory cortex and the superior temporal gyrus, the dorsal route projects through a sensorimotor interface in the posterior Sylvian region at the parietal-temporal boundary, often labelled Spt and lying in or near the planum temporale, to premotor cortex and the posterior inferior frontal gyrus. The pathway is typically more strongly left-lateralised than the ventral one. Its proposed functions include speech production and repetition, learning new words and phonological sequences, and keeping a phonological trace in short-term memory. Damage to the dorsal pathway is associated with conduction aphasia, in which repetition is impaired despite relatively preserved comprehension.

## Alternatives
Other accounts place the dorsal pathway in syntactic processing as well, and anatomical studies of the white matter, notably the arcuate fasciculus, show that the dorsal route is not a single tract. Saur and colleagues (2008) combined functional imaging with diffusion tractography and found that sublexical speech repetition engaged a dorsal pathway, running through the arcuate and superior longitudinal fascicles to premotor cortex, whereas higher-level comprehension engaged a ventral pathway through the extreme capsule.

## Relation to Active Inference
The most direct link is through forward models. Speech production involves predicting the auditory and somatosensory consequences of articulation, and Hickok (2012) interpreted the dorsal sensorimotor interface as implementing state feedback control with such predictions. That fits predictive-processing accounts in which prediction errors correct articulation, and the same formalism describes [[corollary_discharge]]. It is an analogy at the level of architecture, and no Active Inference model of the whole dual-stream system is established.
