# Minimal Self

## Overview
The minimal self is the most basic form of self-awareness: the sense that an experience is mine, given from the first-person perspective, without reflection, memory or any explicit self-description. The notion is developed in phenomenological philosophy and was brought into cognitive science by Shaun Gallagher (2000), who contrasts it with the more elaborate [[narrative_self]]. Dan Zahavi has argued that it is a feature of experience itself, a form of pre-reflective self-awareness present in every conscious state.

## Components
- **Sense of ownership.** The experience that a body part or a mental state belongs to me, studied through illusions such as the rubber hand illusion (see [[body_ownership]]).
- **Sense of agency.** The experience of being the initiator of an action and its effects (see [[agency]]), often linked to comparisons between predicted and actual sensory consequences of movement (see [[efference_copy]] and [[forward_models]]).
- **Perspectival unity.** Experience is organised around a point of view, which is present even when no self-concept is in use.

Gallagher distinguishes the minimal self from the narrative self, which extends across time through memory and the stories people tell about themselves. Philosophers disagree on whether the minimal self is a real feature of consciousness or whether apparent selves are constructions; some, such as Metzinger, deny that there is a self in a substantive sense.

## Relation to Active Inference
This is an active research area. Several authors treat the minimal self as inferred rather than given: the brain models the body as the common cause of interoceptive, proprioceptive and exteroceptive signals, and the experience of being a self is the content of that model (Seth, 2013; Apps and Tsakiris, 2014). Interoceptive inference is emphasised in particular (see [[interoception]] and [[self_modeling]]). These proposals are empirically motivated, for example by studies of ownership illusions, but they remain theoretical, and their relation to the phenomenological claim that the minimal self is pre-reflective is debated.
