# Deep Pyramidal Cells

## Overview
Deep pyramidal cells are the excitatory projection neurons whose cell bodies lie in the infragranular layers of neocortex, layers 5 and 6. They are the main output neurons of a cortical column toward subcortical structures and, together with some superficial cells, toward other cortical areas. In predictive coding accounts they are proposed to carry the predictions that a higher area sends to a lower one.

## Anatomy and physiology
Thick-tufted layer 5 pyramidal cells have large somata and apical dendrites that ascend to layer 1, where they branch into a distal tuft. They project to the striatum, thalamus, midbrain, brainstem and spinal cord, and they contribute backward connections to earlier cortical areas. Layer 6 cells include a population that projects back to the thalamus and modulates its transmission.

A distinctive property is dendritic coupling. Input to the distal tuft in layer 1, which carries much of the feedback, can trigger calcium spikes that interact with action potentials at the soma and favour burst firing when basal and apical inputs coincide (Larkum, Zhu & Sakmann, 1999). Larkum (2013) proposed that this mechanism associates bottom-up input arriving near the soma with top-down input arriving at the tuft.

## Role in Active Inference
Bastos and colleagues (2012) assigned deep pyramidal cells to the representation of expectations about the causes of sensory input. They send predictions down the hierarchy to the superficial cells of the lower area, where mismatches are computed as prediction errors (see [[superficial_pyramidal_cells]] and [[prediction_error]]). The proposal links laminar anatomy to frequency bands, with backward influences carried at lower frequencies such as beta. This is an active research programme that makes testable laminar predictions. Evidence is mixed, and other accounts place prediction-error neurons in different cell classes or describe computations in terms of mismatch signals in interneurons (Keller & Mrsic-Flogel, 2018). The identification should therefore be treated as a hypothesis.
