# Perceptual Constancy

## Overview
Perceptual constancy is the stability of how objects appear when the sensory input they produce changes. A white sheet looks white in dim light and in sunlight, although the light reaching the eye differs greatly. A door looks rectangular as it swings open, although its retinal image becomes a trapezoid. Constancy therefore separates properties of the world from properties of the viewing conditions, and it is one of the central achievements of perception.

## Main forms
- **Size constancy**: perceived size remains roughly fixed as viewing distance changes the retinal image (see [[size_constancy]]).
- **Shape constancy**: perceived shape remains fixed as orientation changes the projected outline (see [[shape_constancy]]).
- **Lightness and colour constancy**: surface reflectance and hue are estimated despite changes in illumination (see [[color_processing]], [[brightness_perception]]).
- **Location and motion constancy**: the world appears stationary when the eyes or head move, and objects keep their position relative to the observer's own movement.

Constancy is rarely perfect. Under reduced cues, such as viewing through an aperture or in darkness, perception shifts toward the proximal stimulus, and the degree of constancy grows with the availability of contextual information such as depth cues, surrounding surfaces and illumination gradients.

## Explanations
The dominant account is inferential. Helmholtz described perception as unconscious inference, and later work formalised the idea as the estimation of distal causes from proximal data. A retinal image is the product of several factors, for example illumination times reflectance, or object size divided by distance. Because many combinations produce the same image, the visual system must use cues and prior knowledge to apportion the image among its causes. A related tradition, associated with ecological psychology, argues that invariants in the optic array specify stable properties directly, so that constancy needs no inference (see [[ecological_psychology]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
The inferential account is closely continuous with predictive processing. A generative model contains separate variables for object properties and viewing conditions, and perceptual inference inverts the model to recover the former while explaining away the latter (see [[perceptual_inference]], [[generative_model]]). On this reading constancy is explaining away, and illusions arise when the prior over viewing conditions is wrong. This is an interpretive framework with a good fit to the behavioural evidence, not a settled neural account; specific implementations remain under investigation.
