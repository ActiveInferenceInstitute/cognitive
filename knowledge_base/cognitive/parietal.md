# Parietal Cortex

## Overview
The parietal lobe lies between the central sulcus and the parieto-occipital sulcus, above the temporal lobe. It combines body-related and spatial information from several senses and uses it to guide attention and movement. This page gives the lobe-level overview; the functionally dense posterior region is treated in [[posterior_parietal]] and the lower lateral region in [[inferior_parietal]].

## Subdivisions and functions
- **Postcentral gyrus (primary somatosensory cortex)**: touch, pressure, and proprioception, arranged somatotopically.
- **Superior parietal lobule**: visuomotor coordination for reaching, body-in-space representation, and spatial working memory.
- **Intraparietal sulcus**: sensory-motor transformations, saccade and attention control, and magnitude processing including number sense.
- **Inferior parietal lobule**: language-related processing, tool use and action understanding, attention reorienting, and aspects of social cognition ([[inferior_parietal]]).
- **Medial parietal (precuneus)**: part of the default mode network, linked to self-related and episodic retrieval processes.

Parietal damage yields characteristic syndromes. Hemispatial neglect, most severe after right-hemisphere lesions, leaves patients failing to attend to contralesional space despite intact primary sensation. Balint's syndrome combines simultanagnosia, optic ataxia, and impaired eye-movement control. Apraxia impairs skilled movement, and Gerstmann syndrome (a controversial grouping) combines finger agnosia, acalculia, agraphia, and left-right confusion. Functional imaging links the dorsal frontoparietal network to goal-directed attention and a ventral network to stimulus-driven reorienting ([[attention_networks]]).

The region is also repeatedly implicated in evidence accumulation for decisions, working memory, and skill learning, which accounts for citations from decision-making and skill-acquisition pages ([[evidence_accumulation]], [[skill_acquisition]]).

## Role in Active Inference
Parietal cortex is linked to the framework mainly through attention and body representation. Attention is modelled as the optimization of precision on sensory channels, and dorsal frontoparietal activity is a proposed locus of that gain control ([[precision_weighting]]). Body-schema and proprioceptive inference involve parietal estimates of limb state that action then fulfils ([[body_schema]], [[proprioception]]). Neglect has been modelled as an imbalance in spatial precision or priors. These are modelling proposals compatible with lesion and imaging data but not unique to Active Inference.
