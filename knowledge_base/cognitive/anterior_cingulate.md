# Anterior Cingulate Cortex

## Overview
The anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) is the medial frontal region curving around the front of the corpus callosum, corresponding broadly to Brodmann areas 24, 32, 33 and the subgenual area 25. It is densely connected with prefrontal, insular, limbic, and autonomic and motor-related structures, and is consistently active when behaviour requires control, error processing, or evaluation of costs and benefits.

## Mechanism
Subdivisions differ in function. The dorsal ACC (and adjacent anterior midcingulate cortex) is associated with [[conflict_monitoring]], error detection (reflected in the error-related negativity in EEG), and effort and action evaluation. Pregenual and subgenual regions are more connected to limbic and autonomic systems and are linked to affect and visceral control. Pain, social exclusion, and negative feedback also engage the region.

Theories of dorsal ACC function overlap but differ in emphasis. The conflict-monitoring account holds that it detects simultaneous activation of competing responses and signals the need for increased control. Shenhav, Botvinick, and Cohen's expected value of control account holds that it estimates the payoff of allocating control and specifies how much and what type to engage. Kolling and colleagues reported ACC signals tracking the value of switching away from the current option in foraging-like decisions. Alexander and Brown proposed that it learns to predict the likely outcomes of actions and signals deviations. Which of these accounts captures the core function is unresolved, and recordings show heterogeneous populations.

## Role in Active Inference
The ACC is a frequently proposed locus of precision control and of prediction-error signalling for action outcomes. Alexander and Brown's predicted response-outcome model treats medial frontal cortex as predicting outcomes and responding to unexpected ones, which resembles prediction-error computation in [[predictive_processing]] accounts. In Active Inference, signals of conflict or surprise would be expected to adjust the precision of competing policies and thereby the engagement of control ([[precision_weighting]], [[policy_selection]]); visceromotor ACC subdivisions would be involved in autonomic and interoceptive prediction. These mappings are proposals: the cortical microcircuit and precision-encoding claims have not been established for the ACC specifically, and several non-Bayesian theories fit the data comparably well.
