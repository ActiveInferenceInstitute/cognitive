# Pars Triangularis

## Overview
The pars triangularis is the triangular middle section of the inferior frontal gyrus, between the pars opercularis behind and the pars orbitalis below and in front. It corresponds approximately to Brodmann area 45. Together with the pars opercularis (area 44) it is the standard anatomical definition of [[brocas_area]], the frontal region associated since the nineteenth century with disturbed speech production after left-hemisphere damage. This page covers the subregion; see [[pars_orbitalis]] for the more ventral part.

## Functions proposed for the region
Imaging studies find activation in the left pars triangularis during sentence comprehension and production, with stronger responses for syntactically complex sentences, such as object-relative clauses, than for simple ones. Friederici (2012) describes a language circuit in which area 45 contributes to syntactic and semantic processing, whereas area 44 is more closely tied to syntactic structure building. Hagoort (2005) proposes a memory, unification and control framework in which the inferior frontal gyrus combines word information into larger structures, with area 45 important for semantic and syntactic unification.

## Specificity debate
Whether the region is specialised for language is contested. Grodzinsky and Santi (2008) review the claim that the region supports syntactic movement operations and compare it with alternatives that stress working memory and cognitive control. Fedorenko and colleagues showed, in individually defined regions, that language-selective responses and responses to general cognitive demand lie side by side within Broca's area (Fedorenko et al., 2012), which suggests that group-averaged analyses can blur two neighbouring populations. Lesion studies also complicate the picture, since persistent speech production difficulty typically requires damage extending beyond the inferior frontal gyrus. The balance of evidence thus favours a heterogeneous region with both language-specific and domain-general components, though the proportion is argued.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is limited. Unifying words into structures could be modelled as inference over hierarchical syntactic states, and control demands as changes in precision, but a model identifying these functions with area 45 specifically is not established.
