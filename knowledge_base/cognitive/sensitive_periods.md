# Sensitive Periods

## Overview
A sensitive period is a time in development when a neural system is especially responsive to a particular kind of input, so that experience has a stronger and longer-lasting effect than at other times. Knudsen (2004) distinguished sensitive periods from critical periods: in a critical period, appropriate input is required for normal development and the effects of its absence are largely irreversible, whereas a sensitive period is a window of heightened plasticity outside which change is still possible but harder. In practice the boundary is graded, and the terms are used loosely.

## Examples
- **Visual cortex.** Hubel and Wiesel (1970) showed that closing one eye in young kittens shifted cortical responses toward the open eye, an effect that was strong only during a limited early period. In humans, early deprivation such as untreated congenital cataract or amblyopia can impair acuity, with treatment most effective when begun early.
- **Language.** Language exposure in early childhood appears to be associated with better eventual proficiency, and late first-language learners often have lasting difficulty in some aspects of grammar and phonology, although the exact timing and shape of the window are debated.
- **Auditory localisation and birdsong.** The barn owl's alignment of auditory and visual maps and the learning of song in some birds show tuned windows.

## Mechanisms
Hensch (2005) reviewed work indicating that the opening of a critical period depends on maturation of inhibitory circuits, particularly parvalbumin-expressing interneurons, and that closure is associated with structural brakes, including perineuronal nets and myelin-related signals. Experimental manipulation of these factors can partly reopen plasticity in adult animals, which is a focus of research on recovery from deprivation.

## Relation to Active Inference
In predictive terms, early life is a period in which a system builds a generative model with high learning rates, and later stability may reflect priors that become more precise. This is a conceptual reading; no single Active Inference model is standard for sensitive periods.
