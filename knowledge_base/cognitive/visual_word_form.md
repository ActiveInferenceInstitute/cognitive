# Visual Word Form Area

## Overview
The visual word form area (VWFA) is a region of the left ventral occipitotemporal cortex, in or near the mid-fusiform gyrus and occipitotemporal sulcus, that responds more strongly to written words and letter strings than to other visual categories such as faces, objects or checkerboards. It is proposed to support recognition of the visual form of a word before its sound and meaning are accessed. A related term, the visual word form, denotes the abstract orthographic representation itself.

## Evidence
- **Imaging.** Cohen and colleagues (2000) reported that the region was activated by words regardless of their position in the visual field, case or font, which suggests an abstract, location-independent code.
- **Lesions.** Damage to this region or its connections is associated with pure alexia, or letter-by-letter reading, in which a person can write and speak normally but reads slowly (see [[pure_alexia]]).
- **Development.** The region emerges with literacy. Compared with illiterate adults, literate adults show stronger responses to written strings in this area, together with changes in neighbouring cortex responsive to faces (Dehaene et al., 2010). Neuronal recycling is the proposal that reading co-opts cortex evolved for object recognition, whose properties suit it for learning the shapes of letters (Dehaene and Cohen, 2007).

## Debate
Price and Devlin (2003) argued that the region should not be regarded as specialised for words, since it responds to other stimuli and tasks, including naming pictures. Supporters respond that the region has stronger word selectivity at the level of its population response when stimuli and tasks are controlled. Both sides agree that the region is consistently involved in reading.

## Relation to Active Inference
Price and Devlin (2011) proposed an interactive account in which the ventral occipitotemporal cortex combines bottom-up visual input with top-down predictions from phonological and semantic regions, using a predictive coding framework. On this view, activity reflects prediction error as well as the stimulus, which accounts for task effects and for responses to non-words. This is a direct application of hierarchical predictive coding to a specific region and is supported by imaging data, though competing accounts also fit the same data.
