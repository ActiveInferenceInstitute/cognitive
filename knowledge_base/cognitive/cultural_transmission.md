# Cultural Transmission

## Overview
Cultural transmission is the spread of information between individuals by social learning rather than genetic inheritance. The transmitted material includes technologies, language, norms and beliefs. Cavalli-Sforza and Feldman (1981) distinguished vertical transmission (parent to offspring), horizontal transmission (among peers) and oblique transmission (from non-parental members of an older generation). Mechanisms include imitation, teaching, emulation and language (see [[imitation]], [[social_learning]], [[observational_learning]]).

## Transmission biases
Boyd and Richerson (1985) analysed how learners choose whom and what to copy, with biases that shape which variants spread:
- **Content bias**: preference for variants that are easier to learn, remember or use.
- **Conformist bias**: disproportionate copying of the majority variant.
- **Prestige or success bias**: copying individuals who appear skilled or successful.

Henrich (2016) argued that cumulative culture, in which innovations accumulate across generations beyond what any individual could invent, is a distinguishing feature of human societies and depends on population size and high-fidelity transmission. Tomasello (1999) emphasised specialised social-cognitive abilities, such as shared attention and intention reading, as supporting this ratchet (see [[joint_attention]], [[theory_of_mind]]). Heyes (2018) argued that many such abilities are themselves culturally acquired. These positions are debated and the empirical evidence for demographic effects on cultural complexity is contested.

## Role in Active Inference
Variational treatments consider culture as shared priors and shared generative models that agents acquire from others and maintain through their joint activity. Constant and colleagues (2018) formulated niche construction variationally, in which agents modify the environment that shapes their own and others' inference. Veissière and colleagues (2020) proposed that cultural learning is inference about, and conformity to, shared expectations, which bias individual priors toward locally normative ones (see [[social_norms]], [[multi_agent_active_inference]]). This is an active research programme with conceptual and simulation results; claims that it explains specific cultural phenomena better than existing evolutionary models are not established, and it draws on, rather than replaces, the cultural-evolution literature.
