# Eye-Hand Coordination

## Overview
Eye-hand coordination is the organisation of gaze and manual movement so that vision supplies the information that reaching, grasping and manipulation need. It requires converting retinal information into a motor plan for the arm, and it includes the temporal alignment of saccades with hand movements, the use of visual feedback to correct the hand, and the use of the hand's position and proprioception to guide the eye.

## Behavioural findings
In everyday tasks, gaze usually leads the hand. Land (2006) reviewed studies of tea-making, sandwich-making and driving showing that fixations fall on objects shortly before they are used, and that fixations are closely tied to the current sub-goal, with little attention to task-irrelevant objects. In object manipulation, Johansson and colleagues (2001) found that gaze is directed to contact points and to targets for the object, and not to the moving hand; the hand is monitored largely through proprioception and peripheral vision. Reaching to a target generally begins after or around the time the eyes reach it, and accuracy is higher when the target is foveated. Gaze typically stays on the target until the hand arrives.

## Neural basis
The posterior parietal cortex is central to transforming visual target locations into reach plans, with the dorsal premotor and primary motor cortex implementing movement (see [[posterior_parietal]]). The cerebellum contributes to the timing and calibration of movements, and the superior colliculus and frontal eye fields support the control of gaze. Disorders affecting these regions, such as optic ataxia after parietal lesions, impair visually guided reaching while leaving other aspects of vision relatively intact.

## Relation to Active Inference
Active Inference treats eye movements as actions that sample the most informative observations (see [[active_sensing]] and [[epistemic_foraging]]), and treats reaching as the fulfilment of proprioceptive predictions generated by a model of the arm. Eye-hand coordination then follows from selecting gaze and reach policies under one generative model. This account is a framework backed by simulations of simple tasks, and its fit to detailed human behaviour is still being tested.
