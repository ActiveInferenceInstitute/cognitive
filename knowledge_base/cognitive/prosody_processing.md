# Prosody Processing

## Overview
Prosody refers to the patterns of rhythm, stress, pitch and timing that extend over stretches of speech larger than individual sounds. Prosody processing is the set of perceptual and cognitive operations by which listeners extract and use these patterns. The acoustic correlates are fundamental frequency, duration, intensity and pauses, which listeners combine into percepts of pitch movement, emphasis and phrasing.

## Functions
- **Word and phrase segmentation.** Stress patterns and phrase-final lengthening provide cues to word boundaries and to the grouping of words into phrases. Cutler, Dahan and van Donselaar (1997) review evidence that listeners use stress and rhythm in spoken-word recognition in a way that depends on the language.
- **Syntactic structure.** Prosodic boundaries can resolve ambiguity, as when a pause distinguishes alternative attachments of a phrase.
- **Information structure.** Pitch accents mark focus and new or contrasting information.
- **Pragmatic and emotional meaning.** Intonation distinguishes questions from statements and conveys speaker attitude and emotion, including irony and affect.

## Neural basis
Evidence from patients and imaging suggests that processing is distributed across both hemispheres. Pitch and affective prosody tend to engage the right hemisphere more strongly, while linguistic prosody that is tied to syntax and lexical tone engages left hemisphere areas. Friederici and Alter (2004) proposed a dynamic dual-pathway model in which the hemispheres interact through the corpus callosum to integrate prosody with syntax. Impaired affective prosody perception and production, termed aprosodia, can follow right-hemisphere damage, and prosody perception is altered in several clinical conditions, including some forms of autism and schizophrenia, though findings vary by task.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is an interpretation within predictive accounts of speech. Prosody supplies a slow temporal scaffold that constrains predictions about when and which words and phrase boundaries will arrive, consistent with hierarchical models operating at several timescales. It has also been proposed that prosodic variation contributes to inferring a speaker's mental state. Direct modelling of prosody within Active Inference is limited.
