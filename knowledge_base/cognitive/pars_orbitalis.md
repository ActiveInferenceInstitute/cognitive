# Pars Orbitalis

## Overview
The inferior frontal gyrus is conventionally divided into three parts: the pars opercularis, the pars triangularis and the pars orbitalis. The pars orbitalis is the most anterior and ventral, lying below the pars triangularis on the orbital side of the gyrus, and corresponds approximately to Brodmann area 47. Unlike the other two parts, it is not usually counted as part of the classical [[brocas_area]], which is generally taken to be areas 44 and 45, although some authors use the term more broadly.

## Semantic control
Functional imaging studies of language comprehension often find activation in the anterior and ventral inferior frontal gyrus when semantic judgements are difficult: when a word has competing meanings, when the relation to be judged is weak or unusual, or when several candidate answers must be chosen between. Badre and Wagner (2007) distinguish two processes in left ventrolateral prefrontal cortex, controlled retrieval of knowledge held in posterior temporal cortex, found more anteriorly, and post-retrieval selection among competing alternatives, found more posteriorly. On this view the pars orbitalis does not store meaning but biases which stored meaning is accessed, working with the stores discussed under [[semantic_memory]]. Hagoort (2005) places semantic unification and control within the left inferior frontal gyrus as a whole, with a gradient from semantic processing in ventral parts to phonological processing in dorsal parts.

## Uncertainties
The division of labour inside the gyrus is not settled. Functional boundaries do not follow cytoarchitectonic ones neatly, individuals differ in anatomy, and some studies report that the same regions respond to non-linguistic control demands. Friederici (2012) assigns syntactic processing to the more posterior part of the gyrus and semantic processing to the more anterior, consistent with the proposal above, but critics note that most evidence is correlational. Lesion work is limited because strokes rarely respect these subdivisions.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is weak. Controlled semantic retrieval might be expressed as top-down selection of an interpretation under uncertainty, and cognitive control is sometimes modelled as precision weighting, but no model specific to this subregion is established.
