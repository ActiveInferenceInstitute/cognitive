# Ecological Psychology

## Overview
Ecological psychology is the approach to perception and action developed by James J. Gibson, set out in The Senses Considered as Perceptual Systems (1966) and The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception (1979). It holds that perception is the pick-up of information that specifies the environment for an animal that is actively moving, so that the proper unit of study is the animal-environment system and not an isolated brain processing inputs.

## Mechanism
Several central concepts structure the approach. The ambient optic array is the structured light available at a point of observation, and Gibson argued that it contains invariants, such as optic flow patterns during locomotion, that specify layout and self-motion without needing to be reconstructed from retinal snapshots. Perception is therefore described as direct: no internal inferential step from impoverished inputs is posited. Affordances are the opportunities for action that the environment offers an animal, relative to its capabilities: a surface affords walking, a gap affords passing through. Warren (1984) showed that people perceive stair-climbability in terms of leg length, with a body-scaled critical ratio, a result often used to support the view that affordances are relational properties of animal and environment. Perception and action form a continuous loop in which movement generates the information that guides further movement, so exploring is part of perceiving.

Later work by Turvey, Shaw, Reed, Michaels and Carello formalized affordances and information in terms of dynamical systems and ecological physics. The approach has been criticized, notably by Fodor and Pylyshyn (1981), for leaving unexplained how information can be picked up without internal representation, and for difficulty with perception of absent or abstract things.

## Role in Active Inference
The relation is productive but tense. Active Inference shares the emphasis on action as part of perception and on the animal-environment loop, and some authors have proposed that affordances correspond to action possibilities that a generative model makes salient (Bruineberg and Rietveld 2014 relate self-organization and free-energy minimization to the field of relevant affordances an agent sensitive to its situation). The tension is that Active Inference is standardly representationalist, with internal generative models and Bayesian beliefs, whereas Gibson rejected mediating representations. Proponents of a non-representational reading of the framework and of a representational reading both exist, so the status of the synthesis is unsettled. The page on 4E cognition discusses the wider context.
