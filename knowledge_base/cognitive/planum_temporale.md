# Planum Temporale

## Overview
The planum temporale is a roughly triangular patch of cortex on the upper surface of the temporal lobe, lying behind Heschl's gyrus within the Sylvian fissure. It forms part of auditory association cortex and, on the left side, overlaps the territory that classical accounts assign to Wernicke's area. Its anatomy and function have been studied mainly in relation to language and to the processing of complex sounds.

## Asymmetry and function
- **Anatomical asymmetry.** Geschwind and Levitsky (1968) reported that the planum temporale is larger on the left than on the right in most human brains. The finding was influential because it suggested a structural correlate of left-hemisphere language dominance. Later work showed that the relationship between asymmetry and language lateralisation is weaker and more variable than first thought, and the reported extent of asymmetry depends on how the region is delimited.
- **Computational hub.** Griffiths and Warren (2002) proposed that the planum temporale segregates and matches spectrotemporal patterns arriving from earlier auditory cortex, and then routes the results to regions concerned with identity, location or action. On this view it is not specific to speech, and it also responds to music, environmental sounds and sound-source movement.
- **Sensorimotor interface.** A posterior portion of the Sylvian region at the parietal-temporal boundary has been proposed to link auditory representations with the motor system for speech production (Hickok & Poeppel, 2007).

## Relation to Active Inference
There is no Active Inference account specific to this region. The relevant framing is that speech and sound perception can be modelled as hierarchical inference over temporally structured causes, with early auditory areas encoding acoustic detail and higher areas encoding sequences and categories; the planum temporale is plausibly an intermediate stage in such a hierarchy. Simulations of generative models for sound sequences exist, but assigning particular computations to this region is an analogy rather than an established result.
