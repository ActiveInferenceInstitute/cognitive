# Temporoparietal Junction

## Overview
The temporoparietal junction (TPJ) is the cortex where the posterior superior temporal, inferior parietal, and lateral occipital regions meet, centred near the posterior end of the Sylvian fissure. It is not a single anatomical area. Its boundaries differ across studies, and parcellation work has found that anterior and posterior parts connect to different networks. Pages that link here use it in three senses: viewpoint-taking, attentional reorienting, and social inference.

## Functions
- **Mental-state reasoning.** The right TPJ (and the left) is more active when people reason about others' beliefs, for instance in false-belief tasks, than when they reason about physical representations ([[theory_of_mind]], [[perspective_taking]]). Whether this is specific to mental states or reflects more general reorienting or contextual updating is debated.
- **Reorienting of attention.** Right TPJ and ventral frontal cortex form a ventral attention network that responds to behaviourally relevant, unexpected stimuli, notably targets at invalid cue locations, and is proposed to interrupt ongoing focus ([[attention_networks]], [[exogenous_attention]]).
- **Self and body.** Stimulation or lesions of TPJ are associated with altered self-location, out-of-body experiences, and sense-of-agency changes, linking it to self-other distinction ([[body_schema]]).
- **Cooperation.** Activation during decisions that require considering a partner's perspective is reported in economic exchange tasks ([[cooperation]]).

## Role in Active Inference
Two interpretive links exist. First, the reorienting response to an invalid cue is a case of surprise relative to a learned expectation, so it can be read as a prediction-error or belief-updating signal about the context ([[prediction_error]], [[precision_weighting]]). Second, mentalising requires inferring another agent's generative model, a computation that is formalised in multi-agent Active Inference ([[multi_agent_active_inference]]). Assigning these computations to the TPJ is a hypothesis; the unification of attention and mentalising functions in a single region is not established.
