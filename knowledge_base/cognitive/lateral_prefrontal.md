# Lateral Prefrontal Cortex

## Overview
Lateral prefrontal cortex (LPFC) is the part of [[prefrontal]] cortex on the outer surface of the frontal lobe, anterior to premotor regions. It is usually divided into a dorsolateral sector, centred on Brodmann areas 9 and 46 (see [[dorsolateral_prefrontal]]), and a ventrolateral sector around areas 44, 45 and 47. It is richly interconnected with parietal, temporal and other prefrontal areas and is a core node of frontoparietal networks (see [[frontoparietal]]).

## Functions
- **Working memory.** Neurons in the primate LPFC show sustained activity during delays, and lesions impair delayed-response tasks.
- **Rule and goal representation.** Miller and Cohen (2001) proposed that prefrontal cortex maintains representations of goals and rules that bias processing elsewhere in favour of task-relevant pathways, which is a central idea in accounts of [[cognitive_control]].
- **Selection and retrieval.** The ventrolateral sector is associated with selecting among competing representations, including semantic retrieval and response inhibition.
- **Hierarchical control.** Imaging studies, such as Koechlin and colleagues (2003), suggest a rostro-caudal gradient in which more anterior LPFC supports control over more abstract contexts, although the exact organisation is debated.

## Role in metacognition
Lateral and anterior prefrontal regions are among those whose activity or structure relates to individual differences in metacognitive accuracy, meaning how well confidence tracks performance (see [[metacognition]] and [[confidence_judgment]]). Fleming and Dolan (2012) review this literature; causal evidence is more limited than correlational evidence.

## Relation to Active Inference
In predictive processing accounts, prefrontal cortex occupies the upper levels of a hierarchical generative model, encoding slowly changing, abstract contextual beliefs that constrain lower-level predictions. Working memory can then be described as maintaining beliefs over hidden states through time, and control as the precision weighting of competing policies (see [[precision_weighting]]). These are theoretical proposals supported by simulations rather than direct measurements.
