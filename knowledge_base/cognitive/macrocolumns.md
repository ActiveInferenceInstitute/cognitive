# Macrocolumns

## Overview
A macrocolumn is a vertically organised module of neocortex, typically described as a few hundred micrometres across, containing many [[minicolumns]] and spanning the cortical layers from the pial surface to the white matter. The concept is mainly due to Vernon Mountcastle, who found that in somatosensory cortex of the cat, neurons encountered along a path perpendicular to the surface shared a response property, such as the same skin region and sensory modality, while those found along oblique paths changed abruptly. Related ideas appear under the broader heading of [[cortical_columns]].

## Evidence in sensory cortex
- **Somatosensory cortex.** Mountcastle (1957) described columns defined by modality and receptive field location.
- **Visual cortex.** Hubel and Wiesel found orientation columns and ocular dominance columns in cat and monkey primary visual cortex (see [[hubel_wiesel]] and [[orientation_tuning]]). A set of columns covering all orientations for one location in both eyes was termed a hypercolumn, which is related to but not identical with the macrocolumn.
- **Hierarchy of scales.** Minicolumns, of the order of tens of micrometres wide, are proposed as basic units grouped by shared inputs and horizontal connections into macrocolumns (Mountcastle, 1997).

## Debate
Columnar organisation is clear in some areas and species and much less clear in others, such as rodent visual cortex, which shows orientation selectivity without orientation columns. Horton and Adams (2005) argued that the function of columns has not been established. The macrocolumn is therefore best treated as an anatomical and physiological regularity of some cortical areas, with uncertain functional status as a general computational unit.

## Relation to Active Inference
Predictive coding schemes need a neural implementation, and one proposal maps the populations of the canonical cortical microcircuit across layers onto the units that carry predictions and prediction errors (Bastos and colleagues, 2012; see [[predictive_coding]]). Column-sized units are a natural level at which to hypothesise such a mapping, but the proposal is a theory with partial anatomical support, not an established finding.
