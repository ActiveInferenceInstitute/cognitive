# Lateral Thinking

## Overview
Lateral thinking is a term introduced by Edward de Bono in 1967 for a set of deliberate methods intended to generate new ideas by changing the way a problem is framed. It is contrasted with vertical thinking, which proceeds by sequential, logically justified steps within a given framework. De Bono's central claim is that ordinary perception organises information into fixed patterns, so that solutions outside the established pattern are not reached by correct reasoning alone.

## Techniques
- **Challenging assumptions.** Identifying and suspending the givens of a problem to see whether they are required.
- **Alternatives.** Producing several ways of describing the situation before committing to one.
- **Provocation.** Introducing a deliberately illogical statement, which de Bono marked with the term "po", to dislodge established views and move toward a new one.
- **Random entry.** Using an unrelated word or object as a starting point for associations.
- **Reversal.** Restating the problem with its elements inverted.

## Relation to research on creativity
Lateral thinking overlaps with divergent thinking, the generation of many varied responses (see [[divergent_thinking]]), and with the restructuring that characterises insight (see [[insight_problem_solving]]). Puzzles such as [[duncker_problem]] show how fixation on a familiar use of an object blocks a solution. The empirical support for de Bono's specific programmes is limited, and much of the evidence for training effects comes from applied settings rather than controlled experiments. The label is best treated as a practical heuristic framework rather than an established psychological construct.

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is an analogy. Reframing a problem can be described as moving to a different generative model or hypothesis, or sampling from low-prior regions of policy space rather than following the most probable path. Exploration with an epistemic drive provides a formal route to such behaviour (see [[epistemic_value]]), but no Active Inference model of de Bono's methods has been established.
