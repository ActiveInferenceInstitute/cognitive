# Emotion Circuits

## Overview
Emotion circuits are the networks of subcortical and cortical regions that detect affectively significant stimuli, generate bodily and behavioural responses, and support the experience and regulation of emotion. A central question is whether each emotion has its own dedicated circuit or whether emotions arise from overlapping, general-purpose networks.

## Key structures
- **Amygdala.** Nuclei in the amygdala are critical for fear conditioning and for assigning salience to threat. In rodents, sensory input reaches the lateral amygdala, which projects to the central nucleus that drives defensive behaviour and autonomic responses. LeDoux described a fast subcortical route and a slower cortical route to this structure, a scheme refined in later work.
- **Hypothalamus and periaqueductal gray.** Coordinate autonomic, endocrine and defensive responses.
- **Insula.** Represents interoceptive state and is associated with feeling states such as disgust and pain.
- **Anterior cingulate and medial prefrontal cortex.** Monitor conflict and evaluate affective meaning, and are involved in regulating emotional responses.
- **Orbitofrontal cortex and ventral striatum.** Encode value and reward, linking emotion with decision-making.
- **Hippocampus.** Provides context, so that responses depend on where and when a stimulus occurs.

The classical limbic-system idea, building on Papez's 1937 circuit, has been criticised as imprecise. Basic-emotion views hold that circuits such as fear, rage or seeking are evolutionarily specific; constructionist views (Barrett) hold that emotions are constructed from domain-general networks and context. The evidence includes findings both for and against specificity, and structures such as the amygdala respond to many kinds of significant stimuli, not only threat. Most circuit evidence comes from animal work, so extrapolation to human experience requires caution.

## Role in Active Inference
Active Inference frameworks treat affect as arising from inference about bodily state. Interoceptive predictions are generated in insular and cingulate cortex and compared with visceral signals (Barrett and Simmons, 2015), and valence has been related to the rate of change of free energy (Joffily and Coricelli, 2013). Deep active inference models derive affective states from higher-level beliefs about the success of policies (Hesp et al., 2021). These are research proposals that map computations onto the circuits above, not settled identifications.
