# Time Consciousness

## Overview
Time consciousness, or temporal consciousness, is the study of how experience itself is temporally structured. When a melody is heard, the notes are not experienced as isolated instants, and the sound just past seems still present while the next is anticipated. The central question is how experience can present succession and duration, and why hearing a sequence of notes differs from a sequence of separate experiences, one for each note. The topic belongs mainly to [[phenomenology]] and the philosophy of mind, and it overlaps with the empirical study of [[subjective_time]].

## Main positions
- **Specious present.** William James took the term from E. R. Clay for the short stretch of time that is directly experienced as present, which is "saddle-back" shaped, with a past edge and a future edge, and not a durationless instant (James, 1890).
- **Husserl's analysis.** Husserl described the structure of the living present in three moments: the primal impression of what is now occurring, **retention**, which holds the just-past as still given, and **protention**, which is the openness to what is about to occur. Retention is distinct from recollection, since it is part of the present experience of a temporal object (Husserl, 1991).
- **Retentional and extensional models.** On a retentional view, each moment of experience has only momentary content but represents the recent past through retention. On an extensional view, the experience itself has temporal extent, and its parts overlap and are experienced together. Dainton (2000) defended an extensional account.
- **Alternatives.** Cinematic views treat the stream as a series of snapshots linked by memory, while Bergson contrasted lived duration with spatialised clock time.

Disputes continue over whether the specious present has a fixed duration, and the answers differ across philosophical and psychological accounts.

## Relation to Active Inference
Protention has been compared to prediction: a generative model with temporal depth continuously anticipates the next states, while retention resembles the persistence of recent inferred states in the model's current beliefs. Several authors in the predictive processing literature have developed this mapping, which gives a computational reading of the phenomenological structure. The mapping is interpretive, since phenomenological analysis was not designed to be tested against a free energy formalism, and it remains an active area of philosophical work, not an established result.
