# Feeling of Knowing

## Overview
The feeling of knowing (FOK) is a subjective judgement, made when a sought item cannot be recalled, that the item is stored in memory and would be recognised if it were presented. It is closely related to the tip-of-the-tongue state, in which the person feels a retrieval is imminent. FOK is a standard example of metamemory monitoring: it provides a way for people to decide whether to continue searching, to seek help, or to stop.

## Measurement
Hart (1965) introduced the standard paradigm. Participants answer general-knowledge questions, then rate for those they failed to recall how likely it is that they would recognise the correct answer, and a later recognition test checks the prediction. FOK ratings are typically above chance in predicting recognition, but the correlation is moderate and can be dissociated from actual memory strength. Predictive accuracy is therefore treated as a property of the judgement that must be established in each study.

## Accounts
Two families of explanation are discussed:
- **Cue familiarity.** The judgement is based on the familiarity of the question or cue itself and not on retrieving the target, so it can be high for cues that feel familiar even if the answer is not stored.
- **Accessibility.** Koriat (1993) proposed that FOK relies on the amount of partial information that comes to mind, whatever its correctness, and on how readily it comes, so the feeling is an inferential, heuristic judgement over retrieval products.

Both accounts agree that FOK is not a direct readout of the target's presence. Evidence suggests that both cue familiarity and partial retrieval contribute, with their weights varying across tasks. Nelson and Narens's monitoring-and-control framework places FOK among the judgements that guide retrieval effort (see [[judgment_of_learning]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is an analogy. FOK can be viewed as a second-order belief about the reliability of a first-order memory inference, which fits hierarchical accounts of metacognition as inference about one's own confidence (see [[metacognition]]). The feeling of knowing something before it is retrieved resembles an estimate of the precision of an impending retrieval. This reading is not derived from the standard memory models and has not been tested specifically against FOK data.
