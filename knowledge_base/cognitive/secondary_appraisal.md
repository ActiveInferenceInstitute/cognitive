# Secondary Appraisal

## Overview
In cognitive appraisal theory, emotions arise from how a person evaluates an event relative to their goals and resources. Primary appraisal asks whether the event matters and in what way (irrelevant, benign, or a harm, threat, or challenge). Secondary appraisal then asks what can be done about it. The term comes from the stress-and-coping framework of Lazarus and Folkman; the labels "primary" and "secondary" describe different questions, not a strict time order, and the two interact.

## Components
Appraisal theorists list several evaluations as part of the secondary stage:
- **Coping potential.** Whether the person can influence the outcome or adjust to it, through problem-focused action or emotion-focused regulation.
- **Accountability.** Who or what caused the event (self, other, circumstance), which differentiates guilt, anger, and sadness.
- **Future expectancy.** Whether the situation is likely to improve or worsen.
- **Control and power.** In Scherer's component process model, coping potential is a late stimulus evaluation check, following the relevance and implication checks and preceding the check for compatibility with norms.

The same primary appraisal of threat can yield fear when coping potential is judged low and anger or determination when it is judged high. Reappraisal strategies in [[emotion_regulation]] operate largely by changing these evaluations.

## Role in Active Inference
There is no standard formal definition of secondary appraisal in Active Inference. The closest correspondences are interpretive. Coping potential resembles expected controllability: how strongly the agent's policies are expected to change outcomes, which relates to confidence in policy selection ([[policy_selection]], [[precision_weighting]]). Hesp and colleagues proposed that affective valence tracks the rate of change in this kind of confidence in deep active inference models. This is a hypothesis under development; whether it captures the appraisal dimensions listed above is untested.
