# Emotional Memory

## Overview
Emotional memory is memory for events that evoke emotion. Arousing events are generally retained better and more vividly than neutral ones, a pattern that is partly adaptive since such events tend to matter for survival. Vividness and confidence should not be confused with accuracy.

## Mechanisms and effects
**Amygdala modulation.** A central finding is that the amygdala modulates consolidation in other regions, notably the hippocampus. Arousal triggers release of noradrenaline and glucocorticoids, and animal studies show that manipulating these systems after learning changes later retention (McGaugh, 2004). Human imaging studies show that amygdala activity at encoding predicts later memory for emotional items.

**Attention and encoding.** Emotional stimuli capture attention, and this alone favours their encoding. The effect is also selective: central details of an emotional event are typically remembered better than peripheral ones, a pattern sometimes called attentional narrowing, as seen in weapon-focus findings.

**Flashbulb memories.** Brown and Kulik (1977) described vivid memories of the circumstances in which one learned of a shocking public event. Later work found that these memories distort over time just as ordinary memories do; what distinguishes them is high confidence and vividness, not consistency (Talarico and Rubin, 2003).

**Reconsolidation.** Reactivated memories may enter a labile period during which they can be modified, which is under investigation as a mechanism of memory updating. Its relevance to clinical disorders such as post-traumatic stress is a research hypothesis, not an established route to intervention.

Effects are not uniform. Intense stress at encoding can impair some kinds of memory, and individuals differ.

## Role in Active Inference
The link is indirect. Affectively significant events are surprising or highly salient, and in predictive processing terms they carry more precision, which would increase their weight in learning. Consolidation of such events can then be viewed as preferential updating of the generative model for outcomes that bear on homeostasis and preferences. This reading is an interpretation consistent with the neurobiology, not a derivation, and standard Active Inference models do not yet reproduce the full set of emotional memory effects.
