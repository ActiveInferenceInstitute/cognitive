# Errorless Learning

## Overview
Errorless learning is a method of instruction in which the learner is guided so that incorrect responses are prevented or kept to a minimum while a new association or skill is being acquired. Guidance usually takes the form of prompts, cues or physical help that are withdrawn gradually as performance becomes reliable. It is contrasted with trial-and-error, or errorful, learning, in which mistakes are allowed to occur and are then corrected.

## Origins and methods
The approach was developed in animal research. Terrace (1963) trained pigeons on a colour discrimination by introducing the negative stimulus early, at low intensity and for brief periods, and increasing its salience step by step. The birds made very few responses to the negative stimulus.

Common techniques include:
- **Cue fading**: a complete or partial answer is shown first, and letters or other hints are removed over successive trials.
- **Expanding retrieval intervals**: retrieval is attempted after gradually longer delays so that success is likely at each step (see [[spaced_retrieval]]).

## Use in memory impairment
Baddeley and Wilson (1994) argued that people with severe amnesia rely mainly on implicit memory, which cannot reliably tell a correct response from an error, so that an incorrect guess is itself strengthened by repetition. They reported better word-list learning in amnesic patients under errorless than errorful conditions. Later reviews, such as Clare and Jones (2008), find that errorless methods often help people with memory impairment to learn specific items but that gains are narrow and generalisation to everyday function is less consistent. In people with intact memory, generating an answer and then receiving feedback can improve retention, so errorful practice is not uniformly worse.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is an analogy and not a derivation. In predictive processing, learning is driven by prediction errors, and these are neural quantities; the behavioural errors that errorless methods avoid are a different thing. A loose reading is that prompting keeps the learner within a region where the current generative model predicts well, so that the policy being practised is the one that becomes strengthened (compare habit formation as accumulated policy priors in [[automaticity]]). No established Active Inference model of errorless training exists.
