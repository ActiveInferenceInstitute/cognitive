# Biofeedback

## Overview
Biofeedback is a learning procedure in which a physiological signal, such as muscle tension, heart rate or skin conductance, is measured and presented to the person in real time, usually as a tone, number or graphic. The aim is for the person to learn to influence the signal and, through it, the underlying state. It is typically delivered with instruction, practice and guidance from a trained practitioner, and it is used in clinical, sports and research settings.

## Modalities
- **Electromyography.** Surface recordings of muscle activity, used for muscle tension and rehabilitation.
- **Heart rate variability.** Feedback on beat-to-beat variation, often combined with slow breathing.
- **Thermal and electrodermal.** Peripheral skin temperature and [[skin_conductance]], used to index sympathetic arousal.
- **Respiration.** Pattern and rate of breathing.
- **Neurofeedback.** Feedback of brain activity measured with EEG or fMRI (see [[eeg_recording]] and [[fmri_studies]]).

## Evidence and mechanisms
Early laboratory work by Miller and colleagues reported that animals could learn to alter autonomic responses through reward contingent on those responses (Miller, 1969), and similar procedures were then tried with humans. Later attempts to replicate the animal findings were inconsistent, and effects on visceral functions in people are usually modest and variable. Biofeedback has been applied to tension-type headache, migraine, incontinence and some stress-related conditions with varying levels of supporting evidence, and results for neurofeedback in particular are debated because of problems with controls and placebo effects. Mechanisms are not settled. One account treats it as [[operant_conditioning]] of a response. Another stresses improved awareness of bodily state and a sense of self-efficacy. Practitioners emphasise that feedback is combined with other training such as relaxation and cognitive strategies (Schwartz and Andrasik, 2003).

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is a hypothesis. In interoceptive inference, bodily feelings reflect the brain's predictions about internal states, and regulation is achieved by action that changes those states (see [[interoception]] and [[allostatic_regulation]]). Biofeedback supplies an additional, clear exteroceptive channel that reports an interoceptive variable and so may improve the precision of the person's model of the connection between their strategies and the signal (see [[precision_weighting]]). This interpretation has not been established and the evidence for specific mechanisms is limited.
