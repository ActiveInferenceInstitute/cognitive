# Cortical Columns

## Overview
Cortical columns are vertical groupings of neurons that run across the layers of the neocortex and share some response property. Mountcastle (1957) recorded from somatosensory cortex of the cat and found that neurons encountered along a path perpendicular to the surface responded to the same type of stimulus at the same body location, whereas neurons encountered along oblique paths changed in modality or location. He later proposed the column as the basic modular unit of the neocortex (Mountcastle, 1997).

## Types and evidence
Columnar organisation is best documented in sensory cortex. Hubel and Wiesel (1962) described cells in cat visual cortex with similar orientation preferences stacked in vertical sequences, and ocular dominance columns have been described in several species. In rodent somatosensory cortex, the barrels that correspond to individual whiskers are a clear anatomical example. Smaller minicolumns, containing on the order of a hundred neurons, are described in histology of primate cortex. Terminology varies, and columns of different kinds differ in size and in how sharply they are defined.

## Debate
The column concept is contested. Horton and Adams (2005) pointed out that orientation columns are absent in some species, such as rodents, which nevertheless have working visual cortex, and that the functional significance of columns remains unclear even where they exist. Many tuning properties vary smoothly or in a mixed fashion rather than in discrete blocks. The notion of a uniform canonical circuit repeated across the cortex is a hypothesis, supported by similarities in laminar connectivity, rather than a settled result.

## Relation to Active Inference
Canonical microcircuit proposals map the computations of hierarchical predictive coding onto laminar structure. Bastos and colleagues (2012) suggested that superficial pyramidal cells encode prediction errors and pass them forward, deep pyramidal cells encode expectations and send predictions backward, and inhibitory interneurons and layer 4 cells fill the remaining roles. The scheme is anatomically motivated and has driven laminar recording studies, but supporting data are partial and some predictions are not borne out. It should be read as an active research programme and not as an established account of columnar function.
