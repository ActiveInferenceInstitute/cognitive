# Inferior Parietal Cortex

## Overview
The inferior parietal cortex (inferior parietal lobule, IPL) lies below the intraparietal sulcus and includes the angular gyrus (Brodmann area 39) and the supramarginal gyrus (area 40). It is a heteromodal association region, receiving visual, auditory, somatosensory, and motor-related input, and it is implicated in several functions that are often studied separately. The temporoparietal junction (TPJ) overlaps its posterior portion.

## Mechanism
Four functional themes recur.

1. **Attention reorienting.** Corbetta and Shulman described a ventral attention network, including right TPJ and inferior frontal cortex, that responds to behaviourally relevant, unexpected stimuli and interrupts the dorsal network's goal-directed focus; see [[attention_networks]] and [[exogenous_attention]].
2. **Action and the mirror system.** In macaques, inferior parietal areas contain neurons that respond to both executing and observing goal-directed actions; the human homologue is inferred from imaging and is a contested point, see [[imitation]] and [[motor_control]].
3. **Social cognition.** The right TPJ is among the regions most reliably engaged by tasks requiring inference about others' beliefs ([[theory_of_mind]], [[perspective_taking]]), and whether this is the same mechanism as attention reorienting is debated.
4. **Semantic and numerical processing.** The angular gyrus is active in semantic integration, reading, and mental arithmetic, and is part of the default mode network with [[medial_prefrontal]] cortex.

Damage to the right IPL can produce hemispatial neglect, and left lesions can produce apraxia, which further shows its role in spatial attention and skilled action.

## Role in Active Inference
Vossel, Geng, and Friston interpreted the reorienting response as the update of beliefs after a violation of expectations, with the ventral network carrying prediction errors that revise the attentional priors held by the dorsal network. Under this reading, attention is the optimisation of precision ([[precision_weighting]]) and the TPJ response reflects a high-level prediction error about context, which would also account for its activation when another person's behaviour is unexpected given one's own model. The mapping unites two literatures conceptually, but it is a hypothesis; imaging cannot show that attention reorienting and belief attribution share the same computation, and the region's anatomy is heterogeneous.
