# Motivation

## Overview
Motivation refers to the processes that initiate, direct, and sustain goal-directed behaviour. It answers why an organism acts and how vigorously, as distinct from how it chooses among actions. The cited pages use it for the "drive" that biases decisions, emotion, and learning; this page takes the standard psychological and neuroscientific sense.

## Core accounts
- **Drive reduction (Hull)**: behaviour is energized by internal deficits such as hunger, and reinforced when they are reduced. This anticipates modern homeostatic accounts ([[homeostasis]]).
- **Incentive motivation**: cues associated with rewards acquire the power to elicit wanting. Berridge and Robinson distinguished "wanting" (incentive salience, dopamine-dependent) from "liking" (hedonic impact, mediated by other systems), a dissociation observed in animal studies ([[dopamine_system]], [[reward_processing]]).
- **Expectancy-value**: effort depends on the expected probability of success and the value of the outcome.
- **Intrinsic motivation**: activities pursued for their own sake, linked to curiosity, competence, and autonomy; self-determination theory (Deci and Ryan) distinguishes intrinsic from extrinsic regulation, and also notes that external rewards can sometimes undermine intrinsic interest.
- **Goal-setting**: specific, challenging goals with feedback improve performance in many settings ([[goal_setting]]).

Neurally, motivation involves midbrain dopamine neurons, the ventral striatum, orbitofrontal and medial prefrontal cortex, and the anterior cingulate, which is implicated in effort-cost evaluation ([[orbitofrontal]], [[anterior_cingulate]]). Impaired motivation (apathy, anhedonia) is a feature of several clinical conditions and an active research topic; computational accounts of it are hypotheses.

## Role in Active Inference
Motivation is not a separate module. Goals appear as prior preferences over outcomes, encoded in the C matrix ([[C_matrix]]), and the agent acts to realize preferred outcomes. Homeostatic drives are set-points in interoceptive priors ([[allostatic_control]]). Intrinsic motivation corresponds to the epistemic, information-seeking term of [[expected_free_energy]] (curiosity), which operates even when preferences are flat. Vigor and effort are tied to the precision over policies, which Friston and colleagues associated with dopamine, giving a reading of the incentive-salience findings. These identifications are theoretical proposals; they unify descriptions but remain contested against alternative reinforcement-learning accounts.
