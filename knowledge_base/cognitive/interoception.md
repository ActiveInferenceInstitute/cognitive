# Interoception

## Overview
Interoception is the sensing, processing and integration of signals from inside the body: heartbeat, respiration, gut distension, blood pressure, temperature, pain and visceral state. These signals inform the brain about physiological condition and are used to regulate it, and they are linked to emotion and the sense of self.

## Mechanism
Signals arrive through vagal and spinal visceral afferents and humoral routes. They are relayed through the brainstem (nucleus of the solitary tract) and thalamus to the insula and anterior cingulate cortex. Craig described a posterior-to-anterior progression in the insula from a primary representation of bodily state to an integrated, subjective representation in the anterior insula.

Researchers distinguish dimensions of interoception (Garfinkel and colleagues): objective accuracy, for instance in a heartbeat detection task (Schandry); sensibility, a person's self-reported confidence; and metacognitive awareness, the match between the two. Measures are imperfect, and heartbeat-counting performance is confounded by prior beliefs about heart rate.

Interoceptive signals feed regulatory loops. Homeostatic reflexes act on a fast timescale, while allostasis, regulation through anticipatory change (Sterling and Eyer), requires predicting needs. Interoceptive processing is associated with emotional intensity, decision making and disorders including anxiety, depression and eating disorders, though effect sizes and causal direction remain contested.

## Role in Active Inference
Interoception is where active inference and physiological regulation meet most directly. Seth proposed interoceptive inference: emotional states are the brain's inferences about the causes of interoceptive signals, generated by hierarchical predictions of bodily state. Barrett and Simmons described interoceptive predictions in cortical agranular regions. Pezzulo, Rigoli and Friston cast homeostatic and allostatic control as active inference, in which autonomic reflexes fulfil interoceptive predictions, analogous to proprioceptive action ([[proprioception]]). Preferred physiological ranges serve as priors, and deviation from them is a source of prediction error, which links the free-energy principle to the maintenance of bodily integrity.

These are influential but largely theoretical proposals. Direct tests of interoceptive prediction error in human brains are limited, and predictions about, for example, the role of precision in psychiatric conditions are still being evaluated.
