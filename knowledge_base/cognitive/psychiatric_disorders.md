# Psychiatric Disorders

## Overview
Psychiatric disorders are conditions defined by clinically significant disturbances in cognition, emotion regulation, or behaviour, diagnosed from reported symptoms and observed behaviour. This page is a conceptual orientation for how disorders are classified and how computational frameworks relate to them. It is not clinical advice and makes no claims about treatment.

## Mechanism
**Classification.** Current diagnostic manuals (the American Psychiatric Association's DSM-5 and the WHO's ICD-11) define categories from symptom criteria. Because no validated biological marker defines most categories, comorbidity is high and heterogeneity within a diagnosis is large. Two dimensional alternatives address this. The NIMH Research Domain Criteria (RDoC) organise research around functional domains (for example negative valence, cognitive systems, social processes) cut across diagnoses. The Hierarchical Taxonomy of Psychopathology (HiTOP) arranges symptoms along empirically derived dimensional spectra.

**Levels of explanation.** Candidate explanations span genetics, neurodevelopment, neurochemistry, circuits, cognition, and environment. Mapping across these levels is the central unsolved problem, and the reason mechanistic cognitive models are of interest.

**Transdiagnostic processes.** Researchers study processes that appear in several diagnoses, such as cognitive control ([[cognitive_control]], [[inhibitory_control]]), threat and reward processing, and [[emotion_regulation]], instead of assuming one mechanism per category.

## Role in Active Inference
Active Inference-based accounts treat symptoms as consequences of atypical inference: for example altered precision on priors or sensory evidence, or atypical beliefs about one's own control and the volatility of the world. This offers a common vocabulary across conditions (see [[schizophrenia]], [[autism]], [[anxiety]]) and fits transdiagnostic frameworks better than one-mechanism-per-diagnosis views. The accounts are hypotheses under active research; empirical findings are mixed, parameters are difficult to identify, and competing models exist. They are not established causal explanations of any condition. For worked treatments see [[psychiatry]] and [[computational_psychiatry]].
