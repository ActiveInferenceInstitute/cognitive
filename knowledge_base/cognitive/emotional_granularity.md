# Emotional Granularity

## Overview
Emotional granularity, also called emotion differentiation, is the specificity with which a person represents and labels their emotional experience. A person with high granularity uses distinct terms such as "irritated", "disappointed" or "anxious" for states that someone with low granularity would lump together as "bad". Lisa Feldman Barrett introduced the term in the early 2000s, distinguishing it from the intensity of emotion.

## Measurement
Granularity is estimated from repeated ratings of experienced emotions, often collected in daily-life studies. If a person's ratings of different negative emotion words (angry, sad, afraid) are highly correlated across occasions, they treat the words as interchangeable and have low granularity. If the ratings differ across occasions, granularity is high. Intraclass correlation and related indices are commonly computed separately for positive and negative emotions. The estimates depend on the number of occasions and items and on how the scale is used, which is a recognised methodological challenge.

## Correlates
Higher negative emotion differentiation has been associated with less intense reactions to stress, more varied regulation strategies, and lower likelihood of drinking heavily or of aggression after provocation in some studies (Kashdan, Barrett and McKnight, 2015). Low granularity has been linked to psychological difficulties such as depression and borderline personality features, and it is related to alexithymia, though not identical to it. Effect sizes are modest in many cases, causal direction is uncertain, and results vary by sample and measure.

## Relation to Active Inference
Barrett's theory of constructed emotion proposes that the brain uses emotion concepts to make predictions that organise interoceptive and sensory signals into a specific experience. On that view, a richer repertoire of concepts gives more precise predictions and finer discrimination of bodily states, while a coarse repertoire produces more generic affect. This is a theoretical proposal drawn from predictive processing, and it is not an established mechanism for the correlations above.
