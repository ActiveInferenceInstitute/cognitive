# Broca's Area

## Overview
Broca's area is a region of the posterior left inferior frontal gyrus, usually identified with Brodmann areas 44 and 45 (pars opercularis and pars triangularis). It is named after Paul Broca, who in 1861 described a patient with severe loss of speech output and a left frontal lesion. Broca's area became the first brain region to be associated with a specific cognitive function, and it remains central in language neuroscience.

## Functions and clinical features
- **Broca's aphasia.** Patients with damage in this territory produce slow, effortful, non-fluent speech with reduced grammatical structure, while comprehension of simple utterances is relatively preserved. The syndrome typically requires lesions larger than Broca's area alone, extending into surrounding frontal cortex, insula and white matter, and small lesions limited to the area often cause milder and transient deficits.
- **Syntax and unification.** Imaging studies show activity in the area when sentences involve complex syntactic structure. Friederici (2011) links the posterior part to hierarchical syntactic processing. Hagoort (2005) proposed that the region supports unification, the combination of lexical elements into larger structures across phonological, syntactic and semantic levels.
- **Speech planning and control.** The area contributes to articulatory sequencing and to selecting among competing alternatives, and it has been linked to verbal working memory and cognitive control more generally.
- **Connectivity.** It connects with posterior temporal regions via dorsal and ventral pathways, which is why modern accounts treat it as a node in a network rather than as an isolated speech centre.

## Relation to Active Inference
Active Inference formulations of communication treat speech production as the fulfilment of predictions, in which a generative model of the speaker's intended message generates proprioceptive and auditory predictions that drive articulation (Friston & Frith, 2015). Under this view, frontal regions involved in production would sit at levels of a hierarchy that specify sequences, but mapping model components onto Broca's area specifically is not established. It is a modelling approach, supported by simulations, that complements empirical accounts of the area rather than replacing them.
