# Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex

## Overview
The dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) is the lateral portion of the frontal lobe, roughly Brodmann areas 9 and 46 (boundaries vary by author), in the middle and superior frontal gyri. It is a main node of the frontoparietal control network and is central to maintaining and manipulating information in service of goals.

## Mechanism
The best-established function is [[working_memory]]. In monkeys, neurons in the principal sulcus and nearby cortex show persistent firing across delay periods that holds task-relevant information, and lesions disrupt delayed-response performance. In humans, DLPFC activity increases with working-memory load and with manipulation of contents, and is engaged by rule maintenance, planning, and [[cognitive_control]] more generally. Miller and Cohen's integrative theory proposes that prefrontal cortex actively maintains representations of goals and rules, which bias processing in other regions toward task-relevant pathways. Its engagement in [[inhibitory_control]], [[cognitive_flexibility]], and selection among competing options fits this role.

Organisation along the lateral axis is hierarchical in some accounts: posterior regions handle concrete stimulus-response mappings and anterior regions abstract rules or temporal context (Koechlin and colleagues described a cascade of control signals). DLPFC interacts with the [[anterior_cingulate]], which signals control demand, and with parietal cortex ([[inferior_parietal]]) in attention and working memory. The specificity of function is limited: the same area responds in many demanding tasks, and deficits after damage are diverse.

## Role in Active Inference
In hierarchical [[generative_model]] terms, the lateral prefrontal cortex is a plausible site for high-level, slowly changing beliefs about context, goals, and rules that provide empirical priors to lower levels and shape policy selection. Delay-period activity would correspond to sustained beliefs about hidden states, and top-down control to the precision-weighted influence of those beliefs ([[precision_weighting]], [[hierarchical_inference]]). Formal Active Inference models of working memory and delay activity exist, and the mapping onto prefrontal anatomy is consistent with the hierarchical-control literature. It is nonetheless a theoretical interpretation: neural evidence cannot yet distinguish it from alternative accounts such as gating models or reinforcement-learning-based working-memory models.
