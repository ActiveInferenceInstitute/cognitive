# Social Development

## Overview
Social development is the process by which children and adults acquire the understanding, skills and relationships that allow them to take part in a social world. It covers early bonds with caregivers, peer friendships, cooperation and conflict, and the understanding of others' minds. It overlaps with [[socioemotional_development]], which places more weight on emotional aspects.

## Major accounts
- **Attachment theory.** Bowlby proposed that the infant's tie to a caregiver is a basis for later relationships, mediated by internal working models (see [[secure_base]]).
- **Sociocultural theory.** Vygotsky (1978) held that higher mental functions originate in social interaction and are internalised, and that learning is advanced by guided participation within a zone of proximal development.
- **Ecological systems theory.** Bronfenbrenner (1979) described development as shaped by nested systems, from family and school to culture.
- **Social learning and cultural learning.** Children learn by observation and imitation. Tomasello (1999) argued that human cognition depends on cultural learning, which rests on understanding others as intentional agents.

## Milestones
Typical milestones include social smiling and gaze following in the first year, joint attention and pointing at around the end of the first year, pretend play and the first signs of empathy in the second year, and success on false-belief tasks at around four to five years, with variation between children and cultures. Peer relationships become more important in middle childhood and adolescence, as friendship, status and group membership take on a larger role. Age ranges for milestones are approximate and depend on method and culture.

## Relation to Active Inference
Active Inference treats social development as the learning of generative models that include other agents. Children are described as acquiring shared expectations through interaction, and cultural practices are described as shaping the niche to which those models are fitted, as in work on niche construction (Constant et al., 2018, see [[constant_2018]]). These are proposals within a growing theoretical literature, and while they suggest testable hypotheses, they have not displaced established developmental accounts.
