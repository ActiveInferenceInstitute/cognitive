# Central Executive

## Overview
The central executive is the attentional control component of the multicomponent model of working memory proposed by Baddeley and Hitch (1974). It is assumed to have no storage of its own and to direct attention, coordinate the modality-specific stores (the [[phonological_loop]] and a visuospatial sketchpad), and link them with long-term memory. Baddeley (2000) later added an episodic buffer as a separate multimodal store.

## Proposed functions
Baddeley (1996) argued that the executive should be decomposed into specifiable functions: focusing attention, switching between tasks, dividing attention between concurrent tasks, and interfacing with long-term memory. This resembles the supervisory attentional system of Norman and Shallice (1986), which intervenes when routine action schemas are inadequate, as in novel, dangerous or error-prone situations. Miyake and colleagues (2000) used latent-variable analysis to identify three separable but correlated executive functions: shifting, updating of working-memory contents and inhibition (see [[task_switching]], [[inhibitory_control]]).

## Criticisms
A single executive risks being a homunculus, a label for whatever is not otherwise explained. Contemporary work therefore tends to specify executive processes and their neural substrates, most associated with lateral prefrontal and anterior cingulate cortex (see [[dorsolateral_prefrontal]], [[anterior_cingulate]]), and to consider that executive control emerges from distributed network interactions. Empirical findings show executive abilities fractionate in patients and vary across individuals.

## Role in Active Inference
Active Inference has no homunculus. Executive control is reinterpreted as inference at higher levels of a hierarchical model, where beliefs about task context and goals set the precision of lower-level predictions and the prior over policies (see [[precision_weighting]], [[hierarchical_models]]). Selecting a task set, shifting, and inhibiting a prepotent response become policy selection under particular preferences and precisions (see [[policy_selection]]). Working-memory maintenance corresponds to sustaining beliefs about past states across time. This is a reformulation that remains to be tested against the specific dissociations the executive literature has reported, and detailed Active Inference models of the Miyake functions are limited.
