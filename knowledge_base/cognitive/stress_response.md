# Stress Response

## Overview
The stress response is the set of physiological and behavioural changes that mobilise an organism to meet a demand or threat. Cannon described the rapid sympathetic "fight or flight" reaction, and Selye (1936) described a more general syndrome of adaptation to harmful agents. Two systems are central. The sympathetic-adrenal-medullary system acts within seconds, releasing adrenaline and noradrenaline, and raising heart rate and blood pressure. The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis acts over minutes to hours, with corticotropin-releasing hormone prompting pituitary release of ACTH, which stimulates cortisol secretion from the adrenal cortex (see [[cortisol]]).

## Regulation and consequences
- **Appraisal.** Whether a situation triggers a stress response depends on appraised threat, novelty, unpredictability and perceived control, and the amygdala and prefrontal cortex take part in this evaluation.
- **Negative feedback.** Cortisol acts on the hippocampus, hypothalamus and pituitary to dampen the HPA axis, which limits the duration of the response in healthy individuals.
- **Allostasis.** McEwen (2007) emphasised that the response is adaptive in the short term, but that chronic or poorly terminated activation produces cumulative wear, termed allostatic load, with effects on metabolism, immune function, and brain regions including the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex.
- **Behaviour and cognition.** Acute stress can alter attention, memory and decision strategies, for example shifting choice toward habitual or reward-driven responding, with effects depending on timing and intensity (see [[decision_making]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
Active Inference treats physiological regulation as the minimisation of surprise about interoceptive states, which makes allostasis a form of prediction: the brain acts to bring bodily states toward expected values before deviations occur. Peters, McEwen and Friston (2017) proposed that stress reflects sustained uncertainty about whether the organism can maintain these states, and that chronic uncertainty drives the pathologies of allostatic load. This is a theoretical interpretation that organises existing findings; it has not been shown to outperform standard physiological accounts in predicting outcomes. See [[allostatic_regulation]] and [[homeostasis]].
