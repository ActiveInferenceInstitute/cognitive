# Attention Economy

## Overview
The attention economy is a way of describing markets and institutions in which the limiting factor is the time and focus of human audiences rather than the supply of information. Simon (1971) stated the premise: a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention, and a need to allocate that attention efficiently among the overabundance of information sources that might consume it. The term was later popularised in business writing and in analyses of the internet (Goldhaber, 1997; Davenport & Beck, 2001).

## Mechanisms
- **Monetisation.** Advertising-funded media sell audience attention to advertisers, so revenue tracks time spent and engagement.
- **Engagement optimisation.** Platforms use ranking and recommendation systems, notifications and interface design that are tuned to behavioural metrics such as clicks and watch time.
- **Competition across scales.** Producers compete for limited human capacity, which is bounded by well-studied limits of selective and divided attention (see [[capacity_limits]]).

Wu (2016) traces the history of this business model from the nineteenth-century penny press to broadcasting and digital platforms.

## Concerns and open questions
Commentators have linked the model to fragmented attention, sensational content, and polarised information environments. The empirical strength of these links varies by claim and remains contested, and some attention research distinguishes effects of media multitasking from broader claims about declining attention spans. Design responses include attention-respecting interface norms, regulation of recommender systems, and individual practices such as attention training.

## Relation to Active Inference
In predictive processing, attention is described as the optimisation of precision, the weighting of prediction errors by their expected reliability (see [[precision_weighting]]). Describing platforms as competing to capture precision is an analogy, not a theory from the framework. The link to the vault is mainly practical: an environment engineered to attract attention shapes which sensory evidence a person samples, and therefore which beliefs they form, which is the concern of [[cognitive_security]].
