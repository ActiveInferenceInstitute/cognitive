# Preattentive Processing

## Overview
Preattentive processing is the analysis of the visual scene that takes place rapidly and in parallel across the visual field before limited-capacity focused attention is directed to a particular item. The term is used mainly in research on visual search, where some targets are found in a time that hardly depends on the number of distractors. In the broader sense used in affective science, it refers to fast detection of significant stimuli, such as threats, before they are consciously attended.

## Feature search and pop-out
Treisman and Gelade (1980) introduced the feature-integration theory. In this account, basic features such as colour, orientation, size and motion are registered in separate maps automatically and in parallel, whereas combining them into a bound object requires focused attention. A target defined by a single feature, such as a red item among green ones, appears to pop out, and the search time stays almost flat as the number of distractors rises. A target defined by a conjunction of features requires a search whose time increases roughly linearly with display size.

## Limits of the concept
Later work indicated that the dichotomy is not sharp. Search slopes lie on a continuum, and attributes that seem preattentive can depend on the task, the similarity of distractors to the target and the observer's state. Joseph, Chun and Nakayama (1997) showed that detection of a supposedly preattentive feature target was impaired when attention was occupied by a concurrent task. Wolfe and Horowitz (2004) reviewed which attributes guide attention, and the term "preattentive" is often replaced by terms such as "guiding features". See [[visual_search]] and [[salience]].

## Emotional stimuli
Studies of emotion report that angry faces and fear-relevant objects such as snakes are found faster than neutral ones in some search tasks (Öhman, Flykt and Esteves, 2001). One interpretation holds that a fast route through the amygdala helps to detect threat automatically ([[amygdala]], [[emotional_processing]]). The degree to which this detection is independent of attention is disputed.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is interpretive. In predictive-processing accounts, a feature that departs strongly from the statistics of its surroundings produces a large prediction error that captures processing automatically, and saliency can be treated as precision-weighted surprise. This gives a description of pop-out in terms of the framework, but the account does not resolve the empirical dispute over the term.
