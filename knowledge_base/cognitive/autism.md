# Autism

## Overview
Autism (autism spectrum condition) is a neurodevelopmental condition defined by differences in social communication and interaction together with restricted or repetitive behaviours and interests, often with atypical sensory processing. It is highly heterogeneous, and many autistic people and researchers prefer a neurodiversity framing over a purely deficit-based one. This page covers computational hypotheses; it is not clinical advice and makes no treatment claims.

## Mechanism
Behavioural and cognitive research has examined several domains: [[theory_of_mind]] and other aspects of [[social_cognition]] (with the caveat that difficulties in mutual understanding may be bidirectional between autistic and non-autistic people), [[joint_attention]] early in development, executive functions, a tendency toward local over global processing, and sensory hypersensitivity or hyposensitivity. No single cognitive theory accounts for all features, and heterogeneity is large.

## Role in Active Inference
Predictive-processing accounts offer a unifying hypothesis for perception and behaviour. Pellicano and Burr proposed that autistic perception relies on priors that are relatively flat or less influential, so the world is experienced as more "real" and less smoothed by expectation. Lawson and colleagues framed this as aberrant precision, with sensory prediction errors given too much weight relative to priors, which would affect sensory sensitivity and slow adaptation to context. Van de Cruys and colleagues proposed that prediction errors are given inflexibly high precision and so are not attenuated when they should be. Sinha and colleagues described autism as a disorder of prediction over time. A related line links the hormone [[oxytocin]] and interoception to precision in social contexts.

These proposals disagree about the mechanism (weak priors, high sensory precision, inflexible precision, or altered estimation of volatility), and empirical support has been mixed across modalities and tasks. They are hypotheses under active research, not established causal explanations, and they do not capture the full range of autistic experience.
