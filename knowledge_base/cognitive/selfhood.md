# Selfhood

## Overview
Selfhood is the condition of being a self: a subject of experience and agent who is continuous over time and distinct from the world. The term covers several things that are often separated. Gallagher (2000) distinguished a minimal self, the immediate sense of being the subject of experience and the owner and agent of one's actions, from a narrative self, which extends across time and is built from autobiographical stories. Questions about what makes a person the same over time belong to [[personal_identity]].

## Philosophical and scientific views
- **Substance and soul views** treat the self as a persisting entity distinct from the body.
- **Bundle and reductionist views**, associated with Hume and later Parfit, hold that there is no separate self beyond a series of experiences and their connections.
- **Phenomenological views** locate the self in the first-person structure of experience, including bodily self-awareness.
- **Narrative views** hold that selves are constituted by the stories people tell about their lives.
- **Self-model views**, notably Metzinger (2003), propose that the self is a representation produced by the brain and not experienced as a representation.

Empirical research addresses components of selfhood, including body ownership in the rubber-hand illusion, sense of agency, and self-related processing in midline cortical regions. Disorders such as depersonalisation and some psychiatric and neurological conditions show that these components can come apart.

## Relation to Active Inference
Seth and others propose that the experience of being a self arises from the brain's predictive modelling of the body and its internal states, an account sometimes described as interoceptive inference (Seth, 2013; Seth, 2021). In the free energy principle more broadly, a self-organising system that maintains its boundary can be described as modelling itself. These are theoretical proposals under active development, with supporting evidence from studies of bodily illusions and interoception, but they are not settled and compete with other views.
