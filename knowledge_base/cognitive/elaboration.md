# Elaboration

## Overview
In memory research, elaboration means processing new material in terms of its meaning and its relations to what is already known, rather than as isolated surface features. Elaborated items are remembered better than items repeated without such processing. The term here refers to elaborative encoding, not to elaboration in a rhetorical or physical sense.

## Mechanism
The levels-of-processing framework (Craik and Lockhart, 1972) proposed that retention depends on the depth of processing at study: attending to meaning produces a more durable trace than attending to appearance or sound. Craik and Tulving (1975) found that words judged in a semantically rich context were later recalled better than words judged on shallow properties, and that richer, more elaborate sentence frames gave better memory than simple ones.

Several mechanisms are proposed to contribute:
- **More retrieval cues.** Each added association is a potential route to the trace.
- **Distinctiveness.** Elaboration makes an item discriminable from similar items.
- **Integration with schemas.** Linking new content to organised knowledge makes it easier to reconstruct.

Practical forms include elaborative interrogation (asking why a fact is true), self-explanation, the self-reference effect, generating examples, and mnemonics that build vivid associations. Elaborative rehearsal is contrasted with maintenance rehearsal, which keeps an item active but yields little long-term benefit. Elaboration is not guaranteed to help: effort spent on irrelevant associations, or on elaborations that fail to match later retrieval demands, can give little benefit, in line with encoding specificity.

## Role in Active Inference
The connection is interpretive rather than a derivation. Learning in a generative model is the updating of its parameters and structure, and new observations that can be explained by existing latent structure are absorbed at low cost in model complexity. Elaboration can be read as constructing such an explanation, making the new item a predictable consequence of established beliefs and so reconstructible from them. Memory consolidation and schema effects are discussed in the same terms. Active Inference does not itself predict the levels-of-processing effect, which was established empirically and is stated in cognitive psychology terms.
