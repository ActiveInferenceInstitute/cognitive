# Cingulate Cortex

## Overview
The cingulate cortex is a band of medial cerebral cortex that curves around the corpus callosum. It is part of the limbic lobe and is commonly divided into anterior, midcingulate, posterior and retrosplenial regions, which differ in cytoarchitecture, connectivity and function. The anterior and midcingulate regions connect with prefrontal, motor and subcortical structures, while the posterior regions connect with parietal and medial temporal cortex.

## Functions
Neuroimaging, lesion and recording studies link the dorsal anterior and midcingulate cortex to detecting response conflict, signalling errors, evaluating effort and reward, and processing pain and other aversive events. Bush, Luu and Posner (2000) distinguished a dorsal cognitive division from a rostral-ventral affective division. Botvinick and colleagues (2001) proposed that anterior cingulate activity signals conflict between competing responses and recruits additional control. Shenhav, Botvinick and Cohen (2013) later argued that the region computes the expected value of exerting control, weighing the benefits of control against its cost. The posterior cingulate and the adjacent precuneus form a core part of the default mode network, which is active during internally directed thought.

## Disorders
Altered cingulate activity or structure has been reported in depression, obsessive-compulsive disorder, schizophrenia and chronic pain. Such findings are correlational and region-wide labels like "anterior cingulate" cover heterogeneous tissue.

## Relation to Active Inference
Several predictive accounts of cingulate function exist. The predicted response-outcome model treats medial prefrontal and cingulate cortex as computing predictions of action outcomes and signalling their violation (Alexander and Brown, 2011), which maps naturally onto prediction error. In Active Inference language, errors and conflict are surprising outcomes under the policy being followed, and the cingulate could signal the need to revise beliefs about policies or increase precision. These are hypotheses at the level of an active research programme; they have not displaced reinforcement-learning and control-theoretic accounts, and the region's functions are still contested.
