# Beta Oscillations

## Overview
Beta oscillations are rhythmic fluctuations of neural population activity in the frequency range of about 13 to 30 Hz, recorded as local field potentials, in EEG and in MEG. They are most prominent over sensorimotor cortex and are also found in the basal ganglia, parietal and frontal cortex. The band boundaries are conventional, and subdivisions into lower and upper beta are common.

## Motor behaviour
Beta power over motor cortex is high during rest and steady muscle contraction, falls before and during movement (event-related desynchronisation), and rebounds above baseline shortly after a movement ends. Engel and Fries (2010) summarised this pattern and proposed that beta activity signals maintenance of the current sensorimotor or cognitive state, the "status quo", so that a change in plan is accompanied by a drop in beta. Related changes appear during motor imagery and action observation. Beta activity is not limited to motor tasks; it is also modulated in working memory, decision-making and top-down attention.

## Clinical relevance
In Parkinson's disease, recordings from the subthalamic nucleus and pallidum show enhanced synchronisation in the beta band. It is suppressed by dopaminergic medication and by deep brain stimulation, and the degree of suppression has been related to improvement of bradykinesia and rigidity (Jenkinson and Brown, 2011). Whether excess beta is a cause of the symptoms or a correlate of the same underlying dopamine loss remains debated.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is a proposed mapping rather than a derivation. Hierarchical predictive coding assigns predictions to deep cortical layers and prediction errors to superficial layers. Studies of visual cortex reported that feedforward influences are carried mainly by gamma-band activity and feedback influences by alpha and beta bands (Bastos et al., 2015), which fits the idea that beta carries predictions downward. The status-quo reading is similar in spirit: sustained beta would express confidence in the current model, and a reduction would permit updating. Evidence is mixed across tasks, and alternative interpretations of the same data exist.
