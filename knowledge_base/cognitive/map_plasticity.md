# Map Plasticity

## Overview
Sensory and motor cortices contain orderly maps, in which neighbouring body surfaces, retinal locations or sound frequencies are represented by neighbouring cortical sites. Map plasticity is the capacity of these maps to change their boundaries and internal organisation with use, injury or training. It was established largely by Michael Merzenich and colleagues, who showed that adult cortical maps are not fixed after development, a conclusion that revised older views confining plasticity to early life (see [[critical_periods]]).

## Key findings
- **Amputation and denervation.** After amputation of a digit in adult monkeys, cortical territory previously driven by that digit came to respond to neighbouring digits and adjacent skin (Merzenich and colleagues, 1984).
- **Practice.** Behavioural training on a tactile or auditory discrimination enlarges the cortical representation of the trained skin region or frequency range in animals.
- **Musicians.** String players have larger cortical representations of the fingers of the left hand, which are used in fine fingering, than non-players, and the effect is larger in those who began early (Elbert and colleagues, 1995).
- **Phantom limb.** Reorganisation after arm amputation was correlated with phantom limb pain in one influential study (Flor and colleagues, 1995), although the causal relation and the extent of reorganisation in humans remain debated.

## Mechanisms
Proposed mechanisms include unmasking of weak or silent connections, changes in inhibition, activity-dependent strengthening and weakening of synapses (see [[synaptic_plasticity]] and [[hebbian_learning]]), and slower structural change such as axonal sprouting (see [[structural_plasticity]]). Neuromodulators such as acetylcholine gate plasticity when stimuli are behaviourally relevant. Competition for cortical territory among inputs is a recurrent theme (Buonomano and Merzenich, 1998).

## Relation to Active Inference
Within predictive processing, map plasticity can be read as learning of the parameters of a generative model so that representational resources follow the statistics and precision of inputs. This reading is plausible and compatible with the empirical record, but it is an interpretation and does not yet yield quantitative predictions about the shape of reorganisation.
