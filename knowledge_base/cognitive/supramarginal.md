# Supramarginal Gyrus

## Overview
The supramarginal gyrus is a gyrus of the inferior parietal lobule that arches over the posterior end of the lateral (Sylvian) fissure. It corresponds approximately to Brodmann area 40 and lies just in front of the angular gyrus and above the posterior superior temporal cortex. In the left hemisphere it belongs to the perisylvian language network. The label covers an anatomically defined gyrus, so functional claims often refer to subregions or to the wider inferior parietal cortex.

## Functions
- **Phonological short-term memory.** Positron emission tomography work found that left inferior parietal activation (around area 40) accompanies tasks requiring storage of verbal material, while inferior frontal activation accompanies rehearsal (Paulesu, Frith and Frackowiak, 1993). This pairing is the usual neural counterpart of the store and articulatory process of the [[phonological_loop]].
- **Sensorimotor integration for speech.** Hickok and Poeppel (2007) described an area at the parietal-temporal boundary of the Sylvian fissure that links auditory representations with articulatory plans. It lies close to the supramarginal gyrus, and the two are often discussed together.
- **Phonological decisions.** Imaging studies of rhyme judgement and phoneme manipulation commonly report left supramarginal activation, and stimulation or lesion studies suggest that disturbing it increases phonological errors.
- **Praxis and tool use.** Left inferior parietal damage is associated with limb apraxia, an impairment of skilled movement not explained by weakness.
- **Social cognition.** Some imaging work implicates the right supramarginal gyrus in distinguishing one's own state from another's, though this finding is less firmly established.

Damage in this region, along with the underlying arcuate fasciculus fibres, is associated with conduction aphasia, characterised by relatively preserved comprehension and fluent speech but poor repetition and phonemic paraphasias. This syndrome is distinct from the comprehension deficit of [[wernicke_aphasia]].

## Relation to Active Inference
Hickok (2012) proposed a state feedback control model of speech production in which motor commands generate forward predictions of auditory and somatosensory consequences, with a sensorimotor interface in the Sylvian parietal-temporal region. Such a model is close in spirit to Active Inference, where speech is action that fulfils sensory predictions. The correspondence is an analogy between control architectures, and the supramarginal gyrus is only one of several candidate sites.
