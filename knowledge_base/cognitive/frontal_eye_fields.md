# Frontal Eye Fields

## Overview
The frontal eye fields (FEF) are a region of prefrontal and premotor cortex, in humans near the junction of the precentral sulcus and the superior frontal sulcus, within Brodmann area 8. Electrical stimulation at low current evokes contralateral saccades, and neurons in the area respond before voluntary saccades and to visual targets. The FEF project to the superior colliculus and to brainstem saccade generators, and they are interconnected with parietal cortex, so they sit at the interface between visual selection and motor output (see [[eye_movements]]).

## Roles
- **Voluntary saccades.** The FEF contribute to selecting where to look next, particularly for saccades made on the basis of goals or instructions rather than reflexively to a sudden stimulus. Damage tends to make saccades slower to initiate and impairs scanning.
- **Covert attention.** Sub-threshold stimulation of FEF in monkeys enhances responses and behavioural sensitivity for stimuli at the corresponding location in the visual field, without producing an eye movement (Moore & Armstrong, 2003). This is evidence that the signals which prepare gaze shifts also bias visual cortex toward particular locations, consistent with premotor theories of attention.
- **Smooth pursuit.** A neighbouring part of the area contributes to tracking moving targets.

In humans, imaging studies place the FEF with the intraparietal sulcus in the dorsal attention network, which maintains top-down, goal-directed spatial priorities (Corbetta & Shulman, 2002; see [[attention_networks]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
Active Inference treats saccades as actions chosen to reduce uncertainty about the causes of sensory input. Friston and colleagues (2012) modelled scene exploration in this way, with saccades selected to maximise expected information gain, which has the effect of foraging for informative locations (see [[epistemic_foraging]]). In such models, attention is the optimisation of precision, and a salience map that biases where to look next plays the role usually attributed to the FEF and related oculomotor structures. The correspondence is a hypothesis about functional architecture, supported by simulations that reproduce aspects of visual search and by the physiology above, but not a demonstrated mapping of model variables onto this specific area.
