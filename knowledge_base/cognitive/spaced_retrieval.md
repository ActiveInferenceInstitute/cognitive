# Spaced Retrieval

## Overview
Spaced retrieval is a learning method in which a person tries to recall an item repeatedly, with the interval between attempts progressively lengthened. If recall succeeds the interval grows, and if it fails the item is restudied and the interval is shortened. It combines two robust findings of memory research: practice distributed over time is retained longer than massed practice (the spacing effect, see [[spacing]]), and retrieving information strengthens memory more than rereading it (the testing effect).

## Origins and evidence
Landauer and Bjork (1978) compared schedules for learning names and found that expanding retrieval intervals gave good retention. Later studies suggested that the main benefit comes from spacing the retrievals and that expanding schedules offer no consistent advantage over equal spacing, although expanding schedules can make early practice more successful. Roediger and Karpicke (2006) showed that taking tests produced better delayed retention than repeated study, even though repeated study felt more effective to learners. A spacing interval that is too long can lower the chance of successful retrieval, and the best gap depends on how long the material must be retained, with longer retention targets favouring longer gaps (Cepeda et al., 2008).

## Applications
- **Education.** Flash-card and quiz routines in medicine and language learning use it, and software schedules reviews based on past performance (see [[spaced_repetition]]).
- **Memory rehabilitation.** Spaced retrieval is used with people who have memory impairment from dementia or brain injury to teach specific information, such as a name or a safety strategy. Success is generally strongest for narrow, practised targets, and generalisation to new settings is limited.

## Possible mechanisms
Explanations include greater encoding variability across spaced attempts, effortful retrieval strengthening cues and traces, and consolidation between sessions. These are not mutually exclusive.

## Relation to Active Inference
The technique has no direct treatment in Active Inference. Interpreted in its terms, retrieval attempts act as inference that updates and reweights a learned association, with difficulty at the moment of recall indicating where the model's predictions are uncertain. This is an analogy and not a tested model.
