# Integrated Information Theory

## Overview
Integrated information theory (IIT), developed by Giulio Tononi and collaborators, starts from the properties of experience and asks what physical systems could have them. It holds that consciousness is identical to the intrinsic cause-effect structure of a physical system, and that the degree of consciousness is quantified by integrated information, denoted Phi ($\Phi$). Unlike [[global_workspace]] theory, IIT is not a theory of access or function but of the substrate of experience.

## Mechanism
IIT begins with axioms claimed to be self-evident about experience: intrinsic existence, composition, information, integration, and exclusion. It derives postulates about physical systems from them. A system must have cause-effect power on itself; its mechanisms must specify distinct cause-effect repertoires; the whole must specify more than its parts do, so that it cannot be partitioned without loss (integration); and only the set of elements that maximises integration (the "complex") is conscious, at its own spatial and temporal grain (exclusion). $\Phi$ measures the loss of information when the system is cut at its weakest partition. Versions 3.0 and 4.0 refined the formalism.

Computing $\Phi$ exactly is intractable for all but very small systems, since it requires searching over partitions, so practical proxies are used. The perturbational complexity index, inspired by IIT's ideas of integration and differentiation, measures the complexity of the EEG response to magnetic stimulation and discriminates conscious from unconscious states in some clinical and experimental settings; it is a theory-inspired empirical measure rather than $\Phi$ itself.

The theory has counterintuitive implications: because $\Phi$ depends on physical causal structure, a digital computer running conscious-seeming software would have very low $\Phi$ on conventional hardware, and some simple, highly interconnected systems would have high values. Critics, including a public 2023 letter, call these implications and the difficulty of testing it a failure of falsifiability; supporters dispute the characterisation. An adversarial collaboration comparing IIT and GNW predictions reported mixed results for IIT.

## Role in Active Inference
The relation to Active Inference is indirect and contested. The two frameworks address different questions: Active Inference describes how a system with a Markov blanket maintains itself through inference, while IIT characterises intrinsic cause-effect structure. Both concern integration of information in a system distinct from its environment, and some authors have explored connections between $\Phi$-like measures and the integration of beliefs in hierarchical models, but no accepted derivation links the two. Treating them as equivalent would overstate the evidence. See [[markov_blanket]] and [[artificial_consciousness]] for related discussion.
