# Retrograde Amnesia

## Overview
Retrograde amnesia is the loss of memories that were acquired before the onset of a causative event, such as a head injury, encephalitis or a surgical lesion. The loss can be confined to a short interval before the event or extend over decades, and may affect autobiographical events, facts about the world, or both. It frequently coexists with anterograde amnesia but can occur alone (see [[anterograde_amnesia]]). The cause may be structural damage, in which case recovery is limited, or functional, as in psychogenic amnesia.

## Temporal gradient
In many patients, recent memories are lost more than remote ones, an observation traced to Theodule Ribot in the nineteenth century, who proposed that newer memories are more fragile, and called Ribot's law. The gradient may span only a few years in some cases and decades in others. It is seen in patients with medial temporal lesions and in electroconvulsive therapy studies, though interpretation is complicated by the difficulty of equating the difficulty of tests across time periods.

## Theories
- **Standard consolidation.** Memories initially depend on the hippocampus and associated cortex, and with time and reorganisation become independent of it. Hippocampal damage thus destroys recent memories but spares remote ones (see [[systems_consolidation]]). Squire and Alvarez developed this view.
- **Multiple trace theory.** Nadel and Moscovitch argued that the hippocampus is always involved in retrieving detailed episodic memories, however old, and that only the gist, or semantic, content becomes independent of it. Extensive loss in patients with large lesions, including loss of remote episodic detail, supports this account.

Some patients show relative sparing of semantic knowledge compared with personal episodes, supporting a distinction between [[episodic_memory]] and [[semantic_memory]].

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is weak and speculative. Consolidation is sometimes described in this framework as optimisation of a generative model during offline states such as sleep, for instance by removing redundant parameters, which would move statistical regularities toward cortical storage and fit the shift from detailed episodes to gist. This remains a proposal, and it does not discriminate between the standard and multiple trace accounts above.
