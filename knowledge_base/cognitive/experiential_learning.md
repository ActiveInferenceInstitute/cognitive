# Experiential Learning

## Overview
Experiential learning refers to learning in which knowledge is built from direct involvement in activity and from reflection on that involvement, in contrast to learning from description or instruction alone. The term covers a family of educational theories, and also a loose set of practices such as laboratory work, apprenticeships, field study, simulations and service learning. Its central claim is that experience is not sufficient by itself: it becomes educational when the learner interprets it and carries the result forward into later action.

## Main accounts
Dewey (1938) argued that education should rest on experience, and that experiences differ in quality according to whether they support continuing growth. His principles of continuity (new experience builds on earlier experience) and interaction (experience arises from the interplay of learner and situation) are widely cited.

Kolb (1984) drew on Dewey, Lewin and Piaget to describe a cycle of four phases:
1. concrete experience;
2. reflective observation;
3. abstract conceptualisation;
4. active experimentation, which produces new concrete experience.

Kolb also proposed learning styles based on preferred positions in the cycle. The cycle is a conceptual model, and the empirical support for the learning-style typology is limited; critics have also questioned whether learners actually move through the phases in order.

## Relation to constructivism and practice
Experiential learning is closely related to [[constructivist_learning_theory]], which stresses that learners actively build understanding, and overlaps with [[discovery_learning]] and [[project_based_learning]]. Evidence reviews of minimally guided instruction suggest that learners benefit when experience is combined with structure, feedback and explicit reflection, so the approaches that work best are usually guided ones.

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is conceptual. Active Inference describes an agent that acts to gather observations and updates its generative model from the outcomes, which resembles the loop of experimentation and reflection in Kolb's cycle. Choosing actions for the information they provide (see [[epistemic_foraging]]) parallels purposeful experimentation. This is an analogy: Kolb's model is a description of education and has not been formalised in these terms.
