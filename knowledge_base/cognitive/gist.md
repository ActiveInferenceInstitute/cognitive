# Gist

## Overview
Gist is the general meaning, theme or essential content of an experience, as opposed to its exact surface form or verbatim detail. People typically remember what a conversation, story or event was about long after the specific wording, order or perceptual details have been lost. The term is applied to memory for texts and events, and also to perception, where the gist of a scene, such as its category or layout, is available within a glance.

## Gist in memory
Bartlett (1932) showed that people retelling an unfamiliar story shortened it, simplified it and altered details to fit their own cultural expectations, which he attributed to the use of schemas (see [[schemas]]). Later work separated the retention of meaning from retention of surface form: memory for the exact words of a sentence decays quickly, while memory for its meaning lasts longer.

Fuzzy-trace theory (Brainerd and Reyna, 1990; Reyna and Brainerd, 1995) formalises this. It proposes that experience is encoded in parallel into verbatim traces, which preserve surface detail but fade quickly, and gist traces, which capture meaning and are more durable and are preferred for reasoning. A prediction of the theory is that false memories for items that fit the gist but were not presented can be as confident as true memories, as in the Deese-Roediger-McDermott word-list paradigm in which a related but unpresented word is often falsely recalled or recognised.

## Gist in perception
Observers can classify the category of a natural scene, such as a beach or a street, from very short exposures, before they identify its objects. Oliva and Torralba (2006) proposed that global image features, such as the spatial layout and the distribution of orientations, allow a rapid representation of scene gist (see [[scene_perception]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is an analogy and a partial formal parallel. Hierarchical generative models represent states at several levels of abstraction, with higher levels being slower and more general, which matches the idea of a durable gist and a more transient detail. Learning that compresses experience into abstract categories is consistent with complexity-minimising accounts of memory, but a detailed Active Inference model of fuzzy-trace effects has not been established.
