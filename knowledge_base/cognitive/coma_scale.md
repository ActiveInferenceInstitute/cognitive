# Coma Scale

## Overview
A coma scale is a standardised bedside score for the level of responsiveness of a patient with impaired consciousness. The best known is the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), introduced by Teasdale and Jennett (1974) to give clinicians a common, reproducible language for describing the depth and duration of impaired consciousness after head injury. It is now used in emergency care, intensive care and trauma research.

## Scoring
The GCS rates the best observed response in three components.
- **Eye opening** from 1 to 4: none, to pressure, to sound, spontaneous.
- **Verbal response** from 1 to 5: none, incomprehensible sounds, inappropriate words, confused, oriented.
- **Motor response** from 1 to 6: none, extension, abnormal flexion, withdrawal, localising, obeying commands.

The sum ranges from 3 to 15. Reporting the three components separately, for example E3 V4 M5, keeps information that the total hides, and it is the recommended practice in a review of the scale after forty years of use (Teasdale et al., 2014).

## Limitations
The score depends on the examiner's technique and on conditions that affect behaviour independently of brain state. Verbal response cannot be assessed in an intubated patient, eye opening may be prevented by facial swelling, and sedation, paralysis or intoxication can lower scores. The GCS does not test brainstem reflexes. The Full Outline of UnResponsiveness (FOUR) score adds brainstem reflexes and breathing pattern and allows scoring of intubated patients (Wijdicks et al., 2005). Other instruments, such as the Coma Recovery Scale-Revised, are used for finer assessment during recovery.

## Relation to awareness and Active Inference
A coma scale measures behavioural responsiveness, which is an indirect indicator of awareness. A patient who scores low may still show signs of awareness on specialised testing, so the score should not be read as a direct measure of conscious experience (see [[disorders_consciousness]]). There is no formal link to Active Inference. Theoretical work in that framework on consciousness concerns the structure of generative models, not clinical scoring.
