# Rehearsal

## Overview
Rehearsal is the repetition of information, aloud or silently, in order to keep it available or to improve later recall. In the multi-store model of Atkinson and Shiffrin (1968) it was the control process that maintained items in a short-term store and, by extension, transferred them to long-term memory. The idea survives in more refined form in working-memory theory, where rehearsal is a mechanism for countering decay.

## Forms of rehearsal
- **Maintenance rehearsal** repeats an item in unchanged form. It keeps the item accessible over a short delay, for example holding a telephone number between hearing and dialling it.
- **Elaborative rehearsal** relates the item to existing knowledge, imagery or meaning. Craik and Lockhart (1972) argued that durable memory depends on the depth of processing rather than on the amount of repetition, and elaborative encoding generally produces better long-term retention than rote repetition.
- **Articulatory rehearsal** is the subvocal speech process in the [[phonological_loop]], a component of the working-memory model of Baddeley and Hitch (1974). It explains word-length and articulatory-suppression effects: lists of long words are recalled less well because fewer can be refreshed before they fade, and suppression prevents refreshing.

Rehearsal is not the only maintenance route. Attention-based refreshing and activity-silent storage have been proposed as alternatives, and rehearsal strategies develop with age, with young children using them far less than adolescents.

## Relation to Active Inference
Rehearsal can be described as repeatedly regenerating a representation so that it remains the best explanation of an internal state, much as a belief that is not renewed loses precision. This reading is an analogy rather than a derived result. Active Inference has formal treatments of working-memory delays through sustained beliefs in generative models, but no standard account singles out rehearsal as a distinct process.
