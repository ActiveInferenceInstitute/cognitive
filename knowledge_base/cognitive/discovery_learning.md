# Discovery Learning

## Overview
In discovery learning, students are not told a principle outright. They investigate materials, problems or phenomena and infer the underlying rule or relationship for themselves. Jerome Bruner (1961) argued that discovery promotes understanding that transfers, intrinsic motivation and skill in the methods of inquiry. The approach is associated with constructivist views of learning, and with progressive education.

## Forms of discovery
The literature separates several degrees of guidance. In **pure discovery** the learner receives little or no instruction, hints or feedback. **Guided discovery** supplies structure, such as well-chosen examples, prompts, feedback or scaffolds, while leaving the learner to make the key inference. **Problem-based** and **inquiry** approaches embed discovery in larger tasks. The term is used loosely, so reports of its effectiveness need to be read with attention to which form is meant.

## Evidence
Reviews have generally found that pure discovery is less effective and less efficient than explicit instruction, especially for novices, in line with cognitive load arguments. Mayer (2004) concluded that pure discovery fails but guided discovery can work, and that "activity" should be cognitive, not merely behavioural. A meta-analysis by Alfieri and colleagues (2011) reported that discovery-based methods outperformed unassisted discovery and, on average, explicit instruction when learners received feedback, worked examples or scaffolding. Findings depend on content, learner expertise and measure of outcome.

## Relation to Active Inference
Active Inference offers a conceptual parallel. Agents choose actions to reduce uncertainty about their environment, through the epistemic value (information gain) term of expected free energy, so exploration that resolves uncertainty is intrinsically favoured, and curiosity can be formalised in this way. This is an analogy to the educational notion of exploratory learning. It makes no claim about which instructional design works best, and empirical educational findings do not follow from it.
