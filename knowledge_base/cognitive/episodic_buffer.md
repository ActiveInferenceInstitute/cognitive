# Episodic Buffer

## Overview
The episodic buffer is a component of the multicomponent model of working memory, introduced by Baddeley in 2000 to address findings that the original phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad and central executive could not explain. It is a temporary store with limited capacity that holds integrated multimodal representations, or episodes. The name does not mean it is part of episodic long-term memory; it is a short-term interface to it.

## Core claims
Baddeley proposed that the buffer:
- **Is multimodal.** It uses a code that combines information from the phonological loop, the visuospatial sketchpad, perception and long-term memory.
- **Binds information into chunks.** For instance, it can link the words of a sentence with visual and semantic content, which explains why recall of prose far exceeds the capacity of the phonological loop alone.
- **Has limited capacity.** Baddeley described this as a small number of chunks, of the order of four, although the exact limit and its units are debated.
- **Is controlled by the central executive.** Binding is an attention-demanding operation, but evidence suggests that binding in some conditions does not require much additional executive resource.
- **Supports conscious access.** Its contents are available to awareness and can be manipulated, retrieved and recombined.

Motivating findings include effects of semantic similarity on immediate recall, the benefit of meaningful structure on span, and patients with severely impaired long-term memory who retain good short-term prose recall. Whether a distinct buffer is needed, or whether the phenomena are explained by activated long-term memory in the embedded-process view, remains debated. The neural basis is also unsettled, with proposals involving frontal and parietal regions and the hippocampus.

## Role in Active Inference
The connection is an analogy, not a derivation. The buffer's function, maintaining a bound, coherent scene-like representation, resembles holding a joint posterior over latent states that explains inputs across modalities. In Active Inference accounts of working memory and attention, sustained activity is cast as beliefs maintained over a short timescale with high precision (Parr and Friston, 2017). Neither the capacity limit nor the buffer's specific role has been derived from the free-energy principle.
