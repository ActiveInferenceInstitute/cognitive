# Supplementary Motor Area

## Overview
The supplementary motor area lies in Brodmann area 6 on the medial surface of the frontal lobe, anterior to the leg representation of the primary motor cortex. A more rostral division, the pre-supplementary motor area, is usually distinguished from the supplementary motor area proper. The two differ in connectivity and function: the proper area connects strongly with primary motor cortex and the spinal cord, while the pre-supplementary area has closer ties with prefrontal cortex and is more involved in cognitive aspects of preparation.

## Functions
- **Sequencing.** Single-neuron recordings in monkeys and imaging in people show activity related to the order of multiple movements and to the retrieval of learned sequences (Tanji, 2001).
- **Self-initiated movement.** Activity precedes movements that are generated internally more than movements triggered by external cues. The medial frontal cortex contributes to the slow negative scalp potential that precedes voluntary movement, known as the readiness potential.
- **Bimanual coordination.** Lesions and inactivation disturb coordinated movements of the two hands.
- **Response selection and control.** The pre-supplementary area is engaged when responses must be selected among alternatives, switched or inhibited (Nachev, Kennard and Husain, 2008).

Electrical stimulation in people has been reported to evoke movements or an urge to move. After surgical removal or damage, a transient reduction of spontaneous movement and speech can occur, which usually recovers in the following weeks. The area is one node of cortico-basal ganglia-thalamic loops, which is one reason movement disorders of the basal ganglia affect self-initiated movement disproportionately.

## Relation to Active Inference
In the Active Inference treatment of the motor system, movement results from descending proprioceptive predictions that spinal reflex arcs then fulfil, rather than from explicit motor commands (Adams, Shipp and Friston, 2013). Sequences of movements correspond to policies over time. The supplementary and pre-supplementary areas are plausible candidates for representing such sequential plans, but this is an interpretation that fits the functional data, not a finding that discriminates between theories.
