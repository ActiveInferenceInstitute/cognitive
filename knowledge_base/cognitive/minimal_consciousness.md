# Minimal Consciousness

## Overview
The term is used in two senses. The clinical sense, the minimally conscious state (MCS), names a condition after severe brain injury in which a person shows reproducible but inconsistent behavioural evidence of awareness. The philosophical sense, minimal phenomenal experience, asks what the simplest form of conscious experience is, with all specific content removed. The cited consciousness pages list it among states of awareness, between unresponsive wakefulness and full consciousness, so the clinical sense is primary here.

## Definition / Formalism
**Clinical.** Disorders of consciousness are distinguished by behaviour. In coma there is neither arousal nor awareness. In unresponsive wakefulness (formerly vegetative state) sleep-wake cycles return but behaviour is reflexive. In MCS, diagnostic criteria published by Giacino and colleagues in 2002 require clearly discernible but fluctuating evidence of awareness, such as following simple commands, intelligible verbalization, or purposeful behaviour. Emergence from MCS is marked by reliable functional communication or object use. Locked-in syndrome is a different case, with preserved awareness but near-total motor paralysis. Behavioural assessment is known to misclassify some patients; neuroimaging and EEG paradigms have detected command-following in some behaviourally unresponsive patients, and this area of research is active ([[neuroimaging]]).

**Philosophical.** Minimal phenomenal experience refers to the hypothesized most basic content-free state of awareness, discussed in relation to deep meditation and tonic alertness (Metzinger, 2020). It is distinct from the minimal self, which concerns the sense of being a subject of experience.

Theories of consciousness make different predictions about what the minimal case requires: global workspace theories point to broadcast across a network ([[global_workspace]]), integrated information theory to nonzero integration ([[integrated_information]]), and higher-order theories to metarepresentation.

## Role in Active Inference
Active Inference-based proposals tie consciousness to deep temporal generative models and to inference about interoceptive and bodily states, with a minimal form associated with basic self-modelling and a sense of agency ([[self_modeling]]). These are hypotheses within the free-energy tradition and have not been validated as clinical markers; they offer no diagnostic tests or guidance on care for patients in MCS.
