# Active Recall

## Overview
Active recall is a study strategy in which a learner tries to produce information from memory, for example by answering a question or writing out what was studied, instead of re-reading the material. In the research literature it is usually called retrieval practice, and the benefit it produces is called the testing effect. Retrieval is treated as a learning event in its own right, not only as a way of measuring what has been learned.

## Key findings
- **Testing effect.** Material that is tested is typically remembered better after a delay than material that is restudied for an equal amount of time, even when the test gives no feedback. The advantage tends to appear at long delays, while restudying can look better in the short term.
- **Retrieval effort.** Retrieval that succeeds but takes effort, such as free recall compared with recognition, tends to produce larger gains. This fits the broader idea of desirable difficulties.
- **Feedback and spacing.** Corrective feedback helps when retrieval fails. Combining retrieval with distributed practice gives more durable retention than either alone (see [[spaced_repetition]] and [[spaced_retrieval]]).
- **Metacognitive illusions.** Learners often judge re-reading as more effective than self-testing, because re-reading feels fluent. Judgments of learning made right after study are poor guides to later performance (see [[judgment_of_learning]]).

## Proposed mechanisms
Accounts differ. Some stress that retrieval strengthens and elaborates the retrieval routes to a memory, so that later access is easier. Others emphasise that retrieving a memory makes it labile and re-stores it in a modified form (see [[reconsolidation]] and [[memory_consolidation]]). No single account is settled, and effects depend on materials, delay and the form of the test.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is an analogy rather than a derivation. Retrieval can be described as inference in a generative model, where a partial cue is completed into a full memory (compare [[pattern_completion]]). Testing the model by generating from it gives informative prediction errors about which parts of the model are weak. This resembles an epistemic action, because it reveals what the learner does not know. No Active Inference model of the testing effect has been established.
