# Constructivist Learning Theory

## Overview
Constructivist learning theory holds that knowledge is not passively received but actively constructed by the learner, who interprets new information in light of existing understanding. Learning is therefore conceived as a change in the learner's own mental structures, and not as a transfer of content from teacher to student. The label covers several related positions rather than a single theory.

## Main strands
- **Cognitive constructivism.** Piaget described learning as the adaptation of schemas through assimilation of new experience into existing structures and accommodation of structures when experience does not fit. Disequilibrium between the two drives conceptual change.
- **Social constructivism.** Vygotsky emphasised that higher mental functions originate in social interaction and are mediated by language and cultural tools. His notion of the zone of proximal development refers to what a learner can do with help but not yet alone.
- **Bruner's approach.** Bruner stressed that learners can discover underlying structure in a subject, and he proposed a spiral curriculum that revisits ideas at increasing depth.
- Radical constructivism, associated with von Glasersfeld, makes the stronger epistemological claim that knowledge fits experience instead of mirroring an independent reality; this differs from the pedagogical position.

## Instructional implications
Constructivist-inspired teaching includes inquiry and problem-based activities, collaboration, scaffolding, and attention to learners' prior conceptions and misconceptions. Critics, notably Kirschner, Sweller and Clark, argued that minimally guided instruction ignores the limits of working memory and that novices learn better from explicit guidance. Defenders reply that constructivism is a theory of how learning occurs, not a prescription for unguided teaching, and that guided approaches can still be constructivist.

## Relation to Active Inference
Active Inference describes perception and learning as the construction and revision of an internal generative model by an agent acting on its world. This parallels the constructivist emphasis on learner-built structure, and structure learning in particular resembles accommodation. The correspondence is an analogy at the level of ideas, and the two literatures are not formally connected.
