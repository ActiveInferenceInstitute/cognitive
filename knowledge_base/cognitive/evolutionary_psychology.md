# Evolutionary Psychology

## Overview
Evolutionary psychology is a research approach that treats the human mind as a set of information-processing mechanisms shaped by natural selection over ancestral environments. Its central proposal is that behaviour should be explained by reference to the adaptive problems these mechanisms evolved to solve, such as choosing mates, cooperating, avoiding hazards and raising offspring (Tooby & Cosmides, 1992). It is one of several ways of linking psychology with evolutionary biology, alongside human behavioural ecology and gene-culture coevolution.

## Core claims and methods
- **Domain-specific mechanisms.** Proponents hold that the mind includes many specialised mechanisms and not only general-purpose learning, a view sometimes called massive modularity.
- **Mismatch.** Because selection acted on past environments, present behaviour may not maximise reproductive success in modern settings.
- **Hypothesis generation.** Researchers derive predictions from adaptive problems, for example about social exchange, kin recognition, sex differences in preferences or threat detection, and test them with experiments, cross-cultural surveys and comparisons with other species.

## Criticisms
Gould and Lewontin (1979) criticised adaptationist reasoning in biology for constructing plausible stories for each trait without testing alternatives such as drift, constraints or by-products. Critics of evolutionary psychology make related points about the difficulty of reconstructing ancestral environments, the evidence for modularity and the role of cultural variation and development. Proponents reply that hypotheses are testable and that cross-cultural and developmental data constrain them. The field continues to debate how far evolutionary arguments should guide explanations of present-day behaviour.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is interpretive. The free energy principle describes how self-organising systems persist, and evolutionary timescales are treated as shaping the priors and generative model structure inherited by individuals, so that some expectations are innate and others learned. Badcock, Friston and Ramstead (2019) proposed a hierarchical free-energy formulation of the human psyche that explicitly draws on evolutionary considerations. This is a theoretical programme, and neither framework provides a derivation of the other.
