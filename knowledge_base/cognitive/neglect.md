# Neglect

## Overview
Neglect is a neuropsychological syndrome in which a person fails to detect, respond to or orient toward stimuli on the side of space opposite a brain lesion, even though basic sensory and motor function is largely intact. It follows right-hemisphere damage more often, and more severely, than left-hemisphere damage, producing neglect of the left side. Patients may omit the left half of a meal, leave out the left side of a drawing, or bisect a line well to the right of its centre. The condition affects daily life and recovery, and it is not a form of blindness. The page [[spatial_neglect]] treats the spatial form in more detail; this page treats the syndrome in general.

## Features and anatomy
Neglect can occur in perception, in action, or in imagery: in a classic study, patients asked to imagine a familiar Milan square described buildings on only one side, and described the other side when imagining the opposite viewpoint (Bisiach & Luzzatti, 1978). The lesions most often implicated lie in right inferior parietal cortex, the temporoparietal junction and inferior frontal regions, and damage to white matter pathways linking them is also reported. The relative importance of each site is disputed, partly because lesions are large and variable. Neglect also overlaps with non-spatial deficits of sustained attention and arousal (Husain & Rorden, 2003).

## Accounts
Explanations include a deficit in directing attention toward contralesional space, an imbalance between the two hemispheres' attentional biases, and a deficit in the ventral attention network that disrupts reorienting, with changes in dorsal network function as a consequence (Corbetta & Shulman, 2011). These accounts are not exclusive, and each captures some but not all dissociations seen across patients.

## Relation to Active Inference
Parr and Friston (2018) built an active inference model of visual search in the line cancellation task and showed that lesioning it at three distinct levels reproduces neglect-like search deficits, with each level mapped onto the neuroanatomy of saccadic eye movements. This is an active research programme in which a lesion is expressed as an alteration of inference, though the model's anatomical mapping remains hypothesised.
