# Addiction

## Overview
Addiction, in clinical classification a substance use disorder or, for some behaviours, a behavioural addiction, is characterised by continued use or engagement despite harmful consequences, loss of control over intake, and strong craving. It is chronic and relapsing in many individuals. Risk reflects genetic, developmental, social and environmental factors, and neither a single cause nor a single mechanism accounts for all cases. The entry here concerns the cognitive and neural accounts of the disorder, not treatment guidance.

## Neural and cognitive accounts
- **Reward and learning.** Addictive drugs increase dopamine signalling in the mesolimbic system, and dopamine is linked to reward prediction errors (see [[dopamine_system]] and [[reward_processing]]). Repeated drug exposure is proposed to hijack learning so that drug-associated cues acquire large motivational weight.
- **Incentive sensitisation.** Robinson and Berridge proposed that sensitisation of mesolimbic systems makes drug cues attract strong motivational "wanting", which can grow while the pleasure of "liking" does not.
- **From goal-directed to habitual to compulsive.** Everitt and Robbins described a progression in which drug use begins as goal-directed action, shifts toward habit controlled by striatal circuits, and may become compulsive (see [[automaticity]]).
- **Impaired control.** Heightened impulsivity, steep temporal discounting and reduced prefrontal regulation are reported, and they may be both risk factors and consequences (see [[impulsivity]] and [[temporal_discounting]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
Addiction is a target of computational psychiatry in the Active Inference framework. Proposals describe the effects of drugs and cues as changes in the precision assigned to beliefs or policies, often linked to dopamine, so that policies leading to drug use are given excessive confidence, or as the use of an inaccurate model of the world that makes drug seeking appear optimal. These accounts reproduce some behavioural features, such as persistence despite cost, and are best seen as hypotheses. They have not displaced other models, for example those from reinforcement learning, and empirical tests remain limited.
