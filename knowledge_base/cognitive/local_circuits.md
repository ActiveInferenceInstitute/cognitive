# Local Circuits

## Overview
Local circuits are the networks of neurons confined to a small region of tissue, typically within a cortical column or area, whose connections are dense and mostly short-range. The cortex shows a recurring laminar and cell-type organization across areas, which has motivated the idea of a canonical microcircuit: a common template of connectivity adapted to different functions. The term here refers to cortical microcircuits, not to long-range projections between areas or to electronic circuits.

## Structure
- **Layers**: input arrives mainly in layer 4; superficial layers (2/3) send output to higher areas and deep layers (5/6) send output to subcortical targets and back to lower areas. The classic functional microcircuit of Douglas, Martin, and colleagues (1989) describes excitatory feedback loops among pyramidal populations stabilized by inhibition.
- **Cell classes**: excitatory pyramidal and spiny stellate cells, and inhibitory interneurons, notably parvalbumin-expressing (perisomatic inhibition), somatostatin-expressing (dendritic inhibition), and VIP-expressing cells that inhibit other interneurons.
- **Motifs**: recurrent excitation, feedforward and feedback inhibition, and disinhibition. These generate gain control, oscillations, and selective amplification.
- **Variation**: layer thickness and cell proportions differ across areas, so "canonical" describes a shared motif and not identical circuits.

## Role in Active Inference
This is one of the more concrete anatomical hypotheses associated with the framework. Bastos and colleagues (2012) proposed that predictive-coding message passing maps onto the canonical microcircuit: superficial pyramidal cells encode prediction errors and send them forward, while deep pyramidal cells encode expectations about causes and send predictions backward, with granular and inhibitory populations completing the loop. A related spectral signature, gamma-band activity in superficial layers and alpha/beta in deep layers, has support in laminar recordings, though how far it generalizes across areas and species is under investigation. The mapping is a process theory that makes testable claims about layers and cell classes; it is not an established description of cortex, and alternative functional interpretations of the same circuitry exist.
