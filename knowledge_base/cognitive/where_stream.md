# Where Stream

## Overview
The where stream is the visual pathway that runs from primary visual cortex dorsally through motion-sensitive area MT and related regions into the posterior parietal cortex. Ungerleider and Mishkin (1982) proposed that it represents the spatial relationships among objects, in contrast to a ventral "what" pathway for identity (see [[what_stream]]). The anatomical system is the same as the [[dorsal_stream]], and the "where" label stresses an interpretation of its function. Goodale and Milner (1992) proposed instead that the pathway supports vision for action, computing the spatial information needed to reach, grasp and orient, which is why it is sometimes called the "how" stream.

## Evidence and functions
- **Lesion dissociation.** In monkeys, parietal lesions impaired judgements of spatial relations, whereas inferotemporal lesions impaired object discrimination (Mishkin, Ungerleider and Macko, 1983). In people, the patient D.F., who had damage to ventral occipital cortex, could not report the orientation of a slot but could post a card through it accurately. Patients with optic ataxia show the opposite pattern, with preserved recognition and poor visually guided reaching (Goodale and Milner, 1992).
- **Motion and location.** Neurons in area MT are tuned to the direction and speed of motion, and parietal neurons encode target locations in reference frames useful for eye and limb movements.
- **Attention.** Parietal regions are involved in orienting spatial attention, and unilateral damage can cause hemispatial neglect (see [[spatial_neglect]]).

The separation is not complete: the two pathways exchange information, and tasks such as grasping objects of different shapes need both. The strict anatomical dichotomy has been refined by later work.

## Relation to Active Inference
Active Inference models of visual search treat gaze as an action chosen to reduce uncertainty about the scene, and attribute the choice of where to look to expected free energy with an epistemic component (Friston et al., 2012). In scene construction models the hidden states are factorised into identity and location, which parallels the two pathways. The factorisation is a design decision that follows the anatomical picture and does not follow from the theory. In adaptive resonance theory, a spatial "where" system complements the "what" system that learns categories.
