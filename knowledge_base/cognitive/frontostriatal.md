# Frontostriatal Circuits

## Overview
Frontostriatal circuits are loops in which regions of the frontal cortex project to the striatum, the striatum projects through the globus pallidus and substantia nigra pars reticulata to the thalamus, and the thalamus projects back to the frontal cortex. They are the principal organisation of the [[basal_ganglia]] with the cerebral cortex, and they are central to selecting actions, learning from outcomes and regulating goal-directed behaviour.

## Organisation
Alexander, DeLong and Strick (1986) proposed that the cortical-basal ganglia projections form several parallel, largely segregated loops. Their original scheme comprised motor, oculomotor, dorsolateral prefrontal, lateral orbitofrontal and anterior cingulate loops. Later work describes a gradient from sensorimotor territory in the dorsolateral striatum, through associative territory, to limbic territory in the ventral striatum, with some crosstalk between loops through shared structures and dopamine projections. Within each loop there are two main routes:
- a **direct pathway**, through striatal neurons expressing D1-type dopamine receptors, which tends to facilitate the selected action;
- an **indirect pathway**, through neurons expressing D2-type receptors, which tends to suppress competing actions.

Dopamine from the midbrain modulates both pathways, and phasic changes in dopamine signal reward prediction errors (see [[dopamine_system]]).

## Functions and disorders
The loops contribute to action selection, habit formation, working memory gating and set shifting, with the more anterior loops supporting cognitive and motivational functions. Disorders involving these circuits include Parkinson's disease, Huntington's disease, obsessive-compulsive disorder, Tourette syndrome and some features of schizophrenia and addiction. The functional specificity of individual loops is a model, and anatomical and behavioural findings show considerable overlap between them.

## Relation to Active Inference
Active Inference proposals map the basal ganglia onto policy selection, with striatal activity encoding the evidence for policies from expected free energy and dopamine encoding the precision of that choice (Friston et al., 2014). The anatomical mapping is a hypothesis, with some support from simulation, and competing reinforcement-learning accounts of the same circuits remain influential (see [[reinforcement_learning]]).
