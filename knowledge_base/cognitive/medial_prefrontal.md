# Medial Prefrontal Cortex

## Overview
The medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) comprises the medial surface of the frontal lobe anterior to the premotor regions, including medial parts of Brodmann area 10 and parts of areas 9, 14, 25, and 32. It is often divided into dorsomedial (dmPFC), ventromedial (vmPFC), and sometimes the adjacent [[anterior_cingulate]]. It is a core node of the default mode network.

## Mechanism
Functions differ by subregion but cluster around internally and socially oriented processing. The dmPFC is repeatedly engaged when people infer others' mental states ([[theory_of_mind]]) and in more abstract social judgements. The vmPFC and adjacent orbitofrontal cortex track subjective value and are involved in decision making, emotion regulation ([[emotion_regulation]]), and extinction memory. The anterior mPFC is active in self-referential processing, such as judging one's own traits, and the region participates in autobiographical memory, imagination of the future, and schema-based memory consolidation.

Amodio and Frith's review distinguished a posterior-to-anterior gradient in medial frontal cortex from action monitoring toward person perception and mentalizing. The default mode network, described by Raichle and colleagues as showing high activity at rest and decreases during demanding external tasks, includes the mPFC, posterior cingulate, and inferior parietal cortex, and has been linked to self-generated thought. Because many reverse-inference claims map one region to one function, the specificity of these roles should be treated with caution: the same mPFC voxels respond across self, social, value, and memory tasks.

## Role in Active Inference
Medial prefrontal and default-mode regions sit near the top of the cortical hierarchy and are proposed to support high-level, temporally extended generative models, including models of oneself and of other agents; see [[self_awareness]] and [[metacognition]]. In this reading, self-referential processing is inference over a self-model, and mentalizing is inference over models of others. The vmPFC's value signals correspond to the expected-outcome and preference terms of the generative model ([[decision_making]]). Alexander and Brown's work treats medial frontal cortex as predicting action outcomes. These mappings are interpretive; there is no consensus that mPFC implements the particular computations described, and alternative frameworks (reinforcement learning, constructive episodic simulation) explain the same findings.
