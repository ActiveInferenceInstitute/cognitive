# Freezing

## Overview
Freezing is a defensive response in which an animal ceases all movement except that needed for breathing and posture. It is best known from rodent studies, in which the proportion of time spent freezing is a standard measure of conditioned fear (an early use is Blanchard and Blanchard, 1969, who scored "crouching"). Comparable behaviour is observed across vertebrate species, and in humans it has been described as a brief pause in movement and a drop in heart rate when threat is detected.

## Function and context
Freezing is one point on a defensive repertoire whose expression depends on how near and how inescapable a threat appears. A common scheme orders responses from avoidance and risk assessment, through freezing when a threat is distant or unlocated, to flight when escape is possible and active defence when the threat is in contact. Freezing may reduce detection by predators sensitive to movement, and it can support attention to the threat. Roelofs (2017) reviews evidence that, in humans, freezing is accompanied by bradycardia, lower postural sway and heightened attentive preparation, and not just by passive helplessness, and that it can precede an active response.

## Neural basis
In rodents, the central nucleus of the amygdala projects to the ventrolateral part of the periaqueductal grey, which in turn drives the brainstem and spinal circuits that produce the immobility (LeDoux, 2000). Different amygdala output pathways and local microcircuits are involved in selecting freezing instead of flight. The prefrontal cortex and hippocampus modulate expression, and extinction training reduces freezing (see [[fear_circuit]]). Dysregulation is of interest in anxiety and trauma-related conditions, but relations between animal freezing and human symptoms are not simple.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is an interpretive analogy. Freezing can be described as a policy chosen when expected free energy favours withholding action while gathering information about an uncertain threat. In human work, this has been linked to a prediction that is not yet resolved between fight and flight, but there is no consensus Active Inference model of freezing, and alternative accounts treat it as a hard-wired defensive mode selected by threat imminence.
