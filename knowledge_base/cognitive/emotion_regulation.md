# Emotion Regulation

## Overview
Emotion regulation is the set of processes by which people influence which emotions they have, when they have them, and how they experience and express them. It can be automatic or deliberate, and it targets the intensity, duration, or type of emotional response.

## Mechanism
Gross's process model organises strategies by the point in the emotion-generating sequence at which they act: situation selection, situation modification, attentional deployment, cognitive change (reappraisal), and response modulation (such as expressive suppression). The first four are antecedent-focused and the last is response-focused. Experimental work has most often compared reappraisal, which reinterprets the meaning of a stimulus, with suppression, which inhibits expressive behaviour.

Neuroimaging studies of voluntary reappraisal report engagement of lateral and medial prefrontal regions ([[dorsolateral_prefrontal]], ventrolateral prefrontal, [[medial_prefrontal]]) and the [[anterior_cingulate]], with modulated activity in the amygdala and insula. The standard interpretation is that prefrontal control systems adjust appraisal and affective responding, in line with general [[cognitive_control]] and [[inhibitory_control]]. Regulation draws on [[working_memory]] and [[cognitive_flexibility]], and individuals differ widely in strategy use and success.

## Role in Active Inference
In Active Inference, emotion is commonly described as inference about the causes and expected consequences of bodily and environmental states, with valence linked to how well the organism is doing at reducing free energy (proposals include valence as the rate of change of free energy). Regulation then corresponds to changing the inference: reappraisal revises beliefs about the situation, attentional deployment changes which prediction errors are given precision ([[precision_weighting]]), and situation selection or modification is action that changes the sensory evidence the agent will receive. Suppression acts on expression rather than on the underlying inference. This mapping is an interpretive redescription of the process model rather than a tested derivation, and the deep-active-inference models of affect that exist are mainly simulation studies. For the homeostatic basis, see [[allostatic_control]].
