# Noun Phrases

## Overview
A noun phrase (NP) is a phrase whose central element, or head, is a noun or pronoun. It may consist of a single word, as in "water" or "she", or be expanded with other elements, as in "the old house by the river". Noun phrases name or describe entities and are one of the principal building blocks of sentences. In many theories, a head combines with its dependents to form a constituent of the same category, which gives phrase structure its hierarchical form (see [[grammar]]).

## Structure
- **Determiners and quantifiers** such as "the", "some" or "three" precede the noun in English and specify reference or quantity.
- **Modifiers** such as adjectives ("old"), prepositional phrases ("by the river") and relative clauses ("that I bought") add information about the head.
- **Complements** such as "that the plan failed" in "the claim that the plan failed" are selected by particular nouns.
- **Recursion.** A noun phrase can contain another, as in "the friend of the owner of the shop", so that arbitrarily long phrases can be formed.

Some syntactic theories treat the determiner as the head of a determiner phrase (DP) containing an NP, an analysis widely used in generative grammar, while others retain the noun as head. Descriptive grammars give a detailed account of English noun phrases (Huddleston and Pullum, 2002).

## Functions
Noun phrases appear as subjects, objects, complements and objects of prepositions. Their relation to the verb determines their role in the event described, such as agent or patient (see [[thematic_roles]]), and combine with [[verb_phrases]] to form clauses.

## Processing
In comprehension, listeners must decide how the noun phrases in a sentence relate to the verb as the words arrive. Temporary ambiguity, as in "the old man the boats", can lead to misparsing when a phrase is initially analysed as an NP and later requires reanalysis (see [[syntactic_parsing]] and [[sentence_processing]]). Agreement between the head noun and verb is also a source of production errors when another noun intervenes.

## Relation to Active Inference
Comprehension can be modelled as inference over a hierarchical structure in which grammatical categories and phrase boundaries are hidden states. Friston and colleagues (2020) discuss generative models of linguistic communication in this framework. Noun phrases are not given a special role there, and the connection is a general one between parsing and Bayesian inference.
