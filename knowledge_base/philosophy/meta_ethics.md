# Meta-Ethics

## Overview
Meta-ethics asks what morality is, as opposed to what is moral. Where [[normative_ethics]] asks which actions are right or which character traits are good, meta-ethics asks about the status of such claims: whether they can be true or false, whether there are moral facts, how moral terms get their meaning, whether moral knowledge is possible, and how moral judgments relate to motivation. The standard three-way division is meta-ethics, normative ethics and [[applied_ethics]].

## Central questions
- **Metaphysical.** Are there objective moral facts or properties, and if so are they natural properties, or irreducibly normative ones?
- **Semantic.** Do moral sentences express beliefs that can be true or false (cognitivism), or attitudes such as approval and commands (non-cognitivism)?
- **Epistemic.** How could moral knowledge be acquired, and what justifies moral beliefs?
- **Psychological.** Do moral judgments necessarily motivate (internalism) or only given suitable desires (externalism)?

## Main positions
- **Moral realism.** Moral claims are true or false independently of what anyone thinks (see [[moral_realism]]). Moore (1903) defended a non-naturalist version, arguing that "good" cannot be defined in natural terms.
- **Error theory.** Mackie (1977) argued that moral claims purport to describe objective values but that no such values exist, so they are systematically false.
- **Expressivism.** Ayer, Stevenson, Blackburn and Gibbard held that moral claims express attitudes instead of describing facts, and developed accounts of how such claims can still be reasoned about.
- **Constructivism.** Moral truths are the outputs of an idealised procedure of rational agreement or reflection.
- **Relativism.** Moral truth or justification is relative to a framework (see [[moral_relativism]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
There is no direct link. Active Inference describes agents with prior preferences over outcomes, and these preferences are specified within a model, not derived from a theory of value; the framework is silent on whether any preferences are morally correct. Work on moral psychology and social norms can be modelled with it, but such modelling describes how agents come to hold norms, not whether the norms are right.

## Related concepts
