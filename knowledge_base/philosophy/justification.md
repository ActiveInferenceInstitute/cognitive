# Justification

## Overview
In epistemology, justification is the status a belief has when it is held for good reasons, or in a way that makes it appropriately likely to be true. The traditional analysis of knowledge treats it as justified true belief, so justification is the element that separates knowing from lucky guessing. What it consists of, and how much of it knowledge requires, remain open questions.

## Main issues
- **Gettier problem.** Gettier (1963) described cases in which a person has a justified true belief that is true only by luck, so the three conditions are not sufficient for knowledge. Responses add a fourth condition, such as no defeating evidence or a reliable causal connection, or abandon the analysis of knowledge in terms of belief plus conditions.
- **Internalism and externalism.** Internalists hold that justification depends on factors accessible to the believer, such as evidence or reasons, with evidentialism as a prominent version. Externalists such as Goldman (1979) hold that it depends on features like the reliability of the process producing the belief, whether or not the believer can tell.
- **Structure.** [[foundationalism]] and [[coherence_theory]] disagree over whether justification flows from basic beliefs or from mutual support within a system.
- **Scepticism.** Arguments from dreaming, deception or regress challenge whether beliefs can be justified at all (see [[skepticism]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
Bayesian epistemology models rational belief as degrees of confidence updated by conditionalisation, and Active Inference implements approximate Bayesian updating in perception and learning (see [[bayesian_inference]]). This gives a computational account of how evidence changes belief, which fits evidentialist and probabilist accounts of justification. A reliabilist reading is also available, since precision-weighting can be seen as a measure of how reliable a signal is. These are interpretive bridges. The framework describes how agents in fact update, and does not by itself decide what justification is, or answer Gettier cases.
