# Downward Causation

## Overview
Downward causation is the claim that a whole, or a higher-level property, exerts a genuine causal influence on its own parts or lower-level processes. Donald Campbell (1974) used the term for hierarchically organised biological systems, where selection at the level of the organism shapes molecular and cellular events.

## Formulations and problems
**Kinds.** Weak downward causation says higher-level organisation acts as a boundary condition or constraint on lower-level dynamics, for instance a pipe's shape fixing the flow of its water molecules; the lower-level laws still hold throughout. Strong downward causation says higher-level properties add causal power not fixed by lower-level laws, which is much more contentious and is the sense associated with strong emergence ([[emergence]]).

**Exclusion argument.** Jaegwon Kim argued that if every physical event has a sufficient physical cause, a distinct mental or higher-level cause would be redundant, giving either reduction or epiphenomenalism. Responses: interventionist accounts of causation, where higher-level variables can be causes if intervening on them changes outcomes; and the point that supervenience means the two are not independent competing causes.

**Related notions.** Haken's slaving principle in synergetics (order parameters enslave fast variables), Sperry's emergent interactionism, and [[circular_causality]] in self-organising systems.

## Role in Active Inference
The Markov blanket formalism gives one precise, though contested, way to talk about the issue. Internal states are conditionally independent of external states given the blanket, which defines a boundary at which a "thing" and its environment can be distinguished; in hierarchical (nested) blanket structures, the dynamics of a higher-level system can be read as setting the priors or boundary conditions for lower-level ones, and slow variables constrain fast ones ([[hierarchical_processing]], [[free_energy_principle]]). This is a weak, constraint-style downward causation, compatible with lower-level laws. Whether it supports anything stronger is disputed, and critics have argued that Markov blankets are a modelling device which cannot by itself settle metaphysical questions about levels. The framework's own statements are generally instrumentalist about which partitioning one chooses ([[instrumentalism]]).
