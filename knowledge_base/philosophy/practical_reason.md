# Practical Reason

## Overview
Practical reason is reasoning about what to do, in contrast to theoretical reason, which concerns what to believe. Its conclusions are intentions or actions, and its premises include desires, ends, norms and facts about the situation. The topic originates in Aristotle's account of deliberation and practical wisdom (phronesis) and in Kant's account of reason as a source of moral law.

## Main positions
- **Humean instrumentalism.** Reason is the servant of the passions: it can identify means to ends and point out inconsistency, but it cannot itself supply ends. Williams (1981) defended a related view that reasons for action are tied to an agent's existing motivations, the "internal reasons" thesis.
- **Kantian constructivism.** Korsgaard (1996) argues that the authority of moral norms derives from the structure of reflective self-consciousness: agents act under practical identities, and she argues that this commits them to the norms those identities carry and ultimately to valuing humanity. Kant's categorical imperative is the paradigm (see [[deontological_ethics]]).
- **Aristotelian accounts.** Good practical judgement is a skill of perceiving what a situation requires, developed through habituation and tied to virtue ([[virtue_ethics]]).
- **Reasons-first views.** Raz (1999) takes reasons as primitive and examines the place of rules and exclusionary reasons in guiding action.

## Intention and planning
Bratman (1987) argued that intentions are not reducible to desires and beliefs. Humans form plans that settle future conduct, constrain further deliberation and coordinate action over time. This planning theory has influenced debates in action theory and in the study of agency ([[action_theory]], [[agency]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is a partial analogy. In Active Inference, selecting a policy by minimising expected free energy resembles the instrumental part of practical reason, since it chooses means given preferences encoded as prior beliefs, and planning as inference treats action selection as inference ([[planning_as_inference]]). The framework does not address the normative questions that divide philosophers, such as whether reason can criticise ends.
