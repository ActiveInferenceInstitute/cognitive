# Philosophy of Medicine

## Overview
The philosophy of medicine examines the concepts and methods of medical science and practice. Its main questions concern how health and disease should be defined, how medical knowledge is gained and justified, what explanatory models underlie clinical practice, and how knowledge relates to the values of care. Questions of medical ethics are treated separately (see [[biomedical_ethics]]).

## Health and disease
Naturalist accounts define disease as departure from normal biological function. Boorse (1977) proposed a biostatistical theory, on which disease is a statistically subnormal functional ability relative to a reference class. Normativist accounts argue that calling a condition a disease already involves a value judgement about what is undesirable, and hybrid accounts combine the two. Canguilhem, writing in The Normal and the Pathological, held that normality in biology is relative to the organism's capacity to establish new norms in response to the environment. The debate is especially active for psychiatric conditions, where disagreement concerns the criteria for disorder.

## Evidence and models
Evidence-based medicine, as defined by Sackett and colleagues (1996), involves the conscientious use of current best evidence in decisions about individual patients; philosophers discuss the ranking of randomised trials above other evidence, the reliability of mechanistic reasoning, and extrapolation from trial populations (see [[evidence_based_medicine]], [[causal_inference]]). Engel (1977) proposed the biopsychosocial model as an alternative to a narrowly biomedical one, in which psychological and social factors are included in explanation and treatment. Its adequacy has been debated.

## Relation to Active Inference
Computational psychiatry is an active research programme in which disorders are modelled as altered precision or priors in generative models (see [[computational_psychiatry]]). This programme proposes mechanisms, and philosophers of medicine can ask whether such models explain disorders, redefine them, or just redescribe them. The framework also relates to physiology through homeostasis and allostasis, a link developed in theoretical papers and not settled clinically.
