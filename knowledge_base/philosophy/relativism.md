# Relativism

## Overview
Relativism is the thesis that the truth or justification of some class of claims depends on a framework, such as a culture, language, conceptual scheme or perspective, with no neutral standpoint from which to rank the frameworks. It appears in several domains. Alethic or cognitive relativism concerns truth and rational belief, [[moral_relativism]] concerns ethical standards, and conceptual relativism concerns the carving of the world into categories. Its roots go back to Protagoras' dictum that man is the measure of all things.

## Forms and arguments
- **Incommensurability.** Kuhn (1962) argued that successive scientific paradigms differ in concepts and standards in ways that make direct comparison problematic. He denied that this made theory choice arbitrary, and the extent of his relativism is disputed.
- **Cultural and descriptive relativism.** Anthropological findings about the diversity of beliefs and mores are used to motivate relativism, though diversity alone does not entail that no view is better.
- **Self-refutation.** The standard objection is that a universal relativist thesis either applies to itself, and so lacks universal standing, or is an exception. Defenders reply that relativists can state their view within their own framework.
- **Critique and defence.** Boghossian (2006) argues that constructivist and relativist views of knowledge are incoherent or unsupported, while Baghramian (2004) surveys the strongest versions and notes that many are more modest than their critics suppose.

Positions near relativism include [[constructivism]], parts of [[postmodernism]] and some perspectivist views in [[social_epistemology]].

## Relation to Active Inference
Bayesian agents with different priors can reasonably hold different posterior beliefs from the same data, which has some resemblance to perspective-relative belief. Yet shared evidence and a common world constrain inference, and with enough evidence, agents with overlapping priors converge, which is closer to a moderate perspectivism than to relativism about truth. The connection is an analogy, and the framework is generally read as compatible with [[scientific_realism]].
