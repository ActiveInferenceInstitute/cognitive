# Philosophy of Mind

## Overview

Philosophy of mind investigates the nature of mental phenomena — consciousness, intentionality, perception, and their relationship to the physical world. The Free Energy Principle offers a distinctive philosophical framework: minds are generative models that minimize surprise through active inference, blurring the boundary between perception and action, mind and world.

## Key Themes

### The Mind-Body Problem
- How mental and physical properties relate
- FEP perspective: mind as the dynamics of a self-evidencing system
- Detailed in [[knowledge_base/free_energy_principle/philosophy/mind_body_problem|mind-body problem]]

### Consciousness
- Phenomenal experience and its neural correlates
- FEP accounts of minimal selfhood and temporal depth
- Related to [[knowledge_base/free_energy_principle/philosophy/consciousness_phil|consciousness]]

### Intentionality
- The "aboutness" of mental states
- Generative models as bearers of content
- Connection to [[pragmatism]] and [[peircean_semiotics]]

### 4E Cognition
- Embodied, embedded, enacted, and extended cognition
- FEP as a unifying framework for 4E approaches
- Detailed in [[4e_cognition]]

### The Dark Room Problem
- Why don't FEP agents seek minimal stimulation?
- Resolution through prior preferences and active inference
- Discussed in [[dark_room_problem]]
