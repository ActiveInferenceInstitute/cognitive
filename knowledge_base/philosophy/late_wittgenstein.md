# Late Wittgenstein

## Overview
Late Wittgenstein refers to the philosophy of Ludwig Wittgenstein after about 1930, presented mainly in the posthumous Philosophical Investigations (1953). It rejects the picture theory of language of his earlier Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus, according to which sentences mirror possible facts, in favour of attention to how words are used in human activities. Biletzki and Matar's Stanford Encyclopedia entry gives an overview, and interpreters differ on how sharp the break with the earlier work is.

## Central themes
- **Meaning and use**: the meaning of many words is their use in the language, not an object they name or a mental image attached to them.
- **Language-games and forms of life**: speaking is part of an activity, and language-games are embedded in shared practices and ways of living.
- **Family resemblance**: concepts such as "game" need not share a single essential feature but may be linked by overlapping similarities.
- **Rule-following**: no rule determines its own application, so following a rule depends on a shared practice. Kripke (1982) read this as a sceptical paradox about meaning, an interpretation whose fidelity to Wittgenstein is disputed.
- **Private language argument**: a language whose words refer to the speaker's private sensations, with no public criteria of correctness, is argued to be impossible. The scope and force of the argument are debated.
- **Philosophy as therapy**: philosophical problems are treated as arising from misunderstandings of ordinary language, to be dissolved by describing its use.

## Influence
The later work shaped ordinary-language philosophy and discussions of mind, where it stands behind criticisms of treating inner states as hidden objects. It is cited by authors in embodied and enactive cognitive science who stress practice and social context over internal representation, and by linguists interested in pragmatics.

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is thematic, not a derivation. Use-based accounts of meaning and communication as shared practice have been taken as motivation by some work on active inference models of communication and cultural niches, which treat shared conventions as learned and mutually inferred. Wittgenstein himself rejected explanatory theorising in philosophy, so the pairing is an interpretive one.
