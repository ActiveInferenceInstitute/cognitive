# Measurement Problem

## Overview

In this repo the term refers to the quantum measurement problem, the sense linked from the consciousness pages, not to the statistical problem of measurement error (see [[measurement_theory]] for that). Quantum theory describes an isolated system with deterministic, linear (unitary) evolution of the state vector, yet observation yields one definite outcome with probabilities given by the Born rule. The theory does not by itself say when or why the second kind of behaviour replaces the first.

## The problem

Suppose a system is in a superposition of two eigenstates of some observable, and a measuring device is coupled to it. Unitary dynamics applied to the combined system produces an entangled superposition of "device reads A with system in A" and "device reads B with system in B". No definite reading appears. Standard textbook quantum mechanics adds a separate collapse postulate for measurement, but leaves the boundary between "system" and "measuring apparatus" unspecified. Three questions follow: what counts as a measurement, why one outcome is realised, and why outcome frequencies follow the Born rule.

## Main responses

- **Collapse theories** (for example spontaneous-localisation models) modify the dynamics so that superpositions of macroscopic objects decay.
- **Many-worlds (Everett)** keeps unitary evolution only and takes all branches as real; the open issue is deriving probabilities.
- **Bohmian mechanics** adds definite particle positions guided by the wavefunction.
- **Decoherence** explains why interference between macroscopic alternatives becomes unobservable through interaction with the environment, but by itself does not select a single outcome.
- **Epistemic and relational readings** (for example QBism and relational quantum mechanics) treat the state as an agent's information or as relative to a system, relocating the problem.

The proposal that consciousness causes collapse (associated with von Neumann's analysis and later with Wigner) is a minority view and faces the objection that it needs an independent theory of consciousness.

## Role in Active Inference

The link is indirect. The free-energy principle is formulated for classical stochastic systems with Markov blankets. A separate research line by Fields, Friston, Glazebrook and Levin recasts the blanket as a holographic boundary between quantum systems and describes the interaction of system and environment in terms of quantum information, treating observers as bounded agents with limited access to the environment. Whether this resolves, dissolves or merely restates the measurement problem is contested, and the framework is not an established interpretation of quantum mechanics. Readers should treat claims that Active Inference explains consciousness-induced collapse as speculative.
