# Naturalism

## Overview
Naturalism is the view that philosophy does not have a separate subject matter or method from the sciences. Everything that exists belongs to the natural world studied by science, and philosophical questions should be pursued in continuity with scientific inquiry. The term covers a family of positions that differ in how strong a claim they make, and it is one of the dominant orientations in contemporary analytic philosophy.

## Forms of naturalism
- **Ontological (metaphysical) naturalism** holds that only natural entities and processes exist. It rejects supernatural beings and is often paired with physicalism about the mind, although it does not strictly require it.
- **Methodological naturalism** is a claim about inquiry: explanations should appeal to natural causes and be answerable to empirical evidence. It is compatible with leaving open what ultimately exists.
- **Epistemological naturalism** is associated with Quine's proposal that epistemology be treated as a chapter of psychology. How beliefs are actually formed and how they relate to sensory evidence replaces the search for an a priori foundation.
- **Pragmatist naturalism** descends from Dewey, who treated human thought and values as continuous with biological and social processes, and rejected a sharp division between mind and nature.

## Challenges
Critics raise several difficulties. It is unclear how to define "natural" without either circularity or an arbitrary appeal to current physics. Normativity, consciousness and mathematical truth are cited as phenomena that resist straightforward naturalisation. Some argue that the position's commitment to science is itself a philosophical stance that science cannot establish. Naturalists typically reply that the most reliable guide we have to what exists is the success of scientific method, and that philosophical puzzles tend to become more tractable when informed by empirical work.

## Relation to Active Inference
Active Inference is a naturalistic research programme: it seeks to explain perception, action and learning using a single mathematical framework grounded in physics and statistics, and it has been applied to questions about the mind that were previously mainly philosophical. The link is of orientation rather than derivation. Naturalism does not entail Active Inference, and accepting the free energy principle does not by itself settle disputes within naturalism, such as the status of representation or consciousness.
