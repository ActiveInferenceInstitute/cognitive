# Growing Block Theory

## Overview
The growing block theory, also called the growing universe or becoming theory, is a view in the metaphysics of time on which the past and the present are real while the future is not. Reality is a four-dimensional "block" that gains new layers as time passes, with the present as the leading edge of the block. It is a middle position between [[presentism]], which holds that only the present exists, and [[eternalism]], which holds that past, present and future are all equally real.

## Development and motivation
- **Origins.** C. D. Broad set out the view in *Scientific Thought* (1923), where he proposed that the sum total of existence increases by the addition of new events, the process being the passage of time. Tooley (1997) developed a version in which the future does not exist, and connected it to an account of causation and tense.
- **Motivations.** Proponents argue that the view accommodates the sense of an open future and of real becoming, while keeping the reality of the past that supports truth about previous events and avoids the problem of how presentists ground truths about the past.
- **Objections.** A well-known objection is the epistemic problem: if the present is just the edge of the block and past events are equally real, it is unclear how one could know that one is located at the edge and not in the past. Another is that the theory seems hard to reconcile with relativity, which denies a privileged global present moment, a difficulty that proponents address with different strategies and that is contested. The Stanford Encyclopedia article on time (Emery, Markosian and Sullivan) surveys the main options.

## Relation to Active Inference
There is no direct relation. Active Inference models agents with generative models that have temporal depth, in which past, present and expected future states are represented, and this modelling is neutral between theories of time. Whether the future is real is a metaphysical claim that such models neither assume nor test. The topic bears on the vault's themes only through the question of how experienced time relates to the structure of time (see [[specious_present]]).
