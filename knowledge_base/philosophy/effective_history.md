# Effective History

## Overview
Effective history, in German *Wirkungsgeschichte*, is a central concept in Hans-Georg Gadamer's philosophical hermeneutics, set out in *Truth and Method* (1960). It names the way in which a text, work or event has been taken up and has had effects across history, and, more fundamentally, the way an interpreter's own understanding is itself shaped by that history. The related term effective-historical consciousness (*wirkungsgeschichtliches Bewusstsein*) names awareness of that fact. Both express what is often called the historicity of understanding: no one understands from a position outside of history.

## Main claims
In the account given by the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, the argument runs as follows.
- **Understanding is an effect of history.** Understanding always occurs against the background of our prior involvement and therefore on the basis of our history. Hermeneutical consciousness is the mode of being that is conscious of its own historical being-effected.
- **Prejudgements are conditions of understanding.** Gadamer rehabilitates "prejudice" (*Vorurteil*), meaning a pre-judgement, as something that opens up what is to be understood, although it can sometimes distort. Prejudgements can themselves be brought into question in the course of understanding. See [[hermeneutic_circle]].
- **Horizons.** The hermeneutical situation is also described by the phenomenological concept of horizon: interpretation occurs from within a horizon fixed by our historical situatedness. The horizon is not static, and in an encounter with another it is open to change.
- **Fusion of horizons.** Understanding is a process in which a common horizon is established through dialogue between what is familiar and what is alien, so that neither is left unaffected. The process is ongoing and never reaches final completion.
- **Authority and tradition.** One consequence is a positive evaluation of tradition as a legitimate source of knowledge. Jürgen Habermas read this as ideological conservatism, a criticism that bears on the relation between hermeneutics and [[critical_theory]], whereas Gadamer saw it as a corrective to the Enlightenment's over-reaction against authority.

The idea draws on Heidegger's analysis of understanding as part of human existence (see [[heideggerian_ontology]]) and is presented more generally in [[hermeneutics]] and [[hermeneutic_phenomenology]].

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is loose and structural. Predictive accounts hold that what is perceived depends on prior expectations that are revised by what is encountered, and Gadamer's prejudgements, which both condition and are revised by understanding, play a role comparable to priors. This is an analogy: Gadamer's claim concerns the historical and linguistic situatedness of interpreters, and no formal result links it to the free-energy framework.
