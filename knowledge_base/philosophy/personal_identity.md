# Personal Identity

## Overview

Personal identity, in the metaphysical sense used here, is the problem of persistence: under what conditions is a person existing at one time numerically the same as a person existing at another. It is distinct from the psychological question of how people form a sense of who they are, which belongs to developmental and social psychology and is linked from [[cognitive_development]].

## Main accounts

- **Psychological continuity (Locke, Parfit)**: a later person is the same if connected to the earlier one by overlapping chains of memory, intention, belief and character. Locke's memory criterion faces the circularity objection that memory presupposes identity; Parfit's "quasi-memory" and continuity chains were designed to avoid it.
- **Bodily or biological continuity (animalism)**: we are organisms, and persistence follows the continuity of life processes, whatever happens to psychology.
- **Soul or simple view**: identity is a further fact not reducible to either physical or psychological relations.
- **Narrative views**: a person is constituted by the self-interpreting story they tell, which ties identity to agency and social recognition.
- **Reductionism and its consequence**: Parfit argued that if identity consists in continuity relations that can hold to degrees, then identity is not what matters in survival; continuity is.

## Test cases

Fission (one brain divided into two continuers), teletransportation, gradual neural replacement and severe amnesia are used as thought experiments. They separate the criteria, since each account gives a different verdict, and they show that ordinary judgments assume the criteria always coincide. None of these cases is settled empirically; clinical findings about memory loss and dissociation inform intuitions but do not decide between theories.

## Role in Active Inference

Active Inference offers a model of the self as a hierarchical inference: the agent maintains a generative model of its own body, goals and history, and "self-modelling" accounts, for example those linking interoceptive inference to a minimal bodily self, treat the self as inferred rather than given. This favours a continuity view in which the self is a pattern maintained by ongoing prediction, in the neighbourhood of [[enactivism]] and autopoietic accounts. The framework does not by itself adjudicate between psychological and biological criteria, and whether a Markov-blanket boundary is the right unit of a person is contested. Identification of personal identity with a persisting blanket is an interpretive proposal, not a result.
