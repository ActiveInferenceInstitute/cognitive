# Hermeneutics

## Overview
Hermeneutics is the study of interpretation. It began as a set of methods for reading authoritative texts, notably scripture, law and classical literature, and was widened in the nineteenth century into a general theory of understanding in the human sciences. In the twentieth century it became a branch of philosophy that treats understanding not only as a method but as a basic feature of human existence.

## Main developments
- **Schleiermacher** framed interpretation as a general art, requiring both grammatical analysis and the reconstruction of an author's individual perspective.
- **Dilthey** distinguished understanding (*Verstehen*) of meaningful human expression from causal explanation of nature, and grounded the human sciences in the former.
- **Heidegger** treated understanding as a mode of being: an agent always interprets from within a prior orientation toward the world.
- **Gadamer** (1960) argued in *Truth and Method* that understanding always starts from prejudgements, that these are conditions of understanding as well as potential distortions, and that interpretation is a fusion of horizons between interpreter and tradition.
- **Ricoeur** combined hermeneutics with structuralist and psychoanalytic approaches to meaning.

The central figure is the **hermeneutic circle**: a whole text is understood through its parts, and the parts through the whole, so that interpretation is iterative and revisable rather than a one-way derivation from given data.

## Relation to Active Inference
The parallel to predictive processing is structural and loose. In both, what is perceived depends on prior expectations, which are revised in light of what is encountered; Gadamer's prejudgements play a role similar to priors, and the circle resembles iterated belief updating between top-down and bottom-up information. Some authors have drawn this comparison explicitly, but it is an analogy between a philosophical description and a statistical one, and hermeneutic thinkers have generally resisted formal and naturalistic treatments of understanding. The shared point is the rejection of a theory-free given in perception (see [[theory_ladenness]]).
