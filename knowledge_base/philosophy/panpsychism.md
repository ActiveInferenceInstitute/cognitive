# Panpsychism

## Overview
Panpsychism is the thesis that mind, or some form of experience, is a fundamental and ubiquitous feature of the physical world, rather than something that appears only in complex nervous systems. It has a long history, including versions in Spinoza, Leibniz and Whitehead, and has been revived in contemporary philosophy of mind as a response to the [[hard_problem]] of consciousness.

## Forms
- **Constitutive panpsychism (micropsychism).** The basic physical constituents have simple experiential properties, and the experience of larger systems is somehow built from them.
- **Cosmopsychism.** The universe as a whole is the primary subject, and individual minds are derivative.
- **Panprotopsychism.** The fundamental entities have properties that are not themselves experiential but can give rise to experience when suitably combined. It is closely related to Russellian monism and to [[neutral_monism]].

## Motivations and objections
A common argument starts from the observation that physics describes what matter does, not what it intrinsically is, and proposes that the intrinsic nature of matter is experiential. Strawson (2006) argued that physicalism, properly understood, implies panpsychism. A second motivation is continuity: if experience cannot emerge from wholly non-experiential parts, it must be present at the base. The principal objection is the combination problem, the difficulty of explaining how many simple subjects or experiences yield a single unified one. Critics also point to the lack of empirical tests, and some argue that it relocates the explanatory problem instead of solving it. Defenders respond that every view of consciousness faces comparable difficulties (Goff, 2019).

## Relation to Active Inference
The free energy principle applies to any system with a Markov blanket, from cells to organisms, and some commentators have noted that this wide scope resembles the breadth of panpsychism. The principle itself is a claim about the dynamics of self-organising systems, and neither entails nor asserts that such systems have experience. Separately, [[integrated_information]] theory is sometimes described as panpsychist in its implications, but it is a different research programme from Active Inference. The connection to this vault is therefore thematic, not a derivation.
