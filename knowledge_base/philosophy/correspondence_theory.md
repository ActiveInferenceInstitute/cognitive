# Correspondence Theory

## Overview
The correspondence theory holds that a belief or statement is true when it corresponds to a fact, that is, when things are as it says they are. It is the oldest and most intuitive theory of truth, traced to Plato and Aristotle, whose remark that to say of what is that it is, and of what is not that it is not, is true, is the usual reference point. Russell (1912) gave a developed early version in which a belief is true when there is a corresponding complex of the objects it concerns.

## Development and objections
- **Tarski's semantic conception.** Tarski (1944) proposed that an adequate definition of truth must entail every instance of the schema "'snow is white' is true if and only if snow is white". This captures the correspondence intuition formally for specified languages, while remaining neutral on what facts are.
- **The correspondence relation.** Critics ask what exactly the relation between a sentence and a fact is, whether it is picturing, causal covariation or something else, and whether facts are anything more than true statements restated.
- **Epistemic access.** Because inquirers can compare beliefs only with other beliefs or experiences, never with reality unmediated, rivals such as [[coherence_theory]] and the [[pragmatism]] of Peirce, James and Dewey offer other accounts of truth, or of its tests.
- **Scope.** Ethical, mathematical and modal statements make a simple fact-matching picture harder to apply.

## Relation to Active Inference
The relation between generative models and the world is a live issue. A [[generative_model]] is not a copy of the environment; it is a structure that supports prediction and control, and the free energy objective rewards models that predict sensory data accurately while remaining simple. Realist readings treat a successful model as approximately corresponding to the causal structure of the world, in line with [[scientific_realism]]. Instrumentalist and enactive readings stress that the model is answerable to the agent's viability, not to a mirror relation. The framework itself does not settle the matter.
