# Concrescence

## Overview
Concrescence, from the Latin for growing together, is the term Alfred North Whitehead uses in *Process and Reality* (1929) for the process by which a single [[actual_occasions|actual occasion]] comes into being. An occasion does not begin as a finished thing; it is the process of unifying a multiplicity of data inherited from the antecedent world into one determinate whole. When the process is complete the occasion has attained what Whitehead calls satisfaction, and it then perishes as a subject and becomes a datum available to later occasions.

## How concrescence works
- **Many become one.** Whitehead's summary of the process is that the many become one and are increased by one: the many data of the past are synthesised into a new unity, which in turn becomes one more item among the many available to later occasions.
- **Feelings.** The synthesis is carried out through feelings, or prehensions, by which the occasion takes account of earlier occasions. Each feeling has an objective content, which is what is felt, and a subjective form, which is how it is felt. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy emphasises that concrescence is driven less by the objective content, what is felt, than by the subjective form, how it is felt, which has the character of a valuation.
- **Self-determination.** Because the subjective form is part of the process, an occasion is conditioned but not fully determined by its past. Whitehead writes that how an actual entity becomes constitutes what that entity is, and that actuality is the decision amid potentiality.
- **Subjective aim.** The process is directed toward a definite outcome, the occasion's subjective aim. In Whitehead's scheme the initial aim is supplied by the ground of order and originality that he calls God, but the occasion is finally responsible for the decision by which any such lure is admitted.

Concrescence is the inward side of an occasion's relatedness: because the occasion is constituted by its feelings of other occasions, its relations are internal (see [[internal_relations]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is an analogy at most. Both settings describe a system that incorporates what it receives into a unified, self-maintaining state, and one can compare the weighting of data by subjective form with precision-weighted belief updating. Whitehead's concrescence is a metaphysical account of how any occasion arises, with an experiential character ([[panexperientialism]]), while belief updating in Active Inference is a mathematical procedure on probabilistic models. Neither theory derives from the other.
