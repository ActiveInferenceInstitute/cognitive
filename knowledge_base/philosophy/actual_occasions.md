# Actual Occasions

## Overview
Actual occasions, also called actual entities, are the basic units of reality in the metaphysics Alfred North Whitehead set out in *Process and Reality* (1929). Instead of enduring material substances that persist through change, Whitehead takes the world to be made of momentary episodes of becoming, each of which comes into being, reaches a definite character, and then perishes, becoming a datum for later occasions. Ordinary objects such as stones, organisms and persons are then societies, or nexuses, of occasions with a pattern of inheritance among them. See [[process_philosophy]] for the wider tradition.

## Main features
- **Atomic becoming.** In the account given in the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Whitehead came to hold that the passage of nature is atomic: it is constituted by discrete occasions, not by a continuum of becoming. He wrote that there is a becoming of continuity but no continuity of becoming. An occasion is the limiting case of an event with a single member, and an event in the general sense is a nexus of occasions.
- **Experiential character.** Each occasion is described as a "drop" or "throb" of experience, a view Whitehead supports by reference to William James. This is the basis of the thesis called [[panexperientialism]], which is distinct from panpsychism because consciousness presupposes experience and not the reverse.
- **Internal relatedness.** An occasion is constituted by how it takes account of the antecedent world through what Whitehead calls prehensions or feelings. Its relations to earlier occasions are therefore internal to it (see [[internal_relations]]).
- **Two kinds of causation.** An occasion is a product of the efficient past and also, in Whitehead's phrase borrowed from Spinoza, *causa sui*: it partly determines how it takes that past into account. Its final cause is its subjective aim.
- **Eternal objects.** Occasions realise pure potentials, which Whitehead calls eternal objects, in their own definite way. He writes that actuality is the decision amid potentiality.

The internal process by which an occasion unifies the many data it inherits into one satisfied whole is treated in [[concrescence]].

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is thematic, not a derivation. Active Inference describes systems that persist by continual exchange across a Markov blanket, and a process reading of that picture, in which things are patterns of ongoing activity, resembles Whitehead's preference for becoming over substance. Whitehead's occasions are metaphysical primitives with an experiential character, and the free-energy formalism neither asserts nor needs them; its equations can be accepted by a substance ontologist as well. The comparison is an interpretive lens only.
