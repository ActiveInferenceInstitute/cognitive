# Paradigm Shifts

## Overview
A paradigm shift is a change in the fundamental concepts, methods and standards of a scientific field, a notion made prominent by Thomas Kuhn in The Structure of Scientific Revolutions (1962). Kuhn used "paradigm" for the exemplary achievements and shared commitments that guide a research community, and he argued that science alternates between long periods of normal science and rarer episodes of revolutionary change. The concept itself is covered in [[kuhn_paradigms]]; this page concerns the transitions.

## Kuhn's pattern
1. **Normal science.** Researchers solve puzzles within a paradigm, which they do not themselves question.
2. **Anomaly.** Results accumulate that the paradigm does not explain.
3. **Crisis.** Confidence in the paradigm falls and rival approaches proliferate.
4. **Revolution.** A new paradigm gains adherents and displaces the old.
5. **New normal science** begins under the new paradigm.

Kuhn held that paradigms before and after a revolution can be incommensurable, in that they differ in standards, problems and even the meanings of shared terms, so that comparison cannot be made by neutral rules alone. Examples commonly discussed include the shift from Ptolemaic to Copernican astronomy and from Newtonian mechanics to relativity.

## Criticism and alternatives
Popper's followers criticised Kuhn for apparently making theory choice depend on group psychology. Lakatos (1970) offered the methodology of scientific research programmes, in which a "hard core" is protected by auxiliary hypotheses and programmes are judged progressive or degenerating. Kuhn himself later qualified his earlier language, stressing that theory choice is guided by shared values such as accuracy and simplicity. Historians dispute how well the model fits particular episodes, and the term is now used loosely outside science.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is an informal analogy. A paradigm can be compared to a strong prior over models that is revised only when accumulated prediction error becomes large, a parallel sometimes drawn in discussions of scientific inference. Active Inference does not derive Kuhn's pattern, and the comparison is not tested.
