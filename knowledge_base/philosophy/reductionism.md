# Reductionism

## Overview
Reductionism is a family of views holding that complex wholes can be explained by, or are nothing over and above, their parts and the interactions among them. Philosophers usually separate ontological reductionism (a whole consists of nothing but its parts), methodological reductionism (the best research strategy is to study parts in isolation) and theory reduction (the laws of one science can be derived from those of a more basic one). The three can come apart: a person may accept the first and reject the others.

## Forms of reduction
- **Theory reduction.** Nagel (1961) modelled reduction as the logical derivation of a higher-level theory from a lower-level theory plus bridge principles linking their vocabularies. A standard objection is multiple realisability: one higher-level kind, such as a mental state or a gene, can be realised by different lower-level kinds, so bridge principles need not be one-to-one.
- **Explanatory reduction.** A system is explained by decomposing it into components and their operations, as when heredity is explained through the molecular behaviour of DNA. Critics note that the organisation of the components and the environment they sit in carry explanatory weight that a list of parts omits.
- **Anti-reductionist arguments.** Anderson (1972) argued that obeying fundamental laws does not imply that the behaviour of large aggregates can be reconstructed from those laws, because each level of complexity brings new regularities. This is the motivation for [[emergence]], in its [[weak_emergence]] and [[strong_emergence]] forms.

## Relation to Active Inference
The free energy principle is formulated for any system that can be partitioned by a Markov blanket, at any scale, from cells to organisms to groups. This makes it a scale-spanning principle rather than a reduction of one science to another: each level is described in its own terms by the dynamics of internal and blanket states. Whether that counts as reductionist is a matter of interpretation. Proponents often present it as a physics-grounded account of mind, while the multi-level structure is closer to a non-reductive, scale-relative picture. The link to the vault's themes is interpretive, not a derivation.
