# Foundationalism

## Overview
Foundationalism is the view that the structure of justified belief resembles a building: some beliefs are basic, justified without depending on other beliefs, and all other justified beliefs derive support from them. It is motivated by the regress argument. If every belief needed support from another, justification would either continue forever or loop in a circle, and foundationalists conclude that the chain must terminate in beliefs that stop the regress.

## Varieties and objections
- **Classical forms.** Descartes took basic beliefs to be indubitable truths grasped by reason, a [[rationalism]]-style foundation. Empiricists such as Locke and Hume located the base in sensory experience (see [[empiricism]]).
- **Modest foundationalism.** Contemporary versions, for example Chisholm (1977), allow basic beliefs to be fallible and defeasible, including perceptual and memory beliefs, and need not require certainty.
- **The given.** Sellars (1956) attacked the "myth of the given", arguing that experience cannot justify beliefs unless it already has conceptual content that is itself open to assessment, which undermines the idea of a non-inferential base.
- **Rival accounts.** [[coherence_theory]] replaces linear support with mutual support across a web, and [[pragmatism]] rejects the search for ultimate foundations in favour of fallible, revisable inquiry. Reliabilist views locate justification in the process producing the belief.

## Relation to Active Inference
Hierarchical predictive models have no layer that is justified independently of the others. Priors at higher levels shape how sensory signals are interpreted, and prediction errors from below revise those priors, so support runs in both directions. That is closer to a fallibilist, coherence-style picture. Sensory evidence nonetheless plays a distinguished role as the quantity the model must predict, which gives a limited analogue of a foundation. The correspondence is an analogy about structure, not a claim that the framework settles the epistemological debate.
