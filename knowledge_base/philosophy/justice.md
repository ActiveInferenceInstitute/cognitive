# Justice

## Overview
Justice is a central concept in moral and political philosophy, concerned with giving persons what they are due and with the fair ordering of institutions and relations. It is applied to individual character, to the distribution of resources, to punishment and to the basic structure of a society. Philosophers disagree on its content, its foundation and its relation to other values such as liberty, welfare and equality.

## Main strands
- **Classical accounts.** In Plato's *Republic*, justice in a person and a city is a harmony in which each part performs its proper function. Aristotle distinguished general justice, roughly lawfulness or complete virtue, from particular justice, which concerns fair shares (distributive) and rectification of wrongs (corrective) (see [[virtue_ethics]]).
- **Social contract tradition.** Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau and Kant treated just institutions as those that could be agreed by free persons. Rawls (1971) revived this approach, defining justice as fairness through the original position, and Hume had treated justice as an artificial virtue arising from convention and utility.
- **Alternatives to the ideal-theory approach.** Sen (2009) argued that identifying perfectly just institutions is neither necessary nor sufficient for reducing injustice, and proposed comparative reasoning about actual arrangements. MacIntyre (1988) argued that conceptions of justice are tied to rival traditions of rationality.
- **Domains.** Distributive justice concerns the allocation of benefits and burdens (see [[distributive_justice]]), retributive justice concerns punishment (see [[retributive_justice]]), and procedural justice concerns the fairness of processes. Other proposed domains include restorative and global justice.
- **Critiques.** Marxist writers have argued that justice is a notion relative to a mode of production, and feminist writers, in the care tradition, that justice-centred theory neglects relationships of dependence (see [[care_ethics]]). Miller's encyclopedia entry surveys the field.

## Relation to Active Inference
The concept has no direct link with Active Inference. Empirical work on fairness norms, such as responses in ultimatum games, concerns people's sense of fairness, not what justice requires, and there is no derivation of normative principles from the free energy principle.
