# Tiantai Buddhism

## Overview
Tiantai is a school of Chinese Mahayana Buddhism that takes the Lotus Sutra as its central scripture and is associated above all with Zhiyi. Brook Ziporyn's Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry describes its most fundamental renovation of Buddhism as the move from a Two Truths model to a Three Truths model. In most Mahayana Buddhism the Two Truths, conventional and ultimate, serve as an epistemological and pedagogical heuristic. In Tiantai the Three Truths are taken to follow necessarily from any proposed determinate thing, and so to apply to every ontological, epistemological and ethical entity alike. The school was later transmitted to Japan, where it is known as Tendai.

## The three truths
The three truths are emptiness, provisional positing, and the middle or center (*kong, jia, zhong*). They can be summarised by the claim that no entity can be either the same as or different from any other. This "neither-same-nor-different" relation is not confined to ultimate truth but is applied to conventional entities too, so that each determinate thing is, in the entry's words, the totality of all other possible things "as this thing". The non-sameness means that the characteristics of all other things are in some sense discoverable in each thing. Ziporyn illustrates it with the ordinary experience of finding a small white marble that turns out to be the tip of the horn of an enormous monster. The same object is round, then sharp, and what it was is reinterpreted by the whole it belongs to.

## Characteristic doctrines
- **Self-recontextualization.** Teachings are read as provisional devices (*upaya*) that are reinterpreted, and not abandoned, once a later teaching is reached.
- **One moment, three thousand worlds.** The contemplation of mind (*guanxin*), in Zhiyi's formulation *yinian sanqian*, finds three thousand worlds in a single moment of experience. The number is derived by multiplying ten realms by ten realms, ten "suchnesses" and three worlds, and is a way of saying "everything" in a form fit for meditation.
- **Evil is inherent in Buddhahood.** Tiantai holds that evil is inherently included in Buddhahood, since delusion and awakening are mutually included, a claim developed by Zhanran, who also defended the thesis that insentient beings have Buddha-nature.

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is an analogy. The Tiantai thesis that each thing contains the characteristics of all others, and that a thing's identity depends on the context in which it is read, can be compared with the holism of a generative model, in which what an observation means depends on the model it is placed in (compare [[internal_relations]]). Tiantai is a soteriological doctrine and makes no claim about inference or the brain, and nothing in the formal framework requires it.
