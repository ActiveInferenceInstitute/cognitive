# Dasein

## Overview
*Dasein* is the term Martin Heidegger uses in *Being and Time* (1927) for the entity that each of us is, the being for whom its own being is an issue. The German word ordinarily means "existence" or "presence" (literally "being-there"). Heidegger employs it deliberately in place of "subject", "consciousness" or "human being", to avoid carrying over assumptions about a detached mind confronting objects, and he uses it to prepare the question of the meaning of being.

## Main features
- **Existence.** Dasein's being is *existence*, understood as a way of relating to its own possibilities, not a fixed property set. It is "in each case mine" (*Jemeinigkeit*), so the proper way to describe it is through first-person possibilities and not through a list of attributes.
- **Existentialia.** The structures of Dasein, called existentials, include being-in-the-world, being-with others, understanding, attunement or mood (*Befindlichkeit*), discourse and falling (see [[being_in_the_world]]).
- **Care.** Heidegger names the unitary structure of these features care (*Sorge*): being ahead of oneself, already in a world, and alongside entities encountered.
- **Facticity and temporality.** Dasein is thrown into a situation it did not choose (see [[facticity]]), projects itself on possibilities, and is finite, a theme developed in [[being_toward_death]]. Temporality is presented as the meaning of its being.
- **Authenticity.** Dasein can exist in a way that is owned or not owned (see [[authenticity]]).

Interpretations vary. Some read the work as a philosophical anthropology, others as fundamental ontology, and Heidegger himself later qualified the approach. Inwood (1999) offers a lexicon of the terminology, and the Stanford Encyclopedia entry (Wrathall) surveys the work.
