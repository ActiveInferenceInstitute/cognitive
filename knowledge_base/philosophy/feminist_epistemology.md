# Feminist Epistemology

## Overview
Feminist epistemology is the study of how gender and other social positions affect knowledge, inquiry and justification, and of how knowledge practices could be reformed. It grew from criticism of sciences that excluded women or reflected male-centred assumptions, and it asks who counts as a knower, what is studied, and how evidence and objectivity should be understood. It is not a single doctrine, and its proponents disagree with one another.

## Main approaches
- **Feminist empiricism.** Biased science can be corrected by stricter application of scientific norms. Longino (1990) argued that objectivity is a social achievement, resting on critical discussion in a community with diverse perspectives and shared standards, since background assumptions guide how data count as evidence (see [[theory_ladenness]]).
- **Standpoint theory.** Harding (1991) and Hartsock hold that the social position of marginalised groups can give epistemic advantages, because those groups must understand both their own situation and that of the dominant group. The thesis is that standpoints are achieved through struggle, not automatically held, and critics question whether any single standpoint exists.
- **Situated knowledges.** Haraway (1988) rejected the view from nowhere and also naive relativism, arguing for partial, accountable perspectives and objectivity as situated.
- **Postmodern strands.** Some authors stress the plurality of knowers and the constructed nature of categories (see [[constructivism]], [[relativism]]), which others criticise as eroding the ground for feminist critique itself.
- **Related work.** The treatment of credibility and interpretive resources developed in [[epistemic_justice]].

## Relation to Active Inference
There is no direct link. A loose analogy is that inference is perspective-dependent: an agent's priors and the observations it can sample reflect its situation, so differently placed agents may arrive at different well-supported models. That is a general feature of Bayesian reasoning and does not imply any of the feminist positions or show what makes a perspective epistemically privileged.
