# Religious Experience

## Overview
Religious experience is a broad term for experiences that subjects interpret as involving the sacred, the divine or an ultimate reality. They include a sense of presence, visions, awe, a sense of union (mystical experience) and conversion. Philosophers and psychologists treat the experiences, which are well documented, separately from the question of whether the beliefs based on them are true.

## Classic accounts
- **James** (1902) studied reports in The Varieties of Religious Experience, characterising mystical states by ineffability, a noetic quality (a sense of insight), transiency and passivity. He argued that the value of such experiences should be assessed by their fruits as well as their origins.
- **Otto** (1917) described the numinous as an experience of a "wholly other" that is both awe-inspiring and fascinating.
- **Alston** (1991) argued in Perceiving God that experiences of God can be treated as a kind of perception, giving prima facie justification for beliefs about God, in the way that sense perception does for beliefs about physical objects. Swinburne's principle of credulity is a related proposal.

## Debates
Critics challenge the perceptual analogy on the ground that religious experiences lack the public checks, such as independent corroboration, available to ordinary perception, and that experiences differ across traditions, a point debated in connection with [[religious_exclusivism]] and [[religious_pluralism]]. Naturalistic explanations appeal to psychological and neural processes, though identifying a mechanism does not by itself show an experience to be illusory, since the same is true of ordinary perception.

## Empirical research
Early claims that weak magnetic fields applied to the temporal lobes produce a sensed presence were not replicated in a blinded study (Granqvist et al., 2005), which pointed to suggestibility. Controlled studies with psilocybin found that many participants reported mystical-type experiences that they rated as personally and spiritually significant (Griffiths et al., 2006). Such work shows that the experiences can be induced and measured, and leaves their interpretation open.

## Relation to Active Inference
Carhart-Harris and Friston (2019) proposed the REBUS model, in which psychedelics relax the precision of high-level priors so that bottom-up signals have greater influence. They suggested this could account for features of psychedelic and mystical experience. The model is an active research programme with some supporting data and continuing debate over its details.
