# Coloniality of Power

## Overview
"Coloniality of power" is the central concept of the Peruvian sociologist Aníbal Quijano (1930-2018), put forward in essays published around 2000 and widely taken up in Latin American and decolonial thought. Quijano's abstract of the International Sociology article states that globalisation is the culmination of a process that began with the constitution of America and of world capitalism as a Euro-centred colonial/modern world power. One of the foundations of that pattern of power was the social classification of the world's population on the basis of the idea of race, a mental construct that expresses the colonial experience and pervades the most important dimensions of world power, including its specific rationality, Eurocentrism.

## Main claims
- **Race as an organising axis.** Race, on this account, arose with the conquest of the Americas as a way of classifying people and legitimating domination. Its colonial origin is retained although it has outlasted the colonial administrations within which it formed.
- **Coloniality distinguished from colonialism.** Colonialism names a formal relation of political rule by one people over another. Coloniality names the pattern of power that continues after formal independence, in the structure of labour, authority, social classification and knowledge.
- **Labour and capital.** The model combines the racial axis with a second axis, the organisation of capital through control of labour and its insertion in the world market.
- **Eurocentrism.** The rationality associated with this pattern of power treats European experience as the universal norm and shapes what counts as knowledge. This theme was developed by later writers into claims about the coloniality of knowledge and of being, for instance in the work of Walter Mignolo and Nelson Maldonado-Torres.

## Reception
The concept is a cornerstone of the modernity/coloniality research programme and of the discussion of decolonising knowledge. It has been applied beyond Latin America, and critics and defenders debate how far a single global axis can account for local histories. This page describes the main thesis only; the literature is large and the concept is used in varied ways.

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is slight. In an Active Inference reading, shared priors shaped by a culture's categories could be said to condition what members of that culture treat as evidence, which parallels the claim that racial classification shapes social perception and knowledge. This is an analogy for discussion and not a finding, and Quijano's account is a historical and sociological analysis that does not use the framework.
