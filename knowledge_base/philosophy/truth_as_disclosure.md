# Truth as Disclosure

## Overview
In *Being and Time* (1927), Martin Heidegger develops an account of truth that shapes much of his later thought (see [[heideggerian_ontology]]). In Mark Wrathall's Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy article it has two parts. The Historical Thesis says that early Greek thinkers such as Parmenides and Heraclitus understood truth as unconcealment, the bringing of things out of concealment, and that the Greek word *aletheia*, read as a privative form meaning "un-hidden", reflects this. The Thematic Thesis says that, whatever the Greeks understood, the structure of truth in general is best understood as unconcealment.

## Four kinds of truth
Heidegger identifies at least four kinds of truth that take part in the logic of unconcealment.
1. **Propositional truth.** An assertion or belief is true when it "points out" or uncovers an entity in a state of affairs.
2. **Ontic truth.** An entity can be uncovered as what it is only because we have well-articulated practices for encountering it, that is, we know how to cope with it (compare [[being_in_the_world]]).
3. **Ontological truth.** The primordial truth about entities, because it discloses in advance what is essential about their being.
4. **Truth as clearing.** In the later work, the unconcealment of historical worlds or understandings of being, such as the medieval Christian, modern or technological "style of being".

Propositional truth is thus one species of a broader genus of unconcealment, and Heidegger tends to use "truth" and "unconcealment" interchangeably.

## Correspondence and relativity
Heidegger rejects theories on which correspondence is a relation between a mental representation and a state of affairs. A disclosive stance "corresponds" with the world when it lets the state of affairs show itself as it is in itself. Wrathall reports that commentators differ over whether Heidegger keeps a redefined notion of correspondence or favours a more naive view of truth as correctness (compare [[correspondence_theory]]), but he does not deny that assertions are true by agreeing with the world. Because propositional truths can be expressed only in light of an ontological disclosure, Heidegger questions the universality of timeless propositional truths. He insists this does not lead to relativism about entities, because once entities have been uncovered they show themselves as what they already were.

## Criticism
Friedlander criticised the historical thesis and the etymology of *aletheia*, and both he and Heidegger later moderated their positions. Tugendhat objected that Heidegger loses the core quality of truth, that it is opposed to falsehood, and illegitimately transfers the normative status of truth to the unconcealment of being. Heidegger eventually conceded that it is misleading to call every type of unconcealment "truth".

## Relation to Active Inference
The relation is an interpretive analogy. The claim that entities show up as what they are only against a background of practices and skills resembles the view that an agent's generative model constitutes the world as it is encountered (see [[being_in_the_world]]). Active Inference represents truth as accuracy of a model with respect to hidden states, and so is not committed to Heidegger's rejection of representational correspondence. Compatibility is therefore a matter of reading.
