# Mathematical Platonism

## Overview
Mathematical platonism is the position that mathematical objects such as numbers, sets and functions exist, that they are abstract, meaning non-spatial, non-temporal and causally inert, and that they exist independently of human thought and language. Mathematical statements on this view are literally true or false in virtue of how these objects are. Defenders include Frege and Gödel; the label comes from the analogy with Plato's forms.

## Arguments for
- **Semantic argument.** Mathematical sentences such as "there are infinitely many primes" appear to be about objects and to be true, so taking them at face value commits one to the objects.
- **Indispensability argument.** Associated with Quine and Putnam, it holds that we should believe in the entities our best scientific theories quantify over, and these theories use mathematics ineliminably.
- **Objectivity.** Mathematicians treat questions such as the continuum hypothesis as having answers not settled by convention.

## Objections and alternatives
Benacerraf (1973) posed an epistemological challenge: if mathematical objects are causally inert, it is unclear how humans could have knowledge of them. Platonists respond in various ways, for instance by appealing to rational intuition or to a plenitude of consistent mathematical structures. Alternatives include nominalism, which denies that abstract objects exist (Field, 1980, attempted to reconstruct science without numbers), fictionalism, formalism, intuitionism and constructivism (see [[mathematical_constructivism]]), and structuralism, which takes mathematics to describe structures rather than individual objects.

## Relation to Active Inference
There is no derivation linking platonism and Active Inference. The two meet only in the background question of what the mathematics of the framework describes. A platonist may regard the probability calculus as describing abstract structure; an instrumentalist treats it as a modelling language whose status is independent of metaphysics. The framework is neutral between these readings, and neither is part of its empirical content.
