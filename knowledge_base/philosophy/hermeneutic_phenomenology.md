# Hermeneutic Phenomenology

## Overview
Hermeneutic phenomenology combines the phenomenological aim of describing experience as it shows itself with the hermeneutic claim that all understanding is interpretive. Heidegger introduced the approach in Being and Time (1927), where he argued that phenomenology of everyday existence cannot be a neutral description by a detached observer, because the interpreter already belongs to the world interpreted. This departs from Husserl's emphasis on pure, presuppositionless description in [[phenomenology]].

## Key ideas
- **Pre-understanding**: interpretation always starts from a background of practices, expectations and language that cannot be wholly bracketed. Heidegger called these fore-structures, and Gadamer (1960) argued that prejudgements (Vorurteile) are conditions of understanding, though they can be tested and revised.
- **Hermeneutic circle**: the whole is understood through its parts and the parts through the whole, so understanding is a movement between them rather than a linear derivation (see [[hermeneutic_circle]]).
- **Fusion of horizons**: Gadamer described understanding a text or another person as a meeting of the interpreter's horizon with that of the other, producing a changed shared understanding.
- **Distanciation and appropriation**: Ricoeur (1981) held that written texts and meaningful action gain independence from their authors, so interpretation requires explanation as well as understanding, and ends in appropriation by the reader.

George's Stanford Encyclopedia entry gives an overview of the tradition. Dilthey's earlier work on understanding in the human sciences is an important antecedent.

## Use in research
Van Manen (1990) developed a method of hermeneutic phenomenological research in education and health, which gathers descriptions of lived experience and interprets themes through writing and reflection. It is widely used in qualitative research, and its critics question the reproducibility and the degree to which researcher interpretation shapes the findings.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is a loose analogy and a matter of ongoing discussion. The role of prior understanding in shaping what is perceived resembles the role of priors in predictive processing, and the circle resembles iterative belief revision. Hermeneutic authors do not use probabilistic language, and mapping Vorurteile onto Bayesian priors involves interpretive licence.
