# Eternalism

## Overview
Eternalism is the view that all times, and the events at them, exist on a par. Dinosaurs, the present moment and future events are equally real, and the privileged status of the present is an indexical matter, like the privileged status of here. Eternalism is typically combined with the B-theory of time, on which only tenseless relations of earlier and later are objective, and it underlies the picture of a four-dimensional block universe (see [[philosophy_of_time]]).

## Motivations
- **Relativity.** In special relativity, simultaneity is relative to a reference frame, so there is no frame-independent division of events into past, present and future. Putnam (1967) and others argued that this makes it natural to treat all events as equally real, though the argument is contested.
- **Truth about the past and future.** If past and future events exist, statements about them have straightforward truthmakers.
- **Spacetime physics.** Physical theories usually describe the world as a four-dimensional manifold with events located in it.

Eternalism is compatible with different views of persistence. Perdurantists, such as Sider (2001), take objects to have temporal parts, while endurantists hold that objects are wholly present at each time at which they exist.

## Objections
Critics say eternalism cannot account for the apparent passage of time, the sense that the future is open, or the special significance of the present. Linked worries concern fatalism and [[free_will]]: if the future already exists, one's choices might seem pointless. Eternalists respond that existence in the block does not remove the causal dependence of later events on earlier ones and our choices, and that compatibility with [[determinism]] is a separate question, as a block universe can contain indeterministic laws.

## Relation to Active Inference
Neither this view nor [[presentism]] is entailed by the framework. There is a formal parallel in its path-integral formulation, in which dynamics are described by functionals over whole trajectories and the expected free energy scores entire policies into the future (see [[path_integral]]). That describes how a model evaluates paths, and is not a claim that the future exists. Agents in these models infer and plan in time without any metaphysical commitment about its nature.
