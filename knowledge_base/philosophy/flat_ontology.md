# Flat Ontology

## Overview
A flat ontology is an ontology that refuses to arrange what exists into levels of greater or lesser reality. The phrase gained wide currency through DeLanda's *Intensive Science and Virtual Philosophy* (2002) and is now used in speculative realism, object-oriented philosophy and assemblage theory. Its two best-known formulations are Manuel DeLanda's proposal of an ontology made exclusively of unique, singular individuals that differ in spatiotemporal scale but not in ontological status, and Levi Bryant's broader use of the term in *The Democracy of Objects* (2011). In both, a person, a city and a nation are all individuals of the same ontological standing, although they differ in size and in the other entities they are composed of.

## Bryant's four theses
Bryant describes flat ontology as a complex concept that bundles together several theses.
- **No ontology of transcendence or presence.** Flat ontology rejects any ontology that privileges one sort of entity as the origin of all others and as fully present to itself.
- **The world does not exist.** This is the claim that there is no super-object that gathers all other objects into a single unity.
- **No privileged subject-object relation.** Following Graham Harman, the relation between human and world is not treated as different in kind from relations between other objects, nor as implicitly included in every such relation. Humans have unique powers, but they are one type of object among many.
- **Equal ontological footing.** No entity, whether artificial or natural, symbolic or physical, has greater ontological dignity than any other. Bryant adds that this does not mean all objects have equal influence: some objects affect their collectives more than others, but it does not follow that they are more real.

Bryant states the strategic aim as reducing an exclusive focus on the human, the subjective and the cultural in social and political theory, in order to cultivate attention to nonhuman actors such as natural entities and technologies, without excluding the former.

## Contrasts and open questions
Flat ontology is contrasted with hierarchical pictures in which higher levels such as society, mind or the world as a whole have a special status, and with reductionist pictures in which only the smallest parts are fully real (see [[reductionism]]). It is also distinct from mere [[materialism]], since it counts symbolic and social entities among the individuals. Natural questions for the view are whether a position that denies hierarchy can still account for emergence and for differences in explanatory power between scales, and whether treating all entities as equally real obscures the difference between natural and conventional ones. Bryant and DeLanda themselves disagree on which entities are metaphysical primitives, a contrast examined by Kleinherenbrink (2020).

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is loose. Treatments of the free-energy principle that stack nested Markov blankets describe a hierarchy of scales, from cells to organisms to groups, which may be read in a flat manner, as one kind of structure repeated at every scale, or in a hierarchical one in which higher levels constrain lower ones (see [[downward_causation]]). The formalism does not decide between these readings, and flat ontology plays no role in the framework.
