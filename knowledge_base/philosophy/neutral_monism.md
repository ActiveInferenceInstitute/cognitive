# Neutral Monism

## Overview
Neutral monism holds that reality is made of one kind of basic entity that is in itself neither mental nor physical. Minds and bodies are then different arrangements, or different ways of grouping, of the same neutral elements. The view is monist, like [[materialism]], but unlike materialism and idealism it does not give priority to either side of the mind-body divide, and so avoids the interaction problem facing [[dualism]].

## History and variants
Spinoza's doctrine that thought and extension are attributes of a single substance is an important precursor, often described as dual-aspect monism. The classical neutral monists were William James, who in 1904 argued that "pure experience" is the single stuff of which both consciousness and objects are made, Ernst Mach, who analysed the world into elements of sensation, and Bertrand Russell, who in the 1910s and 1920s held that mind and matter are logical constructions from events. Russell's later view, in which physics reveals only structure and the intrinsic nature of matter is left open, is distinct and is usually called Russellian monism. It is the form that links most closely to [[panpsychism]].

Objections concern the characterisation of the neutral element, which risks being either mental or physical in disguise, and the details of the constructions that are supposed to yield both kinds of entity.

## Relation to Active Inference
Friston, Wiese and Hobson (2020) proposed a dual-aspect position they called Markovian monism: the same system can be described extrinsically, in terms of its physical states and dynamics, and intrinsically, in terms of the beliefs encoded by its internal states about external states, with the Markov blanket structure licensing both descriptions. The proposal draws on the Spinozist and Jamesian tradition. It is a philosophical interpretation advanced by some researchers within the [[free_energy_principle]] literature, not a consequence of the mathematics, and it remains contested both on philosophical grounds and as to whether it addresses the question of why experience accompanies certain dynamics at all.
