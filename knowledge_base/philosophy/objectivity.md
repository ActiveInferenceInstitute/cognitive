# Objectivity

## Overview
Objectivity is a property attributed to claims, methods, observers and results when they are held to be independent of some kind of subjective influence. The word carries several distinct senses, and much disagreement arises from running them together. Reiss and Sprenger (2020) distinguish objectivity as faithfulness to facts, as freedom from personal bias, as a feature of processes, and as the outcome of social and value-related practices.

## Senses
- **Metaphysical.** Objects and facts exist independently of any mind. This is the sense at stake in [[scientific_realism]].
- **Epistemic.** A claim is objective to the extent it is supported by evidence available to any competent inquirer, or captures what Nagel (1986) called a view from nowhere, a standpoint detached from a particular position.
- **Procedural.** Objectivity belongs to methods such as blinding, replication and mechanical measurement that limit the role of individual judgement.
- **Intersubjective.** Longino (1990) located objectivity in the critical processes of a scientific community, which can expose and correct individual bias.

## History and critique
Daston and Galison (2007) argue that scientific objectivity has a history: the nineteenth-century ideal of "mechanical objectivity", the restraint of the observer's will, replaced earlier ideals of truth to nature and was later supplemented by trained judgement. Feminist and social epistemologists have criticised the idea of a wholly detached standpoint, with some proposing standpoint theory and others holding that objectivity remains a worthwhile regulative ideal reached through diverse and critical communities (see [[feminist_epistemology]], [[social_epistemology]]). Defenders of the traditional view reply that value-ladenness in the choice of problems need not undermine the objectivity of results.

## Relation to Active Inference
The relation is conceptual and partial. In Active Inference an agent has only a perspectival generative model of its environment, with hidden causes inferred and never directly accessed, which is compatible with realist and with constructivist readings. Objectivity in the procedural sense reappears in how scientists compare models by evidence, a practice external to the framework.
