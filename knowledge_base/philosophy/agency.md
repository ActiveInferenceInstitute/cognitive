# Agency

## Overview
Agency is the capacity of an entity to act, and in a narrower psychological sense the experience of controlling one's own movements and through them events in the world. The term covers several questions that are often run together: what it takes to be an agent at all, what distinguishes an agent's actions from events that merely occur in it, and how agents come to feel that they are the authors of what they do.

## Philosophical and psychological distinctions
- **Standard conception.** Many philosophers treat agency as the capacity to act intentionally, where an action is explained by the agent's reasons (see [[action_theory]]). Some accounts, such as those of Frankfurt and Velleman, add reflective or self-governing capacities to separate full agency from mere goal-directed activity.
- **Sense of agency versus sense of ownership.** Gallagher (2000) distinguished the sense of agency, the feeling that I am the one causing or generating an action, from the sense of ownership, the feeling that a movement or body part is mine. The two can dissociate: in involuntary movement ownership may remain while agency is absent. See [[body_ownership]].
- **Comparator accounts.** A widely discussed model proposes that agency is experienced when sensory consequences predicted from an efference copy of the motor command match actual feedback (see [[efference_copy]] and [[corollary_discharge]]). Disorders of this process have been linked by some researchers to experiences of passivity in schizophrenia, a hypothesis still debated.
- **Collective and non-human agency.** Debates also ask whether groups, organisms or artificial systems can be agents.

## Relation to Active Inference
Active inference supplies an account in which action is generated by predictions and in which the reliability of sensory consequences is weighted by precision. On this account a sense of agency can be treated as inference about the causes of one's own sensations, and attenuated processing of self-generated input follows from reduced sensory precision during movement. This is an active modelling programme with supporting simulations and some empirical studies, not a settled explanation. It addresses the sense of agency more directly than the metaphysical question of what agency is.
