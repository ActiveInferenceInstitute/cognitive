# Critical Pedagogy

## Overview
Critical pedagogy is an approach to education, developed above all by the Brazilian educator Paulo Freire (1921-1997), that regards teaching and learning as inseparable from questions of power and that aims to help people understand and change oppressive social conditions. The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes Freire as one of the most influential philosophers of education of the twentieth century, whose goal was to eradicate illiteracy among people from formerly colonised countries and continents and whose ideas were rooted in the social and political realities of the descendants of former slaves. His best-known work, *Pedagogy of the Oppressed*, was written in the first years of his exile from Brazil, published in 1968 and translated into English in 1970.

## Main ideas
- **The banking model.** Freire criticised teachers who see themselves as the sole possessors of knowledge and their students as empty receptacles into which knowledge is deposited. The IEP presents this as a vertical relationship from teacher to student, one that Freire compared to colonisation and described as treating students as things, and that fails to encourage students to think critically for themselves.
- **Problem-posing education.** In place of banking, critical pedagogy uses dialogue among equals in a democratic setting. The teacher problematises the matter under discussion, so that ready-made answers are not available, and adopts a humble and open attitude. The relationship becomes horizontal, with teacher and students learning from each other. Freire's cultural circles replaced teachers with "coordinators" and students with "participants".
- **Conscientização.** Usually rendered as critical consciousness, this is the process of becoming aware of social and political contradictions and then acting against the oppressive elements of one's conditions. It typically begins with a person becoming aware of her own social, political and economic context, gender, class, sexuality and race, and of her agency to choose and create her reality.
- **Internalised oppression.** Freire argued that oppression is also internal: peasants who had taken a plantation could not guard their landowner because they had internalised him. Freedom therefore requires more than the removal of institutional obstacles.
- **Humanisation.** As a humanist, Freire held that it is every person's ontological vocation to become more human, and that both oppressor and oppressed are diminished when their relations are dehumanising.

Freire's influences, as the IEP lists them, include Marx, existentialism and the critical theory of Erich Fromm.

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is distant. Critical pedagogy is a normative and political theory of education. One can say that teaching is a process in which a learner's generative model is updated through dialogue, which resembles the active, inquiry-driven learning that Freire favours over deposit, but this is a loose analogy and is not a claim made by either field.
