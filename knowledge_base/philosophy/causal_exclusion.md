# Causal Exclusion

## Overview
Causal exclusion is the name for a family of arguments and principles about when more than one cause can be credited with an effect. Its main philosophical use is Jaegwon Kim's exclusion argument in the philosophy of mind. A second, unrelated use is the exclusion postulate of integrated information theory, which holds that only the maximal cause-effect structure counts as conscious.

## Kim's exclusion argument
Kim (1998, 2005) argued that non-reductive physicalism faces a problem. Mental properties are claimed to be distinct from physical properties and yet to cause behaviour. But if every physical event has a sufficient physical cause (causal closure), a distinct mental cause would be superfluous, and it would have to be a case of systematic overdetermination, which is implausible. Kim concluded that mental properties are either reducible to physical ones or causally inert. This argument is not specific to the mind: it applies to any higher-level property that supervenes on a lower level, and so it bears on [[strong_emergence]] and [[downward_causation]].

Responses include:
- **Reductive physicalism**, accepting reduction and so removing the problem.
- **Interventionist accounts of causation** (associated with Woodward), on which higher-level variables can be causally relevant if manipulating them changes outcomes.
- **Rejecting causal closure** or accepting benign overdetermination.
- **Weak emergence**, where macro-level patterns are explanatory though derivable from micro-level dynamics (see [[weak_emergence]]).

## Exclusion in integrated information theory
Integrated information theory (IIT) posits an exclusion postulate: of overlapping candidate systems and spatial and temporal grains, only the one whose cause-effect structure is maximally irreducible exists as a conscious entity (Oizumi, Albantakis and Tononi, 2014). This resembles Kim's principle in forbidding double counting, though IIT's rule is a postulate about consciousness and not an argument about physicalism (see [[integrated_information]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
The issue is relevant to the framework's multi-scale claims. In the free energy framework, systems are individuated by Markov blankets nested across scales, and whether macroscale states have causal relevance beyond their components is the question exclusion raises. Interpretations differ, and the framework does not settle it.
