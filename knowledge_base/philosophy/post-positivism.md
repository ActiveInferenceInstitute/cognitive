# Post-Positivism

## Overview
Post-positivism is a label for positions that emerged in response to the failures of [[logical_positivism]]. Post-positivists generally keep the commitments that there is a reality independent of observers and that inquiry should be disciplined by evidence, but they drop the claims that knowledge rests on indubitable observational foundations or that meaningful statements must be verifiable. The term is used in philosophy of science and, in a looser sense, in social research methodology to describe a paradigm between strict empiricism and constructivism.

## Core commitments
- **Fallibilism.** Scientific claims are provisional. Evidence can lend support but cannot establish a theory with certainty.
- **Falsification and critical testing.** Following Popper, theories are valued for making risky predictions that could fail, and progress comes from conjecture and refutation rather than accumulation of confirmations.
- **Theory-ladenness of observation.** Observation is shaped by prior concepts and expectations, an argument developed by Hanson and Kuhn and supported by the Duhem-Quine point that hypotheses are tested only in bundles with auxiliary assumptions.
- **Critical realism.** The world exists independently, but our descriptions of it are partial and revisable. Objectivity is pursued as a regulative ideal through replication, peer criticism and triangulation of methods, rather than assumed to be achieved by a single observer.

## Historical context
Quine's critique of the analytic-synthetic distinction and of reductionism, Popper's replacement of verification by falsifiability, and Kuhn's account of paradigm shifts together undermined the positivist programme. Post-positivism is not one doctrine, and writers described by it disagree about how far to follow Kuhn's emphasis on social and historical factors.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is an analogy. Post-positivist accounts of inquiry as fallible hypothesis revision under uncertainty resemble the Bayesian picture in which agents hold probabilistic beliefs that are updated by prediction error, and Active Inference treats perception and action as hypothesis testing. The parallel concerns the shape of the reasoning, and it does not show that the framework adopts a particular philosophy of science.
