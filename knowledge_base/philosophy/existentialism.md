# Existentialism

## Overview
Existentialism is a family of philosophical views, mostly from nineteenth- and twentieth-century Europe, that take the concrete existence of the individual as the starting point of inquiry. It asks how a person should live when no external authority, essence or system settles the question in advance. Kierkegaard and Nietzsche are usually counted as precursors. Heidegger, Sartre, de Beauvoir, Jaspers and Merleau-Ponty developed the major positions in the twentieth century, while Camus is associated with the movement but rejected the label.

## Central themes
- **Existence precedes essence.** Sartre (1946) argued that a human being has no predetermined nature and defines itself through choices.
- **Freedom and responsibility.** Freedom is treated as unavoidable, and with it responsibility for one's choices, which Sartre linked to anguish and bad faith, the attempt to deny one's freedom.
- **Authenticity.** Heidegger (1927) described authentic existence as owning one's possibilities, including finitude and death, rather than conforming to the anonymous "one".
- **The absurd.** Camus described the confrontation between the human demand for meaning and a world that does not supply it.
- **Embodiment and situation.** Merleau-Ponty and de Beauvoir emphasised that freedom is exercised by embodied agents in particular social situations.

Not all of these thinkers agreed, and several, including Heidegger, rejected the existentialist label for their own work.

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is indirect. Phenomenology, especially Heidegger and Merleau-Ponty, influenced enactive and embodied approaches to cognition, and some authors have drawn on these sources when interpreting Active Inference. The framework itself is a mathematical account of self-organising systems and makes no claims about meaning or authenticity. Its phrase "existential imperative", used for the tendency of living systems to remain in their characteristic states, refers to persistence and survival, not to the philosophical sense of existence discussed above. Whether Active Inference can say anything about freedom or choice is taken up under [[free_will]].
