# Skepticism

## Overview
Philosophical skepticism challenges the claim that we have knowledge, or justified belief, in some domain. Skeptical positions range from local doubts, about the past, other minds or ethics, to global doubts about the external world as a whole. The tradition serves both as a position and as a test: much of [[epistemology]] can be read as a response to skeptical arguments.

## Main traditions
- **Pyrrhonian skepticism.** Associated with Sextus Empiricus, it recommends suspending judgment (*epoché*) on non-evident matters, with the aim of tranquillity.
- **Academic skepticism.** Arcesilaus and Carneades argued that nothing can be known with certainty and that conduct may be guided by plausibility.
- **Cartesian doubt.** Descartes used the dreaming and evil-demon arguments to ask whether any belief resists doubt. Modern brain-in-a-vat scenarios have the same structure: experience would be the same whether or not the external world is as it seems.
- **Humean skepticism.** Hume argued that inductive inference and belief in an external world cannot be justified by reason alone.

## Responses
Moore appealed to the greater certainty of common-sense claims than of skeptical premises. Contextualists hold that standards for "knows" shift with conversational context. Externalists, including reliabilists, deny that knowledge requires the ability to rule out skeptical scenarios by reflection. Others reject the skeptic's underlying picture of experience as an intermediary between mind and world (see [[empiricism]] and [[rationalism]] for the classical foils).

## Relation to Active Inference
Inferential theories of perception revive a version of the skeptical predicament. On the Active Inference view, the brain has access to the world only through sensory samples, and it infers hidden causes from them, behind a Markov blanket. Hohwy (2013) draws out the resemblance to the problem of the external world and argues that prediction-error minimisation gives an internalist, inferential picture. Others, including enactive and embodied theorists, reject the framing and hold that action and embodiment ground direct engagement with the world. The link is a live philosophical debate, not a derivation from the formalism.
