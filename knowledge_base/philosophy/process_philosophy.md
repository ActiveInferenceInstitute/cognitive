# Process Philosophy

## Overview

Process philosophy is the metaphysical position that processes, events and becoming are the basic constituents of reality, and that enduring things are stable patterns within change. It opposes substance metaphysics, in which objects with fixed properties come first and change is something that happens to them. The tradition is associated with Heraclitus as a precursor, and in the twentieth century with Alfred North Whitehead and Henri Bergson, with related emphases in William James and John Dewey.

## Core claims

- **Becoming over being**: the primary units are occasions or events, such as Whitehead's "actual occasions", and objects are sequences of related events.
- **Relationality**: entities are constituted by their relations and interactions, not possessed of intrinsic properties prior to them.
- **Creativity and novelty**: the world is not just rearrangement of fixed elements; new patterns arise that were not already determined.
- **Time as real**: duration and temporal passage are not illusions or mere coordinates. Bergson contrasted lived duration with clock time.
- **Organism as model**: Whitehead's "philosophy of organism" and later work in philosophy of biology see living systems, which exist only by continual exchange and self-renewal, as paradigms rather than exceptions.

## Contrast with substance metaphysics

A river is the stock example: the water changes entirely while the river persists, so persistence belongs to the pattern. Flames, whirlpools and cells fit the same description. Process views argue that physics and biology increasingly describe things in this way, though substance ontologists reply that processes still need bearers.

## Role in Active Inference

The free-energy principle describes a system as a non-equilibrium steady-state density maintained by continuous flow, where the thing persists only by active exchange across its Markov blanket. This is a dynamical, process-flavoured ontology: "thingness" is a pattern of ongoing inference and regulation, closely aligned with [[autopoiesis]] and [[enactivism]]. Pragmatism shares the stress on activity and consequences over static representation. The correspondence is thematic. Active Inference is a mathematical framework, whose proponents rarely cite process philosophy as its source, and process metaphysics does not follow from the formalism; a substance ontologist can accept the same equations. Treat the connection as a useful interpretive lens, not a derivation.
