# Lived Body

## Overview
The lived body is the body as it is experienced from the first-person perspective: the body through which one perceives, moves and acts, and which is normally not an object of attention. The notion comes from phenomenology, where it contrasts with the body regarded as a physical object among others. German-language authors mark the difference with *Leib* for the lived body and *Körper* for the body as thing.

## Main ideas
- **Husserl.** Husserl described the body as the "zero point" of orientation, the centre from which spatial relations are organised, and as the organ through which kinaesthetic sensations and perception are coordinated.
- **Merleau-Ponty.** In *Phenomenology of Perception* (1945), Merleau-Ponty argued that the body is the subject of perception and not just its object, and that skilled movement shows a practical, pre-reflective grasp of space, which he called motor intentionality. His discussions of the phantom limb and of patients with specific impairments were intended to show that bodily experience cannot be reduced to either purely physiological or purely psychological explanation.
- **Body schema and body image.** Gallagher (2005) distinguished the body schema, a nonconscious system of sensorimotor capacities that regulates posture and movement, from the body image, a set of conscious perceptions, beliefs and attitudes about one's body (see [[body_schema]]).
- **Transparency.** The lived body tends to withdraw from awareness when functioning smoothly and becomes prominent in pain, illness, fatigue or awkwardness. Feminist and disability phenomenologists have extended the analysis to the social shaping of embodied experience.

See [[existential_phenomenology]] for the wider tradition and [[body_ownership]] for work on the sense that the body is one's own.

## Relation to Active Inference
Active inference supplies a formal account of embodied perception and action, in which interoceptive and proprioceptive signals are predicted and regulated. Seth (2013) proposed that emotion and the experience of being an embodied self depend on interoceptive inference about the causes of bodily signals, an account that is a candidate model of aspects of the lived body (see [[interoception]], [[proprioception]]). It is a research proposal, with partial empirical support, and it addresses the mechanisms of bodily experience more than the philosophical analysis of its structure.
