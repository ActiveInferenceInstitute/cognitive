# Critical Theory

## Overview
Critical theory, in the sense of the Frankfurt School, is social theory that aims to diagnose domination and contribute to emancipation, not only to describe society. Horkheimer (1937) contrasted "traditional" theory, which treats the social world as an object to be explained from outside, with "critical" theory, which understands itself as part of the social process it studies.

## Core claims
**Ideology critique.** Beliefs that appear natural may serve particular interests. Critical theory asks how such beliefs are produced and sustained and what they conceal, drawing on Marx, Hegel and Freud.

**Instrumental reason.** Horkheimer and Adorno's *Dialectic of Enlightenment* (1947) argued that reason reduced to means-ends calculation, detached from questions of ends, can become a tool of domination. This is a different sense of "instrumental" from [[instrumentalism]] in philosophy of science, where it concerns the status of theories.

**Communicative action.** Habermas, in the second generation, replaced the earlier pessimism with a theory of rationality grounded in discourse oriented toward mutual understanding, and distinguished cognitive interests (technical, practical, emancipatory). He drew explicitly on American [[pragmatism]], especially Peirce and Mead, for the claim that inquiry and truth are social processes.

**Methodological stance.** Critical theory is not a method, but insists that knowledge claims be examined for their social conditions, a position sometimes compared with the sociology of scientific knowledge.

## Role in Active Inference
The connection is weak and mostly analogical, and should not be overstated. Active Inference is a formal framework in the natural-science tradition that critical theory calls "traditional". Points of contact are: (1) beliefs as priors shaped by niche and culture, which gives a mathematical shape to the claim that cognition depends on social context ([[social_norms]], [[cultural_cognition]]); (2) the observation that belief updating can be self-reinforcing when an agent's own actions select its evidence, which resembles ideology reproducing itself, a parallel that is suggestive, not a result in either field; (3) the critical question of whose preferences are encoded as $C$ when a model is applied to institutions. These are discussion prompts, not established links.
