# Scientific Anti-Realism

## Overview
Scientific anti-realism is the family of positions that reject scientific realism, the thesis that mature scientific theories are approximately true and that the unobservable entities they posit exist (see [[scientific_realism]]). Anti-realists accept the empirical success of science while disputing that this success warrants belief in what theories say about unobservables such as electrons or fields.

## Main positions
- **Instrumentalism.** Theories are tools for prediction and organisation of observations, not descriptions of hidden reality. Statements about unobservables are treated as useful devices, without a claim to truth. Logical positivists and some operationalists held versions of this view ([[logical_positivism]], [[operationalism]]).
- **Constructive empiricism.** Van Fraassen (1980) holds that science aims at empirical adequacy, meaning truth about observable things and events, and that accepting a theory involves belief only in its empirical adequacy, with no commitment to the truth of its claims about unobservables. He takes the observable-unobservable boundary to be set by what unaided human senses can detect.
- **Social constructivism.** Some sociologists and historians of science argue that scientific facts are shaped by social negotiation, a view often called anti-realism about science though its commitments are disputed.

## Arguments
The argument from underdetermination holds that evidence does not uniquely fix a theory, since empirically equivalent rivals are conceivable. The pessimistic meta-induction, as stated by Laudan (1981), notes that many past successful theories were later rejected and contain terms that fail to refer, so current theories may share their fate. Stanford (2006) argues for a related claim, that past scientists failed to conceive of well-confirmed alternatives. Realists reply with the no-miracles argument, according to which only truth explains success, and with structural realism, which keeps what survives theory change ([[structural_realism]], [[paradigm_shifts]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
The debate recurs inside the framework. Realist readings treat generative models and Markov blankets as describing the structure of real systems, while instrumentalist readings treat them as modelling devices whose use is justified by predictive and explanatory utility. The question which reading the free energy principle deserves is under active discussion in the philosophical literature, and the framework itself does not settle it.
