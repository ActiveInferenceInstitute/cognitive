# Logical Positivism

## Overview
Logical positivism (logical empiricism) was the philosophical movement of the Vienna Circle and allied Berlin group in the 1920s and 1930s, associated with Schlick, Carnap, Neurath, Reichenbach and Ayer. It combined empiricism with modern formal logic and held that meaningful statements are either analytic truths of logic and mathematics or empirically verifiable.

## Core claims
**Verification principle.** A synthetic statement is cognitively meaningful only if it can be verified, or in later versions confirmed, by observation (see [[verification_theory]]). Metaphysical claims, on this criterion, are not false but meaningless.

**Analytic/synthetic divide.** Logic and mathematics are true by convention or definition; everything else answers to experience.

**Unity of science and reconstruction.** Carnap's *Aufbau* tried to construct all concepts from elementary experience by logical means; Neurath proposed physicalism as a common language. Theoretical terms were to be tied to observation by correspondence rules.

**Collapse.** Hempel showed the empiricist criterion could not be stated without excluding good science or admitting nonsense. Quine's "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" attacked the analytic/synthetic divide and confirmational holism. Kuhn and others showed observation is theory-laden, undermining the observational/theoretical split. The movement is regarded as superseded, though its standards of clarity and its demand for testability persist.

## Role in Active Inference
The influence is indirect and mostly critical. The recurring objection that the free-energy principle is unfalsifiable is a positivist-style demand applied to a framework that deliberately sits above any single hypothesis. The usual reply separates the principle from process theories: predictive coding, discrete-state belief updating and specific policy-selection schemes make empirical claims that can be tested, even if the principle does not. This repository follows that division; its tests pin mathematical identities and behaviour of specific models rather than the principle.

Operationalism, which this positivist tradition helped inspire, appears in practice whenever a construct such as "precision" or "preference" is defined by the code that computes it. That definition is useful, but under the criticisms above it cannot be the whole meaning of the construct.
