# Animal Ethics

## Overview
Animal ethics asks whether nonhuman animals have moral status, what treatment of them is permissible, and on what grounds. Practical topics include farming, research, hunting, captivity and wildlife management. Positions differ on both the foundation of moral status and the conclusions drawn, and this page presents them without adjudicating.

## Positions
- **Utilitarian.** Singer (1975) argued, following Bentham's remark that the question is whether animals can suffer, that equal consideration of interests applies across species, so that comparable suffering counts equally. This view is related to [[consequentialism]].
- **Rights-based.** Regan (1983) argued that animals who are "subjects-of-a-life" have inherent value and rights that should not be overridden for aggregate benefit, in line with [[deontological_ethics]]. Kantian approaches that tie moral status to rational agency have been used both to exclude animals from direct duties and, in revised forms, to include them.
- **Virtue and care.** Virtue theorists ask what treatment of animals expresses about character, and care ethicists emphasise relationships of dependence and responsiveness (see [[virtue_ethics]], [[care_ethics]]).
- **Capabilities and relational views.** Nussbaum extended the capabilities approach to animals, and relational approaches such as Donaldson and Kymlicka's treat different animal groups, such as domesticated and wild, as having different relations to human communities.
- **Skeptical or human-centred positions.** Some argue that duties regarding animals are indirect or that human interests take priority; others hold that moral status requires capacities animals lack. Proponents of each give extended arguments.

Empirical questions bear on the debates, especially which animals are sentient, which depends on evidence from behaviour and neuroscience (see [[animal_cognition]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
Active Inference does not address the moral question directly. Its relevance is empirical and indirect: models of valence, affect and interoception bear on how sentience might be evidenced and compared across species, but whether and how that evidence fixes moral status is a philosophical issue outside the framework.
