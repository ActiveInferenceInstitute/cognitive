# Hilary Putnam

## Overview
Hilary Putnam (1926-2016) was an American philosopher and mathematician who taught at Northwestern, Princeton, MIT and Harvard. He was unusual among analytic philosophers for publicly revising his own positions. He first advanced scientific realism and a functionalist account of mind, then criticised both, and he moved toward a pragmatist and anti-reductionist outlook in his later work. In the revival of pragmatism after 1970 (see [[pragmatism]]) he is one of the figures most often named alongside Richard Rorty and Robert Brandom.

## Main positions
- **Semantic externalism.** In "The Meaning of 'Meaning'" (1975) Putnam used the Twin Earth thought experiment to argue that the extension of a natural-kind term such as "water" is fixed partly by the environment and by a linguistic division of labour, so that "meanings just ain't in the head". Two speakers who are internally identical can mean different things.
- **Functionalism.** In the 1960s Putnam proposed that mental states are individuated by their functional roles, not by their physical makeup, so that they could be realised in different substrates (multiple realisability). This became a founding idea of the [[computational_theory_mind]]. In *Representation and Reality* (1988) he argued against it.
- **Internal realism.** In *Reason, Truth and History* (1981) he argued, drawing on model-theoretic considerations and the brain-in-a-vat scenario, that the world does not come with a single description-independent structure of objects. Truth is idealised rational acceptability inside a conceptual scheme, not correspondence with a scheme-independent world (compare [[correspondence_theory]] and [[coherence_theory]]).
- **Late pragmatism and natural realism.** In his later writing, including *Renewing Philosophy* (1992) and the 1994 Dewey Lectures "Sense, Nonsense, and the Senses", Putnam rejected the idea that perception is mediated by inner representations and defended a direct, "commonsense" realism. He treated the pragmatists, particularly James and Dewey, as allies.
- **Fact and value.** *The Collapse of the Fact/Value Dichotomy and Other Essays* (2002) argues that factual inquiry already presupposes epistemic values, so that the sharp separation of fact from value cannot be maintained.

## Relation to Active Inference
The links are interpretive. Active Inference treats an agent's generative model as a statistical description of how its sensory states are coupled to an environment (see [[generative_model]]), which fits the externalist point that what an agent's states are about depends on its embedding, but the framework does not itself take a side in the Twin Earth debate. Putnam's later rejection of inner representations as intermediaries is closer in spirit to enactive readings (see [[enactivism]]) than to representational readings of the generative model. Nothing here is a claim made by Putnam about the free energy principle.
