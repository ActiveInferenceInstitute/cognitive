# Mono no Aware

## Overview
*Mono no aware* is usually translated "the pathos of things". The meaning of the phrase is complex and has changed over time, but it basically refers to a feeling (*aware*) for things (*mono*) that arises from their transience. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (Parkes and Loughnane) places it first among the traditional Japanese aesthetic categories, ahead of *wabi*, *sabi*, *yugen*, *iki* and *kire*.

## Sources and examples
- **Early poetry.** In the eighth-century anthology *Man'yoshu* the feeling of *aware* is typically triggered by the plaintive calls of birds or other animals.
- **Narrative.** It plays a major role in Murasaki Shikibu's early eleventh-century *Tale of Genji*. The opening lines of the later *Tale of the Heike* link impermanence to the feeling directly: the bells of Gion echo the impermanence of all things.
- **Essays.** Kenko, in *Essays in Idleness* (1332), writes that if people never faded like dew or vanished like smoke, things would lose their power to move us, and that "the most precious thing in life is its uncertainty".
- **Everyday practice.** The most frequently cited contemporary example is the love of cherry blossoms. The blossoms are not intrinsically more beautiful than those of other fruit trees, but are more highly valued because their beauty is so short.

## Norinaga's theory
Motoori Norinaga brought the idea to the front of literary theory with a study showing it to be the central theme of *The Tale of Genji*. He argued for a broader understanding of it as a profound sensitivity to the emotional and affective dimensions of existence, and held that the work's achievement lies in portraying characters with this sensibility so that readers are able to feel it with them. He contrasted this with didactic Buddhist and Confucian readings that judged the tale by its moral lessons (see [[kokugaku_nativism]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
The relation is interpretive and loose. Active Inference accounts of emotion and aesthetic experience (see [[aesthetic_value]]) connect affect to the way an agent's predictions about its own states are met or violated over time, and one could ask how attention to transience fits that picture. *Mono no aware* is a literary and aesthetic notion, not a quantity in a model, and nothing in the framework requires it.
