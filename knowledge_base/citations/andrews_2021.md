# The Math Is Not the Territory: Navigating the Free Energy Principle

## Authors
- **Mel Andrews** (University of Cincinnati)

## Publication Details
- **Journal**: Biology & Philosophy
- **Year**: 2021
- **Volume**: 36
- **Issue**: 5
- **Pages**: 30
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s10539-021-09807-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10539-021-09807-0)

## Abstract
This paper provides a systematic philosophical critique of the free energy principle, arguing that the FEP often conflates mathematical formalism with empirical content. Andrews distinguishes between the FEP as a mathematical framework (which is tautological or definitionally true), the FEP as an empirical theory (which makes testable claims), and the FEP as a normative framework (which prescribes how to build models). The paper argues that many of the most dramatic claims made on behalf of the FEP (e.g., that all self-organizing systems perform inference) rest on equivocating between these different readings.

## Key Contributions

### Distinguishing Three FEPs
- **FEP-Math**: The mathematical formalism (tautological, unfalsifiable)
- **FEP-Empirical**: Empirical claims about brains and organisms (testable)
- **FEP-Normative**: A framework for building computational models (useful)
- **Equivocation**: Many arguments illicitly slide between these readings

### Markov Blanket Critique
- **Reification**: Markov blankets are statistical constructs, not physical boundaries
- **Arbitrariness**: Any system can be partitioned into a Markov blanket given sufficient variables
- **Ontological Inflation**: Moving from "can be described as" to "is performing" inference
- **Map-Territory Confusion**: Mathematical descriptions mistaken for physical mechanisms

### Tautology vs Empirical Content
- **Definitional Truth**: If the FEP is true by definition, it cannot be falsified
- **Empirical Claims**: If it makes empirical claims, it can be tested
- **Moving Target**: The FEP seems to shift between these when challenged
- **Explanatory Power**: Questions whether the FEP truly explains or merely redescribes

### Inference Skepticism
- **Literal vs Metaphorical**: Does a thermostat literally "infer" temperature?
- **Intentionality**: Inference implies intentional states -- do all systems have these?
- **Scaling Problem**: What is informative about saying rocks and bacteria "minimize free energy"?
- **Cognitive Inflation**: Risk of ascribing cognitive properties to simple physical systems

## Core Arguments

### The Tautology Charge
If the FEP states that "any system that exists minimizes free energy," this is either:
1. **Tautological**: True by definition (existence = minimizing free energy) -- in which case it cannot explain anything
2. **Empirical**: Making a substantive claim about how systems work -- in which case it needs to be falsifiable

Andrews argues the FEP often equivocates between these readings.

### The Map-Territory Confusion
```
Mathematical description: "Internal states can be DESCRIBED AS minimizing F"
Ontological claim: "Internal states ARE minimizing F"
```

The paper argues that moving from the first to the second is unjustified. A mathematical description (the map) is not the same as the physical process (the territory).

### The Markov Blanket Problem
- **Problem 1**: Markov blankets are analyst-relative constructs
- **Problem 2**: The same system can have multiple valid Markov blanket decompositions
- **Problem 3**: Having a Markov blanket is not sufficient for performing inference
- **Problem 4**: The "particular partition" may not exist for real physical systems

### Scope and Explanatory Power
- **Too Broad**: If everything minimizes free energy, the principle cannot distinguish between things
- **Too Narrow**: When applied to specific systems, it may add nothing beyond existing explanations
- **Just Right?**: The paper questions whether there is a Goldilocks zone for the FEP's scope

## Philosophical Framework

### Scientific Realism vs Instrumentalism
- **Realist Reading**: The FEP describes real inference processes in nature
- **Instrumentalist Reading**: The FEP is a useful tool for modeling, not a claim about reality
- **Andrews' Position**: The instrumentalist reading is defensible; the realist reading is problematic

### Levels of Description
- **Physical Level**: Particles, forces, energy
- **Computational Level**: Algorithms, inference, optimization
- **Intentional Level**: Beliefs, desires, goals
- **Category Error**: Conflating levels of description

### Falsifiability
- **Karl Popper**: Scientific theories must be falsifiable
- **FEP Challenge**: Can the FEP be falsified?
- **Auxiliary Hypotheses**: FEP proponents can always modify the generative model
- **Progressive vs Degenerative**: Is the FEP research program progressive?

## Responses and Defenses

### Possible Responses to Andrews
1. **Scale Matters**: The FEP is trivially true for simple systems but explanatorily powerful for complex ones
2. **Process Theory**: The FEP is complemented by process theories (predictive coding) that are empirical
3. **Pragmatic Defense**: The FEP is useful for building models, regardless of ontological status
4. **Dual Aspect**: The mathematical and physical descriptions are two aspects of the same thing
5. **Bayesian Mechanics**: Recent work (Da Costa et al. 2021) provides more rigorous foundations

## Impact and Applications

### Philosophy of Science
- **Demarcation**: What makes the FEP a scientific theory?
- **Explanation**: What constitutes explanation in the FEP framework?
- **Reduction**: Is the FEP reductionist, eliminativist, or integrative?

### Philosophy of Mind
- **Panpsychism Debate**: Does the FEP imply panpsychism (or "pan-inference-ism")?
- **Intentionality**: The scope of genuine intentional states
- **Consciousness**: Whether free energy minimization relates to consciousness

### For FEP Researchers
- **Clarity**: Need to distinguish mathematical from empirical claims
- **Rigor**: Need to specify falsifiable predictions
- **Humility**: Acknowledge limits of mathematical formalism as description of reality

## Citations and Influence
This paper has become the standard philosophical critique of the FEP and is essential reading for anyone working in the area. It has forced proponents of the FEP to be more careful about distinguishing mathematical formalism from empirical content and has contributed to a more philosophically sophisticated discussion of the theory's scope and limits.
