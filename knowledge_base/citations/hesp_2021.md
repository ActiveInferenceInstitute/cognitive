## Abstract
This paper develops an account of emotional valence within the deep active inference framework. The authors propose that affective valence emerges from the rate of change of free energy over time -- specifically, that positive valence corresponds to decreasing free energy (things going better than expected) and negative valence corresponds to increasing free energy (things going worse than expected). Using deep temporal models, they show how emotional states emerge from the dynamics of hierarchical inference and how mood disorders can be understood as pathological patterns of free energy dynamics.

## Key Contributions

### Valence as Free Energy Dynamics
- **Positive Valence**: Rate of free energy decrease (better-than-expected outcomes)
- **Negative Valence**: Rate of free energy increase (worse-than-expected outcomes)
- **Temporal Derivative**: Valence is dF/dt, not F itself
- **Hierarchical**: Valence operates across multiple temporal scales

### Deep Temporal Models
- **Hierarchical Policies**: Nested policies at different time scales
- **Slow and Fast Dynamics**: Mood (slow) and emotion (fast) as different temporal scales
- **Meta-Learning**: Higher levels learn about lower-level dynamics
- **Prior Preferences**: Deep priors shape emotional dispositions

### Mood and Emotion
- **Emotion**: Rapid changes in free energy (fast temporal scale)
- **Mood**: Sustained patterns of free energy change (slow temporal scale)
- **Temperament**: Stable patterns reflecting deep generative model structure
- **Mood Disorders**: Aberrant dynamics at slow temporal scales

### Interoceptive Inference
- **Body States**: Interoceptive predictions as part of the generative model
- **Allostasis**: Anticipatory regulation of physiological states
- **Emotional Embodiment**: Bodily feelings as interoceptive prediction errors
- **Somatic Markers**: Body-based valence signals in decision making

## Core Concepts

### Valence as dF/dt
The central proposal formalizes valence as:
```
Valence(t) = -dF/dt
```

Where:
- Positive valence: F is decreasing (prediction errors being resolved)
- Negative valence: F is increasing (prediction errors accumulating)
- Neutral: F is stable (expectations being met)

### Deep Temporal Hierarchy
The generative model operates at multiple time scales:
```
Level 1: Fast dynamics (seconds) -- emotions, reactions
Level 2: Medium dynamics (hours/days) -- mood, affect
Level 3: Slow dynamics (weeks/months) -- temperament, disposition
Level N: Deepest dynamics (years) -- personality, character
```

### Mood Disorders as Aberrant Dynamics
- **Depression**: Sustained negative valence from persistently high free energy at slow time scales
- **Mania**: Sustained positive valence from aberrantly low free energy expectations
- **Anxiety**: Anticipated increases in free energy (expected negative valence)
- **Anhedonia**: Failure to register free energy decreases (blunted positive valence)

## Mathematical Formalism

### Free Energy Rate of Change
```
dF/dt = dE_q[ln q(s) - ln p(o, s)]/dt
```

This can be decomposed into contributions from:
- Changes in beliefs: `dq(s)/dt`
- Changes in observations: `do/dt`
- Changes in model parameters: `dtheta/dt`

### Deep Model Structure
The generative model includes hierarchical policies:
```
p(o, s_1:L, pi_1:L) = p(o|s_1) * prod_l [p(s_l|s_{l+1}, pi_l) * p(pi_l|s_{l+1})]
```

Where `l` indexes hierarchical levels with progressively slower dynamics.
