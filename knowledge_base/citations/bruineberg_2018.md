# The Anticipating Brain Is Not a Scientist: The Free-Energy Principle from an Ecological-Enactive Perspective

## Abstract
This paper challenges the "scientist" metaphor commonly associated with the free energy principle and predictive processing. The authors argue that the brain should not be understood as a detached scientist making inferences about the world from behind a Markov blanket (as in Hohwy's internalist reading). Instead, drawing on ecological psychology (Gibson) and enactivism (Merleau-Ponty), they propose that the FEP is better understood as describing the dynamics of an embodied, skillfully engaged organism coupled to its environment. The brain is an anticipating organ, not a hypothesis-testing scientist.

## Key Contributions

### Critique of Inferential Isolation
- **Against Hohwy**: Challenges the internalist, "brain in a vat" reading of the FEP
- **Coupling over Inference**: Emphasizes organism-environment coupling over internal inference
- **No Veil of Ideas**: The brain is not cut off from the world by its Markov blanket
- **Skilled Engagement**: Organisms are skilled agents, not detached observers

### Ecological-Enactive Reading
- **Affordances**: The environment offers affordances, not neutral data
- **Skilled Coping**: Expert behavior involves direct engagement, not hypothesis testing
- **Organism-Environment System**: The relevant unit of analysis is the coupled system
- **Non-Representational**: Challenges the need for internal representations

### Reinterpreting the FEP
- **Attracting Set**: Free energy minimization maintains the system on its attracting set
- **Life-Form**: The attracting set is defined by the organism's form of life
- **Grip on the World**: The organism maintains an optimal "grip" on the environment
- **Dynamical Coupling**: FEP describes coupled dynamics, not isolated inference

## Core Concepts

### The Scientist vs the Organism
- **Scientist Metaphor**: Brain generates hypotheses, tests against data, updates beliefs
- **Organism Alternative**: Embodied agent maintaining skillful coupling with environment
- **Problem**: The scientist metaphor implies passivity and detachment
- **Solution**: Reinterpret FEP through ecological-enactive lens

### Affordances and Solicitations
Drawing on Gibson and Rietveld:
- **Affordances**: Action possibilities offered by the environment
- **Solicitations**: Experienced pull toward relevant affordances
- **Skilled Intentionality**: Expert agents are solicited by relevant affordances
- **Field of Affordances**: The landscape of action possibilities

### Optimal Grip
From Merleau-Ponty:
- **Optimal Grip**: Organisms seek the best possible engagement with the environment
- **Free Energy as Grip**: Minimizing free energy is maintaining optimal grip
- **Not Inference**: This is not hypothesis testing but skilled engagement
- **Embodied**: The whole body, not just the brain, participates

### Reinterpreting Markov Blankets
- **Not Epistemic Barriers**: Markov blankets are not walls cutting the brain off from the world
- **Dynamic Boundaries**: They describe dynamic coupling between organism and environment
- **Permeable**: Information flows through them via sensory and active states
- **Relational**: They define a relation, not a separation

## Philosophical Framework

### Ecological Psychology (Gibson)
- **Direct Perception**: Organisms directly perceive affordances
- **Ecological Information**: Information specifies affordances
- **No Representation Needed**: Perception does not require internal models

### Enactivism (Merleau-Ponty, Varela)
- **Embodied Cognition**: Cognition is embodied, not purely neural
- **Sensorimotor Contingencies**: Perception through action
- **Autopoiesis**: Self-producing systems
- **Lived Body**: First-person bodily experience

### Synthesis
The paper synthesizes these traditions with the FEP:
- **FEP as Ecological**: Free energy minimization as ecological coupling
- **FEP as Enactive**: Active inference as sensorimotor engagement
- **Non-Representational FEP**: FEP without internal representations
- **Embodied FEP**: Whole-organism (not brain-centric) free energy minimization

## Citations and Influence
This paper has been widely cited in philosophical discussions of the free energy principle. It opened up an important interpretive debate about whether the FEP commits us to representationalism and internalism, or whether it is compatible with (or even best understood through) ecological and enactive approaches. It remains a key reference in the ongoing philosophical debate about the implications of the FEP.
