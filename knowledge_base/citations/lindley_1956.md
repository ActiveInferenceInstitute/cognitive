# Lindley (1956)

**On a Measure of the Information Provided by an Experiment**

## Reference

Lindley, D. V. (1956). On a measure of the information provided by an experiment. *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics*, 27(4), 986-1005.

## Key Contributions

- Formal definition of expected information gain from experiments
- Foundation for Bayesian experimental design and optimal data collection
- Connection between information theory and statistical decision theory
- Measure of epistemic value that anticipates later developments in active inference

## Relevance to Active Inference

Lindley's expected information gain is a direct precursor to the epistemic component of expected free energy in active inference. When an active inference agent evaluates policies, the information gain term (which drives epistemic, exploratory behavior) formalizes the same quantity Lindley introduced for optimal experimental design. This connection places active inference's treatment of curiosity and exploration on rigorous information-theoretic foundations.
