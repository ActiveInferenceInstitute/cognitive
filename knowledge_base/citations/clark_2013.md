# Whatever Next? Predictive Brains, Situated Agents, and the Future of Cognitive Science

## Authors

## Abstract
This influential target article provides a comprehensive review of predictive processing as a unifying framework for understanding brain function. Clark argues that the brain is fundamentally in the business of predicting its own sensory inputs and that this prediction-based approach unifies perception, action, and cognition. Unlike Hohwy's more internalist reading, Clark emphasizes how predictive processing is compatible with -- and enriched by -- embodied, embedded, extended, and enactive (4E) approaches to cognition. The paper includes extensive peer commentary and author response.

## Key Contributions

### Comprehensive Review
- **Prediction Machines**: Brains as hierarchical prediction engines
- **Action-Oriented**: Predictions guide action as much as perception
- **Situated Cognition**: Predictive processing for embodied, embedded agents
- **Future Directions**: Roadmap for cognitive science research

### Unifying Framework
- **Perception**: Top-down predictions constrain bottom-up processing
- **Action**: Proprioceptive predictions drive motor behavior
- **Emotion**: Interoceptive predictions and allostatic regulation
- **Language**: Linguistic predictions at multiple levels
- **Social Cognition**: Predicting other agents' behavior

### Embodied Predictive Processing
- **Body as Model**: The body is part of the generative model
- **Environmental Coupling**: Prediction includes body-environment dynamics
- **Extended Mind**: Predictive processing beyond the skull
- **Enactive Dimension**: Perception through sensorimotor prediction
- **Scaffolding**: Cultural and environmental prediction scaffolds

### Contrasts with Hohwy
- **Externalist Reading**: Predictions extend into body and environment
- **Against Isolation**: Brain is not epistemically sealed
- **Action-Oriented Representations**: Representations for action, not just belief
- **Material Culture**: External structures as part of the predictive apparatus

## Core Concepts

### The Prediction Machine
The brain is a multi-level prediction machine that operates according to:
1. **Generate**: Top-down predictions about expected sensory input
2. **Compare**: Match predictions against actual sensory input
3. **Update**: Propagate prediction errors upward to revise models
4. **Act**: Issue motor predictions to change sensory input

### Precision-Weighted Prediction Error
Not all prediction errors are equal:
- **Precision**: Expected reliability of prediction errors
- **Attention**: Gain on precise prediction errors
- **Action Selection**: Based on predicted precision-weighted outcomes
- **Psychopathology**: Aberrant precision in mental illness

### Action as Prediction
Motor behavior is recast as prediction fulfillment:
- **Proprioceptive Predictions**: Expected body states
- **Motor Reflex Arcs**: Fulfill proprioceptive predictions
- **Goal-Directed Action**: Higher-level predictions about desired states
- **Tool Use**: Extended body schema through predictive models
