# The Predictive Mind

## Authors
- **Jakob Hohwy** (Monash University)

## Publication Details
- **Publisher**: Oxford University Press
- **Year**: 2013
- **DOI**: [10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199682737.001.0001](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199682737.001.0001)

## Abstract
This book provides the first comprehensive philosophical treatment of the predictive processing framework. Hohwy argues that the brain is fundamentally a hypothesis-testing machine that continuously generates predictions about sensory input and updates its models based on prediction errors. He draws extensively on the free energy principle to develop a new picture of perception, action, attention, and consciousness, while engaging deeply with philosophical questions about the nature of mind, representation, and experience.

## Key Contributions

### Philosophical Framework
- **Prediction Error Minimization**: Core explanatory principle for mental life
- **Inferential Isolation**: The brain is epistemically secluded behind its Markov blanket
- **Evidentiary Boundaries**: Sensory surfaces as the brain's only contact with the world
- **Representation**: Generative models as a form of mental representation

### Perception as Hypothesis Testing
- **Top-Down Predictions**: Perception is dominated by predictions, not sensation
- **Prediction Errors**: Bottom-up signals encode only unpredicted information
- **Precision Weighting**: Attention modulates the gain on prediction errors
- **Illusions**: Explained as strong priors overriding weak sensory evidence

### Consciousness and Self
- **Phenomenal Experience**: Arises from the brain's best hypothesis about sensory causes
- **Self-Models**: The self as the brain's generative model of its own body and agency
- **Metacognition**: Higher-order monitoring of prediction error minimization
- **Agency**: Sense of agency from predicted and actual sensory consequences

### Engagement with Philosophy
- **Internalism vs Externalism**: Defends an internalist view via inferential isolation
- **Bayesian Epistemology**: Knowledge as probabilistic inference
- **Problem of Perception**: Addressed through prediction error minimization
- **Mental Causation**: How beliefs cause actions through active inference

## Core Concepts

### The Prediction Error Minimization Framework
The brain minimizes prediction error across a hierarchy:
- **Low Levels**: Detailed sensory predictions (edges, frequencies)
- **Mid Levels**: Object and scene predictions
- **High Levels**: Abstract conceptual predictions
- **Highest Levels**: Self-model and world-model predictions

### Inferential Isolation
A key philosophical claim: the brain is isolated from the world behind its sensory surfaces (Markov blanket). It can only infer the causes of its sensory input, never directly access them. This has deep implications for epistemology and philosophy of perception.

### Precision and Attention
Precision weighting is the mechanism for attention:
- **High Precision**: Prediction errors are amplified, driving belief updating
- **Low Precision**: Prediction errors are suppressed, favoring prior predictions
- **Clinical Relevance**: Aberrant precision explains psychotic symptoms

## Citations and Influence
The Predictive Mind established predictive processing as a major framework in philosophy of mind and cognitive science. It brought the free energy principle to the attention of philosophers and generated extensive debate about the nature of perception, consciousness, and representation. It remains one of the most important philosophical treatments of predictive processing.
