# Vector Equilibrium

## Overview
Vector equilibrium (VE) is Buckminster Fuller's name for the cuboctahedron considered together with its centre. The mathematics below is standard; the broader claims Fuller attached to it, in the system he called synergetics, are interpretive and not part of accepted physics.

## Geometry
**Coordinates.** The 12 vertices are all permutations of $(\pm1,\pm1,0)$. Each vertex is at distance $\sqrt2$ from the centre, and the nearest-neighbour distance between vertices is also $\sqrt2$, so radius equals edge length. I verified this numerically, along with the count of 24 edges between vertices.

**Counts.** The cuboctahedron has 12 vertices, 24 edges and 14 faces (8 triangles and 6 squares). Including the 12 radial lines from the centre to the vertices gives 36 lines in the "vector" picture, all of equal length, which is the property Fuller meant by equilibrium of vectors.

**Close packing.** The 12 vertices are the centres of 12 equal spheres touching a central one: the kissing configuration of the face-centred cubic (FCC) lattice, which is the densest sphere packing in three dimensions (Hales's proof of the Kepler conjecture). The cuboctahedron is the vertex figure of the FCC lattice and an Archimedean solid.

**Jitterbug.** Fuller described a continuous deformation of the cuboctahedron, rotating the triangular faces about their axes, through an icosahedron to an octahedron, and finally a tetrahedron by collapse. It is a real kinematic transformation of a hinged model of the polyhedron.

## Role in Active Inference
There is no derivation from Active Inference. [[synergetic_information_geometry]] and [[tensegrity_information_geometry]] use VE as a geometric motif for balanced configurations in networks of interacting agents, and the repository's [[synergetics]] page uses it to illustrate equilibrium and packing. Those pages offer an analogy: a balanced local structure resembles a system at a free-energy minimum or with symmetric message exchange. Fuller's claims that VE is a "zero-phase" of all energy transformation, or a model for the vacuum, are not accepted physics, and nothing in the free energy principle depends on them. Treat VE here as a well-defined polyhedron and a source of geometric intuition.
