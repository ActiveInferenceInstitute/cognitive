# Geodesic Equations

## Overview
A geodesic is a curve that is locally length-minimising and has constant speed, the generalisation of a straight line to a curved space. In local coordinates $x^k(t)$ on a manifold with metric $g_{ij}$, geodesics satisfy the second-order system
$$\ddot x^{k}+\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\,\dot x^{i}\dot x^{j}=0,$$
using the summation convention. The coefficients $\Gamma^k_{ij}$ are the Christoffel symbols of the Levi-Civita connection,
$$\Gamma^{k}_{ij}=\tfrac12\,g^{kl}\left(\partial_i g_{jl}+\partial_j g_{il}-\partial_l g_{ij}\right).$$
In flat space with Cartesian coordinates the symbols vanish and the equation reduces to $\ddot x=0$.

## Derivation
Consider the energy functional $E[x]=\tfrac12\int g_{ij}(x)\,\dot x^i\dot x^j\,dt$. The Euler-Lagrange equations for the Lagrangian $L=\tfrac12 g_{ij}\dot x^i\dot x^j$,
$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot x^k}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial x^k}=0,$$
give exactly the geodesic equation after expanding the derivatives. Critical points of the energy, parametrised proportionally to arc length, are critical points of length, so the variational problem and the geometric one coincide. The equation also states that the velocity vector is parallel-transported along the curve.

## Examples and properties
On a sphere of radius $r$ the geodesics are great circles. In the hyperbolic half-plane with $ds^2=(dx^2+dy^2)/y^2$ they are vertical lines and semicircles centred on the axis $y=0$. Given a point and an initial velocity, the geodesic is unique locally, and the exponential map sends the velocity to the point reached at unit time. Geodesics are locally but not always globally minimising, because of conjugate points and cut loci.

## Relation to Active Inference
The relevance is geometric. With the Fisher-Rao metric on a statistical manifold, geodesics are the paths of shortest statistical distance between beliefs, and information-geometric treatments of the free energy principle use them to describe the length of belief trajectories. This is an active but specialised line of work. Belief updating in standard schemes follows gradient flows, not geodesics, although natural-gradient dynamics are defined through the same metric.
