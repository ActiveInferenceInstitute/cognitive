# Sigma-Algebras

## Overview
A $\sigma$-algebra on a set $\Omega$ is a collection $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of $\Omega$ that contains $\Omega$, is closed under complements, and is closed under countable unions. Closure under countable intersections follows from De Morgan's laws. The sets in $\mathcal{F}$ are the measurable sets, those to which a measure can assign a size. In probability, $\Omega$ is the sample space, $\mathcal{F}$ is the family of events, and the triple $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)$ is a probability space in Kolmogorov's axiomatisation.

## Constructions
- **Extreme cases.** The trivial algebra $\{\emptyset,\Omega\}$ is the smallest and the power set $2^\Omega$ is the largest.
- **Generated algebras.** For any collection $\mathcal{C}$ of subsets, $\sigma(\mathcal{C})$ is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal{C}$, the intersection of all $\sigma$-algebras that contain it.
- **Borel algebra.** On a topological space, the Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{B}$ is generated by the open sets. On $\mathbb{R}$ it is generated by the intervals $(-\infty,a]$, which is why a real random variable is determined by its distribution function.
- **Product algebras.** For product spaces, the product $\sigma$-algebra is generated by measurable rectangles. It underlies the definition of stochastic processes as random elements indexed by time ([[kolmogorov_extension]]).
- **Restriction on size.** The power set of $\mathbb{R}$ cannot carry a translation-invariant measure extending length, because of Vitali-type sets that require the axiom of choice. This is why measure is defined on a $\sigma$-algebra smaller than the power set.

## Measurability and information
A function $X:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ is measurable if $X^{-1}(B)\in\mathcal{F}$ for each Borel set $B$. The $\sigma$-algebra $\sigma(X)=\{X^{-1}(B)\}$ consists of the events whose occurrence is determined by the value of $X$. In this sense a $\sigma$-algebra represents a body of information. A filtration $(\mathcal{F}_t)$, an increasing family of $\sigma$-algebras, represents information accumulating over time, and conditional expectation $\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{G}]$ given a sub-$\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{G}$ is the best prediction of $X$ using only that information ([[martingale_theory]], [[conditional_probability]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
The notion is foundational, not specific. Measure-theoretic statements of the [[free_energy_principle]] require the state spaces of the agent and environment to be measurable spaces, and conditional independence of internal and external states given a Markov blanket is a statement about sub-$\sigma$-algebras. Finite-state models in practice take the power set as the $\sigma$-algebra, which makes these technicalities invisible ([[measure_theoretic_active_inference]]).
