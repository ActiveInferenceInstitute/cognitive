# Diffusion Models

## Overview
Diffusion models are [[generative_models|generative models]] that produce data by reversing a gradual noising process. A fixed forward process corrupts a data point with small amounts of Gaussian noise over many steps until it is close to pure noise. A neural network is trained to undo the corruption, and new samples are generated by starting from noise and denoising step by step. They are now a leading approach for images, audio and other high-dimensional data.

## Formulation
In the discrete formulation the forward chain is $q(x_t\mid x_{t-1})=\mathcal{N}\big(\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,x_{t-1},\,\beta_t I\big)$ for a schedule of small variances $\beta_t$. The learned reverse chain $p_\theta(x_{t-1}\mid x_t)$ is trained by maximising a variational lower bound on the log-likelihood, which in the common parameterisation reduces to a regression in which the network predicts the added noise; Ho et al. (2020) found that dropping the bound's per-step weights works well in practice.

The continuous-time view describes the forward process as a stochastic differential equation $dx=f(x,t)\,dt+g(t)\,dw$. Its time reversal is another SDE whose drift contains the score $\nabla_x\log p_t(x)$ of the noised data distribution, so the network can be trained to estimate the score (score-based generative modelling). A related deterministic reverse process, the probability-flow ODE, links diffusion models to [[neural_odes]] and [[normalizing_flows]]. Sampling is slow because it needs many network evaluations, and much recent work reduces the number of steps.

## Relation to Active Inference
The similarities are formal and the status is analogy. Training uses a variational bound, the same device as in [[variational_autoencoders]] and in variational free energy. The score-based reverse dynamics resemble [[langevin_dynamics]], which are noisy gradient flows on a log-density, the form the free-energy-principle literature gives to dynamics at a non-equilibrium steady state. Diffusion models are not part of standard Active Inference formulations, and an agent that perceives and acts is not the same thing as a model that generates samples. Proposals that use them as generative models inside such agents are exploratory.
