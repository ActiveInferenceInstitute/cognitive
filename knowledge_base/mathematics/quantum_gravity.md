# Quantum Gravity

## Overview
Quantum gravity is the name for the search for a theory that merges general relativity, which describes gravity as the curvature of spacetime, with quantum theory, which describes matter and the other forces. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry by Weinstein and Rickles describes it as a physical theory still under construction. The theory would be needed to describe spacetime at the Planck scale, which lies far beyond current experiments, so empirical tests are extremely difficult and there is no consensus on the correct theory.

## Why the problem is hard
Treating the gravitational field as one more quantum field gives a theory that cannot be handled by the usual perturbative methods of [[quantum_field_theory]]. There are also conceptual tensions. General relativity is background independent, since the geometry is itself dynamical, whereas standard quantum theory uses a fixed background. Canonical approaches meet the problem of time, because the constraints of general relativity leave no external time variable. The entry lists time, ontology, the Planck scale and the necessity of quantisation among the philosophical issues.

## Main approaches
- **String theory** does not quantise gravity directly. It quantises one-dimensional strings and obtains general relativity at low energies. The entry notes the landscape of a vast number of possible vacua, with no clear principle for selecting ours.
- **Loop quantum gravity (LQG)** is a canonical approach that quantises general relativity using connection variables of the kind introduced by Ashtekar in place of the metric. States of the geometry are described by networks labelled with representations of $SU(2)$, called spin networks, and the theory is often said to predict discrete spectra for area and volume. Rovelli (2008) and Ashtekar and Lewandowski (2004) review its construction. The entry records that the problem of time and the difficulty of solving the Hamiltonian constraint remain open.
- **Other approaches** include causal sets, causal dynamical triangulations and asymptotic safety, among others.

## Hints from thermodynamics
Black holes obey laws that resemble those of thermodynamics, and the entropy associated with a horizon scales with its area. This has motivated the holographic principle, the proposal that the information in a region is encoded on its boundary (see [[holographic_principle]] and [[black_hole_thermodynamics]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is speculative. The free energy principle is a statement about classical and quantum systems that have a boundary, and not a theory of spacetime. The one concrete bridge is the holographic framing used by Fields, Friston, Glazebrook and Levin (2022), who treat the Markov blanket between a system and its environment as a holographic screen. This borrows the vocabulary of holography, and it does not show that Active Inference constrains any theory of quantum gravity, nor that quantum gravity bears on cognition.
