# Quantum Groups

## Overview
Quantum groups are not groups in the usual sense. The term refers to certain non-commutative, non-cocommutative Hopf algebras that deform either the universal enveloping algebra $U(\mathfrak{g})$ of a Lie algebra or the algebra of functions on a Lie group. They depend on a parameter $q$, and as $q\to 1$ the classical object is recovered. The theory was developed in the 1980s, notably by Drinfeld and Jimbo, motivated by integrable models in statistical mechanics.

## Structure
The standard example is $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$, generated by $E$, $F$, $K$ and $K^{-1}$ with relations
$$KEK^{-1}=q^2E,\quad KFK^{-1}=q^{-2}F,\quad [E,F]=\frac{K-K^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}.$$
In the limit $q\to1$ these reduce to the relations of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$. A Hopf algebra supplies a coproduct, which defines tensor products of representations, and an antipode. Because the coproduct is not cocommutative, the two orderings of a tensor product are related by a universal R-matrix, which satisfies the quantum Yang-Baxter equation $R_{12}R_{13}R_{23}=R_{23}R_{13}R_{12}$.

## Connections
- **Poisson-Lie groups.** The semiclassical limit of a quantum group is a Poisson-Lie group, a Lie group carrying a compatible Poisson structure; quantum groups arise as quantisations of these (see [[poisson_geometry]]).
- **Braided categories.** The representation category of a quantum group is braided, a point of contact with [[category_theory]].
- **Low-dimensional topology.** Representations of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ produce the Jones polynomial of knots through the Reshetikhin-Turaev construction.
- **Integrable systems.** R-matrices solve the Yang-Baxter equation, which underlies exactly solvable lattice models.
