# Neural SDE

## Overview
A neural stochastic differential equation is an Itô equation
$$dX_t=\mu_\theta(X_t,t)\,dt+\sigma_\theta(X_t,t)\,dW_t,$$
in which the drift $\mu_\theta$ and diffusion $\sigma_\theta$ are neural networks and $W_t$ is a Wiener process. Setting $\sigma_\theta=0$ recovers a neural ordinary differential equation, in which a network defines the vector field of a continuous-depth model (Chen et al., 2018). Adding noise allows the model to represent stochastic dynamics, uncertainty and multimodal distributions over trajectories.

## Formulation
Under Lipschitz and linear-growth conditions on $\mu_\theta$ and $\sigma_\theta$, the equation has a unique strong solution (Øksendal, 2003). Activations such as tanh or softplus satisfy the Lipschitz condition and are smooth enough for gradients to be computed through the solver. Samples are generated by discretisation, most simply the Euler-Maruyama scheme
$$X_{k+1}=X_k+\mu_\theta(X_k,t_k)\,\Delta t+\sigma_\theta(X_k,t_k)\,\sqrt{\Delta t}\,\xi_k,\qquad \xi_k\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I),$$
which converges with strong order $1/2$ in general.

Several training approaches exist. A model can be fitted as a latent variable model with a variational posterior over paths, whose objective includes a KL divergence between path measures. When the two processes share the same diffusion, this divergence has a closed form that depends on the squared difference of drifts weighted by the inverse diffusion (Girsanov's theorem). Gradients through the solver can be computed by backpropagating through discretised steps or by an adjoint method that solves a backward stochastic equation (Li et al., 2020). Another approach uses the SDE as a generator and trains it adversarially. Score-based diffusion models use a closely related SDE in which the drift is built from a learned score function (see [[diffusion_models]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
Continuous-time Active Inference represents agents and environments with stochastic differential equations of the same Itô type, with random fluctuations entering the dynamics of hidden states. A neural SDE can be used as a flexible generative model for such dynamics, and the path-space KL divergence is the continuous-time counterpart of the complexity term in the free energy. The relationship is structural and methodological. Standard Active Inference uses hand-specified generalised-coordinate models and not learned drifts, so neural SDEs are a possible extension and not part of the established theory.
