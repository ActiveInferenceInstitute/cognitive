# Quantum Control

## Overview
Quantum control designs external fields or measurements that steer a quantum system toward a target state or operation. Applications include gate synthesis in quantum computing ([[quantum_computing]]), spin manipulation in NMR, and laser control of chemical reactions.

## Formalism
**Open-loop control.** A closed system obeys $i\hbar\,\dot\psi=\big(H_0+\sum_k u_k(t)H_k\big)\psi$, where $H_0$ is the drift Hamiltonian and $u_k(t)$ are control fields. The propagator lives in the unitary group, so reachability is a Lie-theoretic question.

**Controllability.** The system is (operator) controllable if the Lie algebra generated by $\{iH_0,iH_k\}$ is the full $\mathfrak{su}(N)$ (or $\mathfrak u(N)$). For a qubit with $i\sigma_z$ and $i\sigma_x$ the generated algebra is 3-dimensional, all of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, as computed numerically; with $i\sigma_z$ alone it is one dimensional. See [[lie_theory]].

**Optimal pulses.** The control $u(t)$ is found by maximising fidelity with respect to the target, using gradient methods (GRAPE) or Krotov iteration, subject to cost on pulse energy. This is optimal control with a Schrodinger-equation constraint ([[optimal_control]]).

**Measurement-based feedback.** Continuous measurement yields a stochastic master equation. The conditional state $\rho_t$ is a quantum filter (Belavkin), a Bayesian estimate updated by the measurement record; feedback applies controls as a function of $\rho_t$, so the design splits into estimating the state and choosing the control, as in classical filtering and control.

**Open systems.** Decoherence enters through Lindblad terms, and control may be coherent (Hamiltonian), incoherent (reservoir engineering) or measurement driven.

## Role in Active Inference
The link is indirect. Quantum filtering has the same structure as the belief-updating half of Active Inference: a posterior is recursively updated from observations and used for action. Fields, Friston, Glazebrook and Levin extended free-energy arguments to generic quantum systems, but that work concerns boundaries and information exchange in quantum theory, not the engineering of control pulses. No standard quantum control method is formulated as expected-free-energy minimisation, and the references in [[free_energy_principle]] treat the topic as a future direction.
