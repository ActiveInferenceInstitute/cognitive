# Hamiltonian Flows

## Overview
Given a smooth Hamiltonian $H(q,p)$ on a phase space with positions $q\in\mathbb{R}^n$ and momenta $p\in\mathbb{R}^n$, Hamilton's equations are
$$\dot q=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p},\qquad \dot p=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q}.$$
Their solution defines a flow $\varphi_t$ on phase space, the Hamiltonian flow. In geometric language the flow is generated by the Hamiltonian vector field $X_H$ determined by $\iota_{X_H}\omega=dH$, up to a sign convention, where $\omega=\sum_i dq_i\wedge dp_i$ is the symplectic form.

## Conserved quantities
- **Energy.** Along any trajectory, $\dot H=\partial_qH\cdot\dot q+\partial_pH\cdot\dot p=0$, so $H$ is conserved when it has no explicit time dependence.
- **Symplectic form.** Each map $\varphi_t$ is a symplectomorphism, preserving $\omega$.
- **Phase-space volume.** Preservation of $\omega$ implies Liouville's theorem, that the flow preserves volume, so the divergence of $X_H$ is zero and the flow cannot have asymptotically stable attractors.
- **Observables.** For a function $f(q,p)$, $\dot f=\{f,H\}$ using the Poisson bracket, and functions with vanishing bracket are conserved. Noether's theorem links such quantities to symmetries.

## Numerical and statistical use
Standard integrators such as forward Euler do not preserve the symplectic structure and drift in energy. Symplectic schemes, among them the leapfrog method, preserve a nearby Hamiltonian and give bounded energy error over long times. Hamiltonian Monte Carlo exploits this: it draws a momentum, integrates the flow for target density $\pi(q)\propto e^{-U(q)}$ with $H=U(q)+\tfrac12p^\top M^{-1}p$, and applies a Metropolis correction (Neal, 2011).

## Relation to Active Inference
In the free energy principle, the dynamics of a system at a nonequilibrium steady state are decomposed into a dissipative part that climbs the log steady-state density and a solenoidal part that circulates around its level sets, which has the structure of a Hamiltonian flow in a suitable sense. This decomposition is a derived result for stated assumptions about the dynamics, and the solenoidal part is not literally Hamiltonian mechanics with a conserved energy unless the dissipative part is absent. Similarly, generalised coordinates of motion in continuous-time Active Inference have a formal resemblance to phase-space descriptions.
