# Berry Connection

## Overview
The Berry connection is a vector potential defined on the parameter space of a quantum system, which encodes how an eigenstate changes as the parameters are varied. It determines the geometric, or Berry, phase that a state acquires when the parameters are carried slowly around a closed loop, in addition to the usual dynamical phase. Michael Berry (1984) identified this phase for adiabatic cyclic evolution, and Barry Simon (1983) gave its geometric interpretation as a holonomy.

## Definition
Let a Hamiltonian $H(\mathbf R)$ depend on parameters $\mathbf R=(R^1,\dots,R^m)$, and let $|n(\mathbf R)\rangle$ be a normalised, non-degenerate eigenstate with energy $E_n(\mathbf R)$. The Berry connection of state $n$ is
$$A_\mu^{(n)}(\mathbf R)=i\,\langle n(\mathbf R)\,|\,\partial_\mu\,n(\mathbf R)\rangle,\qquad \partial_\mu=\frac{\partial}{\partial R^\mu},$$
which is real because normalisation implies $\langle n|\partial_\mu n\rangle$ is purely imaginary. If the parameters traverse a closed loop $C$ slowly, the adiabatic theorem keeps the system in the instantaneous eigenstate, and the state acquires, beyond the dynamical phase $-\hbar^{-1}\int E_n\,dt$, the geometric phase
$$\gamma_n(C)=\oint_C A_\mu^{(n)}\,dR^\mu.$$
Sign and factor-of-$i$ conventions differ between authors.

## Gauge structure and curvature
The eigenstate is defined only up to a phase. Under $|n\rangle\to e^{i\chi(\mathbf R)}|n\rangle$, the connection transforms as $A_\mu\to A_\mu-\partial_\mu\chi$, like an electromagnetic vector potential. The phase $\gamma_n$ is gauge invariant modulo $2\pi$ for a closed loop, provided the state is single-valued. The **Berry curvature**
$$F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu$$
is gauge invariant, and by Stokes' theorem $\gamma_n(C)=\iint_S F_{\mu\nu}\,dS^{\mu\nu}/2$ for a surface $S$ bounded by $C$. In the language of fibre bundles, the eigenstates form a line bundle over parameter space, the Berry connection is a connection on it, and the geometric phase is the holonomy of parallel transport around the loop (Simon, 1983; Nakahara, 2003). Curvature is concentrated at degeneracies, which act like magnetic monopoles in parameter space. Its integral over a closed surface, divided by $2\pi$, is an integer, the Chern number, which underlies topological properties of band structures (Xiao, Chang and Niu, 2010).

## Relation to Active Inference
The Berry connection has no established role in the free energy principle or in Active Inference models. It is sometimes invoked in discussions of geometric phase effects in path-integral formulations, where it supplies the geometric content of phases accumulated along paths. An information-geometric analogue, using the Fisher metric and connections on spaces of probability distributions, is more common in inference, but the parallel is structural, not an equivalence.
