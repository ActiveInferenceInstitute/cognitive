# Lie Theory

## Overview
Lie theory is the study of continuous symmetry. Its central idea, due to Sophus Lie, is that a smooth group of transformations can be understood through its infinitesimal generators, which form a vector space with a bracket operation, a Lie algebra. Linear algebra at the identity then answers questions about the whole group. [[lie_groups]] covers the group side in more detail.

## Formalism
**Lie algebra.** A vector space $\mathfrak g$ with a bilinear, antisymmetric bracket $[\cdot,\cdot]$ satisfying the Jacobi identity $[X,[Y,Z]]+[Y,[Z,X]]+[Z,[X,Y]]=0$. For matrix groups the bracket is the commutator $[X,Y]=XY-YX$. For $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ with the standard generators $L_x,L_y,L_z$, one has $[L_x,L_y]=L_z$ and cyclic permutations (checked numerically).

**Exponential map.** $\exp:\mathfrak g\to G$, for matrices $\exp(X)=\sum_k X^k/k!$. In $SO(3)$, $\exp(\theta L_z)$ is rotation by $\theta$ about the $z$ axis, with $\det=1$ and $RR^\top=I$.

**Lie correspondence.** Every finite-dimensional real Lie algebra is the Lie algebra of a unique simply connected Lie group, and Lie-algebra homomorphisms integrate to group homomorphisms on simply connected groups.

**Further structure.** The adjoint representation, the Killing form (used to classify semisimple algebras), representation theory, and the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula expressing $\log(e^Xe^Y)$ through nested brackets.

**Controllability.** For a system $\dot U=(H_0+\sum_k u_kH_k)U$ the reachable set is governed by the Lie algebra generated by the $H$'s (the Lie algebra rank condition). A qubit with generators $i\sigma_z$ and $i\sigma_x$ generates a 3-dimensional algebra, all of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$; $i\sigma_z$ alone generates one dimension. See [[quantum_control]].

## Role in Active Inference
The connection is through the geometry used around the theory, not through a result specific to it. Hamiltonian and [[geometric_mechanics]] on Lie groups give Lie-Poisson brackets ([[poisson_geometry]]); symmetry reasoning via Noether's theorem relates conserved quantities to group actions; and the Fisher metric on Gaussian families is invariant under affine-group actions ([[information_geometry]]). Papers applying FEP to symmetry-bearing systems use these tools, but Lie theory is not part of the core Active Inference equations.
