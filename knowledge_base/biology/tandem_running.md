# Tandem Running

## Overview
Tandem running is a form of ant recruitment in which an informed individual guides one naive nestmate to a destination, such as a food source or a new nest site. The follower keeps close to the leader by touching the leader's body with its antennae, and the pair advances in stops and starts. It is the simplest recruitment system in ants and occurs in several subfamilies, often in species with small colonies.

## Mechanism
- **Initiation.** A leader that has found a resource returns to the nest and signals to a nestmate, in some species with a glandular secretion that attracts the follower.
- **Contact maintenance.** The follower taps the leader's hind legs and abdomen with its antennae. When contact is lost, the leader pauses until the follower catches up, and the follower may search locally before the pair resumes (see [[antennation]]).
- **Route learning.** The follower acquires route and landmark information, and later may itself lead further tandem runs.

Because only one nestmate is recruited per trip, information spreads slowly compared with pheromone trails in which many workers respond to one signal (see [[mass_recruitment]]). In exchange, the information is transferred with high fidelity and little waste.

## Teaching
Franks and Richardson (2006) studied tandem running in the rock ant *Temnothorax albipennis* and argued that it meets standard criteria for teaching in animals: the leader changes its behaviour in the presence of a naive follower, pays a cost in travel speed, and the follower learns the route more quickly as a result. This was presented as an example of teaching in a non-human animal that does not rely on a large brain. The interpretation has been discussed, but the behaviour remains a standard example in comparative work on social learning.

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is an analogy at present. A leader reduces the follower's uncertainty about where a resource lies by providing evidence it cannot cheaply obtain on its own, which resembles the epistemic aspect of communication in multi-agent Active Inference. No established model in this framework describes tandem running specifically, and it is better treated as a case study for work on [[social_learning]] and [[collective_behavior_ants]].
