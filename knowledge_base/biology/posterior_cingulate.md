# Posterior Cingulate Cortex

## Overview
The posterior cingulate cortex (PCC) is a region on the medial surface of the parietal lobe, corresponding roughly to Brodmann areas 23 and 31 and lying next to the retrosplenial cortex and the precuneus. It is a core node of the default mode network (DMN) and one of the most densely connected, metabolically active regions of the human cortex at rest.

## Anatomy and function
The PCC connects to the medial temporal lobe including the hippocampus, to the medial prefrontal cortex ([[medial_prefrontal]]), to the inferior parietal lobule ([[inferior_parietal]]) and to parts of the attention networks ([[attention_networks]]). Its activity is high during rest and during internally directed thought such as autobiographical memory retrieval ([[episodic_memory]]), planning the future and self-referential judgement, and typically decreases during externally demanding tasks. The DMN pattern of anticorrelation with task-positive networks is a consistent finding in resting-state imaging.

Leech and Sharp proposed that the PCC is not functionally uniform: its subregions differ, and the region may regulate the balance between internally and externally focused attention, increasing activity when attention is directed inward and adjusting the focus of attention. Evidence comes largely from functional imaging, which is correlational, and the precise computational contribution of the PCC remains unresolved. Reduced PCC metabolism is an early imaging finding in Alzheimer's disease, and altered PCC connectivity has been reported in several psychiatric conditions, as descriptive observations and not as established mechanisms. Its role in consciousness research is discussed in [[consciousness_and_awareness]], where changes in PCC activity accompany anaesthesia and disorders of consciousness.

## Role in Active Inference
Carhart-Harris and Friston proposed that the DMN sits at the top of a hierarchical generative model, encoding high-level, self-related priors that constrain lower levels, and that this makes it important for sustaining a coherent sense of self ([[self_modeling]], [[hierarchical_inference]]). The PCC, as a DMN hub, would take part in the integration implied by such a hierarchy. The proposal is a hypothesis; it generates testable predictions about changes in DMN coupling under psychedelics and in some disorders, but is not a settled account of PCC function.
