# Adaptive Immunity

## Overview
Adaptive immunity is the part of the vertebrate immune system that recognises specific molecular targets, called antigens, and improves its response on repeated exposure. It acts in concert with innate immunity, which responds quickly and non-specifically through barriers, phagocytes and inflammatory signals. The adaptive response takes days to develop on a first encounter and is much faster on a second, which is the basis of vaccination.

## Mechanism
Two classes of lymphocyte carry out the response. B cells produce antibodies, the basis of humoral immunity, and T cells either kill infected cells (cytotoxic T cells) or coordinate other immune cells (helper T cells). Each lymphocyte carries antigen receptors of a single specificity. Diversity of receptors arises by somatic recombination of gene segments (V(D)J recombination) during development, giving a very large repertoire before any pathogen is met. T cell receptors recognise peptide fragments presented on major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules.

The central principle is clonal selection (Burnet, 1959). An antigen binds the few lymphocytes whose receptors fit it, and these clones proliferate and differentiate into effector cells and long-lived memory cells. B cells also undergo somatic hypermutation and selection in germinal centres, which raises antibody affinity. Self-reactive lymphocytes are largely removed or restrained, which is the basis of tolerance, and its failure underlies autoimmune disease (Murphy & Weaver, 2016).

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is an analogy and a speculative research direction, not a derivation. Clonal selection resembles a population-level inference process in which a repertoire is reweighted by evidence, and selection of lymphocyte clones has been compared with Bayesian updating and with natural selection (see [[natural_selection]]). Some authors have extended Active Inference to the immune system by treating immune responses as inference about the state of the body, in connection with [[homeostasis]]. These proposals remain largely theoretical.
