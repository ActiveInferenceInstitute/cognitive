# Cell Proliferation

## Overview
Cell proliferation is the increase in cell number through repeated rounds of division. Each round is a passage through the cell cycle, in which a cell copies its DNA in S phase and divides in mitosis, separated by gap phases G1 and G2. Proliferation is tightly regulated, and failures of control underlie cancer.

## Control of the cycle
Progress through the cycle is driven by cyclin-dependent kinases, whose activity depends on partner proteins called cyclins. Checkpoints halt the cycle when DNA is damaged or incomplete, giving time for repair. External signals such as growth factors determine whether a cell in G1 commits to a new round or leaves the cycle to differentiate or rest. Cells that withdraw permanently from division are called postmitotic.

## Neural proliferation
In the developing vertebrate nervous system, neural stem and progenitor cells line the ventricles. Early divisions are mainly symmetric and expand the progenitor pool. Later divisions are often asymmetric, producing one progenitor and one neuron or intermediate progenitor, so neurogenesis proceeds while the pool is maintained. Neurons are postmitotic once formed. In the cerebral cortex, radial glia act both as progenitors and as scaffolds for migrating neurons. The radial unit hypothesis proposed that the surface area of the cortex depends on the number of founder progenitor units, whereas its thickness depends on the number of rounds of division within each unit, so changes in proliferation can alter cortical size. Extended proliferation in the human lineage has been proposed to contribute to the expansion of the cortex, though details remain under investigation. Disorders of proliferation can cause abnormally small or large brains.

## Relation to Active Inference
Cell proliferation has no specific treatment in Active Inference. It matters to the vault's themes because the number and type of neurons produced set the architecture within which learning later takes place. Development is sometimes described as a slow, evolutionary-scale form of model optimisation, but that is an interpretive framing, and the regulation of the cell cycle is explained by molecular cell biology.
