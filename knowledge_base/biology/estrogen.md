# Estrogen

## Overview
Estrogens are steroid hormones, of which estradiol is the most potent and best studied. They are produced mainly in the ovaries, and in smaller amounts in other tissues including fat, adrenal glands and the testes. The brain can also synthesise estradiol locally from androgens using the enzyme aromatase. Estrogens are present in all sexes, and their concentration varies over the menstrual cycle, pregnancy and ageing.

## Mechanism
Estradiol acts through two nuclear receptors, ER-alpha and ER-beta, which regulate gene transcription over hours, and through membrane-associated receptors, including the G protein-coupled receptor GPER, which produce faster signalling effects. These receptors are expressed in many brain regions, including the hippocampus, amygdala and hypothalamus, so the hormone is positioned to influence both physiology and cognition (McEwen & Alves, 1999).

## Effects on the nervous system
- **Plasticity.** In female rats the density of dendritic spines and synapses on hippocampal neurons fluctuates across the oestrous cycle with estradiol levels (Woolley & McEwen, 1992), and estradiol can modulate long-term potentiation.
- **Neuromodulation.** Estrogens influence dopaminergic, serotonergic and cholinergic systems (see [[neuromodulation]], [[dopamine_system]], [[serotonin]]).
- **Mood and cognition.** Human studies link hormonal transitions to changes in mood and some memory measures, but findings are mixed, effects vary by individual and timing, and causal interpretation is difficult.
- **Social behaviour.** In many species estrogen signalling contributes to affiliative and reproductive behaviour, often together with oxytocin (see [[oxytocin]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is indirect. Active Inference associates neuromodulators with the encoding of precision and synaptic gain, and estrogen modulates several of these systems, so hormonal state could in principle shift how strongly predictions or sensory evidence are weighted. This remains a hypothesis; few formal models connect sex hormones to Active Inference parameters.
