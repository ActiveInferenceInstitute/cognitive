# Trophic Levels

## Overview
A trophic level is a position in a food chain defined by how many feeding steps separate an organism from the primary source of energy. Producers, mostly photosynthetic plants, algae and cyanobacteria, occupy the first level. Herbivores that eat them form the second, carnivores that eat herbivores the third, and higher carnivores follow. Decomposers and detritivores recycle dead material and are usually treated as a parallel pathway connected to every level.

## Energy transfer
- **Loss between levels.** At each step, most energy in food is lost as respiration, heat and undigested material, so only a fraction supports growth and reproduction at the next level. Lindeman (1942) framed this as trophic efficiency. An often quoted rule that about a tenth passes on is a rough approximation, since measured efficiencies vary widely among ecosystems and organisms.
- **Pyramids.** Because of these losses, the energy flow available at higher levels is smaller, a consequence of the second law of thermodynamics (see [[thermodynamics]]). Pyramids of biomass can be inverted in some aquatic systems, where small producers turn over rapidly.
- **Chain length.** Most food chains have three to five levels. Energy supply, ecosystem size and the dynamics of predator-prey interactions are among the factors proposed to limit it.
- **Omnivory.** Many animals feed at several levels, so trophic position is better expressed as a fractional value, an average weighted by diet. Pauly et al. (1998) used the mean trophic level of fisheries landings to document a decline in the average level of marine catches over time.

## Ecological significance
Trophic structure determines how nutrients and contaminants move through ecosystems and how disturbances travel. Predators can regulate lower levels through trophic cascades, a topic within [[food_web_theory]]. The services ecosystems provide, such as fisheries yield, depend on the productivity of the lower levels (see [[ecosystem_services]]).

## Relation to Active Inference
There is no direct link. Trophic levels belong to ecosystem ecology and bioenergetics. Free energy in the Active Inference sense is an information-theoretic quantity and is not the same as the metabolic energy tracked in trophic accounts, so the shared term should not be taken to imply a connection.
