# Response Diversity

## Overview
Response diversity is the diversity of responses to environmental change among species that contribute to the same ecosystem function. Elmqvist et al. (2003) introduced the term in the context of resilience: if several species fill a functional role but react differently to a disturbance such as drought, fire, grazing or warming, the function can persist when some of them decline because others hold steady or increase.

## Distinction from related ideas
Functional diversity concerns the range of roles organisms perform. Functional redundancy refers to several species performing a similar role. Response diversity adds a further dimension: among apparently redundant species, do they differ in their sensitivity to stressors? Redundancy without response diversity offers little insurance, since all members of the group may fail together. The idea is related to the insurance hypothesis, in which biodiversity buffers ecosystem processes against fluctuations in space and time, and to the stability literature that distinguishes the persistence of a system from the constancy of its components (Holling, 1973).

## Evidence and measurement
Response diversity can be examined through experiments or monitoring that measure how species abundances change along environmental gradients, then compare these responses within a functional group. Studies of pollinators, grazers and plant communities have supported the idea, though quantifying response diversity is difficult, in part because species respond to different drivers at different scales and because the traits that determine response are often unknown. It is a conceptual framework with empirical support in specific systems rather than a settled general law.
