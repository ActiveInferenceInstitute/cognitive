# Umwelt Theory

## Overview
Umwelt theory, developed by the Estonian-German biologist Jakob von Uexkull in the early twentieth century, holds that every animal experiences and acts in its own subjective surroundings, the Umwelt (German for "surrounding world"), which differs from the physical environment described by an outside observer. Two animals in the same meadow inhabit different Umwelten because their sense organs and actions select different features as significant. Von Uexkull set out the view in Streifzuge durch die Umwelten von Tieren und Menschen (1934), translated in part as "A stroll through the worlds of animals and men" and fully as "A Foray into the Worlds of Animals and Humans" (2010).

## Key concepts
- **Merkwelt and Wirkwelt**: the perceptual world (what an animal can sense) and the effector world (what it can do). Together they form the Umwelt.
- **Functional circle (Funktionskreis)**: perception and action are coupled in a loop. A perceived feature triggers an action, and the action changes the situation so that new perceptual cues arise.
- **Meaning-carriers**: features of the surroundings acquire significance relative to the animal's needs, not as neutral physical properties.
- **Example**: von Uexkull's account of the tick describes a small set of cues, including butyric acid, warmth and hair-free skin, that organise its behaviour, illustrating how sparse an Umwelt can be.

Von Uexkull opposed purely mechanistic explanations and also differed from Darwinian emphasis on adaptation alone, preferring a plan-like or structural view of organisms. These positions are historical, and modern biology treats Umwelt chiefly as a conceptual lens. The idea has been taken up in ethology, in the biosemiotics of Thomas Sebeok, and in philosophy.

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is conceptual and widely noted. A generative model encodes only those causes that matter for an agent's survival and action, which parallels an Umwelt, and the functional circle resembles the perception-action loop in which action samples the sensory data used for inference. Authors working on the Markov blanket formalism have described an agent's blanket as defining its own boundary of exchange with the world (Allen and Friston, 2018, discuss embodiment and autopoiesis in this setting). The mapping is an analogy and an interpretive resource, not a derivation from von Uexkull's work.
