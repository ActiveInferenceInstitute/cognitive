# Antennation

## Overview
Antennation is the touching or tapping of another individual or of an object with the antennae. In ants it is among the most frequent social behaviours, performed during greetings, food exchange, grooming, recruitment and the checking of unfamiliar individuals. The antennae combine mechanical, chemical, thermal and humidity sensing in a single mobile organ, so one contact can transfer several kinds of information at once.

## Sensory basis
Ant antennae are elbowed, with a long basal segment (the scape) and a flexible tip (the flagellum). The flagellum bears many types of hair-like or peg-like sensilla, and each contains sensory neurons with receptors for a specific class of stimulus. Some respond to volatile odours at a distance, others to contact chemicals such as the compounds of the cuticle (see [[cuticular_hydrocarbons]]). In a study of the Japanese carpenter ant *Camponotus japonicus*, recordings from a single type of sensillum showed responses that distinguished nestmate from non-nestmate profiles (Ozaki et al., 2005). The information then passes to the antennal lobe of the brain, where it is processed together with other sensory input.

## Functions
- **Recognition.** Contact with the body of another ant allows comparison of its chemical profile with the colony template.
- **Information exchange.** During trophallaxis, antennal contact signals requests for food and conveys cues about the donor's diet and condition.
- **Recruitment.** In [[tandem_running]], the follower's contact with the leader keeps the pair together, and a leader waits when contact is lost.
- **Environmental sensing.** Antennae probe the substrate, edges, trails and obstacles during walking.

Antennation is an active, repeated sampling movement. The ant chooses where and how often to touch, and so decides what evidence it will receive.

## Relation to Active Inference
The behaviour fits the notion of active sensing: action is directed at reducing uncertainty about the identity or state of what is touched. The link is conceptual. It is an analogy to epistemic action in Active Inference (see [[epistemic_foraging]]) and is not backed by a dedicated formal model of ant antennation.
