# Autopoiesis

## Overview
Autopoiesis (Greek for self-production) is the organizational definition of living systems proposed by Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela in the early 1970s. An autopoietic system is a network of processes that produces the components that, through their interactions, regenerate that same network and constitute it as a distinct unity in space.

## Mechanism
The defining idea is organizational closure of production: components are made by processes that depend on those same components. A cell is the standard example: metabolic reactions synthesize the molecules, including the membrane lipids, that form the boundary which in turn keeps those reactions localized and concentrated. Maturana and Varela distinguish organization (the relations that define the system's identity) from structure (the particular components realizing it); a system can change its structure while conserving its organization, but loses identity if organization is lost. Interaction with the environment is described as structural coupling: the environment triggers but does not specify changes in the system. They further claimed that living and cognition coincide, which became a founding commitment of enactivism (Varela, Thompson and Rosch 1991).

Several related formalisms address the same territory: Rosen's (M,R)-systems and Kauffman's autocatalytic sets define closure in terms of catalysis, and chemoton models by Ganti combine metabolism, template replication, and membrane. Di Paolo added adaptivity, the system's ability to regulate its own conditions of viability, as a necessary extension if autopoiesis is to ground sense-making. The idea has also been applied to social systems by Luhmann, a use that is disputed by Maturana.

## Role in Active Inference
Autopoiesis and the free-energy principle address the same question, how a bounded system persists, and the link is real but contested. Friston (2013) describes a Markov blanket separating internal from external states, with a system that remains in a non-equilibrium steady state as self-evidencing, which resembles autopoietic self-maintenance. Kirchhoff and colleagues (2018) argued that the framework can give autopoiesis a formal, probabilistic reading. Critics respond that the Markov blanket is a statistical partition, not a physical boundary produced by the system itself, so the formalism does not by itself capture material self-production. The relation should therefore be read as a productive analogy and a research program, not an established equivalence.
