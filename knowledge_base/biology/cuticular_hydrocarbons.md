# Cuticular Hydrocarbons

## Overview
Cuticular hydrocarbons (CHCs) are lipids of long carbon chains that coat the outer surface of the insect cuticle. Their primary function is physiological: the wax layer reduces water loss through the cuticle, which is a central problem for small terrestrial animals. In many species the same compounds were also recruited as signals, and in social insects they carry information about species, colony, caste and reproductive status.

## Chemistry and variation
CHC profiles are mixtures that usually include straight-chain alkanes, methyl-branched alkanes and unsaturated alkenes. A profile is characterised by which compounds are present and their relative amounts, and differences in profile can be detected chemically even between neighbouring colonies of the same species. Profiles depend on genetic factors and on environment, including diet and nest material, so a colony's signature is partly inherited and partly acquired (Blomquist & Bagnères, 2010).

## Nestmate recognition
Ants contact one another with their antennae and compare the profile of an encountered individual with an internal template of the colony odour (see [[antennation]]). A mismatch tends to elicit aggression, and a match tolerance. A widely used account, the gestalt model, proposes that the colony odour is a blend that members continuously share and mix, for example through grooming and food exchange, so that all nestmates carry a similar overall profile. Recognition is not perfectly categorical; thresholds are context dependent and can change with experience, and recognition errors occur.

## Other signalling roles
In some species, CHC composition differs between egg-laying queens and workers, and changes in the profile can signal fertility or reproductive dominance. CHCs also function in parasitism and mimicry, because social parasites and some myrmecophiles can evade detection by acquiring or imitating host profiles (Hölldobler & Wilson, 1990).

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is an analogy. Recognition can be described as inference about group membership from noisy chemical evidence, with a colony template acting as a prior, but this is an interpretive framing rather than an established Active Inference model.
