# Vesicle Traffic

## Overview
Vesicle traffic, also called membrane trafficking, is the movement of material between membrane-bound compartments of eukaryotic cells in small carriers known as vesicles or tubular-vesicular intermediates. A carrier buds from a donor membrane, travels, usually along cytoskeletal tracks, and fuses with an acceptor membrane, delivering both lumenal cargo and membrane lipids and proteins. The scheme explains how organelles of different composition exchange material while keeping their identities (Alberts et al., 2015).

## Components of the process
- **Budding and coats.** Coat protein complexes, among them COPII at the endoplasmic reticulum, COPI in the Golgi and retrieval pathways, and clathrin at the plasma membrane and trans-Golgi network, deform the membrane and select cargo.
- **Targeting.** Small GTPases of the Rab family, along with tethering factors, help match carriers to their destination.
- **Fusion.** Pairing of SNARE proteins on vesicle and target membranes draws the bilayers together. Rothman (1994) summarised the evidence for this machinery, including the role of cytosolic factors that disassemble SNARE complexes after fusion.
- **Routes.** The secretory pathway carries newly synthesised proteins from the endoplasmic reticulum through the Golgi to the plasma membrane (Palade, 1975). Endocytosis and recycling carry material in, and a route leads to lysosomes for degradation. Genetic screens in yeast identified many of the genes involved, which are conserved in animals.

## Specialised cases
In neurons, synaptic vesicles undergo a local cycle of filling with transmitter, docking, calcium-triggered fusion and endocytic retrieval (see [[transmitter_release]]). Regulated secretion in endocrine cells follows a similar logic. Defects in trafficking components are associated with a range of diseases, but the mechanisms are specific to each.

## Relation to Active Inference
The vault cites vesicle traffic as an example of complex, regulated flow in cellular systems. It has no direct connection with Active Inference. A loose parallel can be drawn between the maintenance of distinct compartment identities and the Markov blanket idea of separating internal from external states, but that is an analogy, not a theoretical claim.
