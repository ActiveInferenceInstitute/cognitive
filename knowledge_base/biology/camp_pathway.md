# cAMP Pathway

## Overview
Cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) is a second messenger that relays signals from cell-surface receptors to the interior of the cell. The pathway was first characterised in the study of hormone action by Earl Sutherland and colleagues, and it is now known to regulate metabolism, gene expression and neuronal function.

## Core cascade
Binding of a ligand to a G protein-coupled receptor that couples to the stimulatory G protein Gs activates adenylyl cyclase, which converts ATP to cAMP. In its inactive state, protein kinase A (PKA) is a tetramer of two regulatory and two catalytic subunits. cAMP binds the regulatory subunits and releases the catalytic subunits, which phosphorylate target proteins on serine and threonine residues. Receptors coupled to the inhibitory protein Gi lower cAMP production. Phosphodiesterases degrade cAMP, and so shape the amplitude and duration of the signal. Anchoring proteins confine PKA near particular targets, creating local signalling compartments within a cell.

## Role in plasticity and memory
In neurons, PKA phosphorylates ion channels, receptors and synaptic proteins and, after translocating to the nucleus, can activate the transcription factor CREB. Work on the sea slug Aplysia showed that serotonin acting through cAMP and PKA supports short-term and, with repeated exposure, long-term facilitation of synaptic connections underlying sensitisation. Related roles in mammalian hippocampal long-term potentiation and in memory consolidation have been described, often alongside the MAP kinase pathway (see [[mapk_pathway]]) and calcium signalling.

## Relation to Active Inference
There is no derivation of Active Inference from the cAMP pathway. Neuromodulators such as dopamine act partly through G protein-coupled receptors that raise or lower cAMP, and some computational accounts interpret neuromodulation as setting the precision or gain of prediction errors. The cAMP cascade is therefore a candidate molecular substrate for such precision control, but that mapping is a hypothesis and the molecular details are not part of standard Active Inference models.
