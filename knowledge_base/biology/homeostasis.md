# Homeostasis

## Overview

Homeostasis is the biological process by which living systems maintain internal stability despite external perturbations. Under the Free Energy Principle, homeostasis is understood as a fundamental consequence of self-organization — systems that persist must actively resist the dissipation of their characteristic states, effectively minimizing surprise by keeping essential variables within viable bounds.

## Key Concepts

### Classical Homeostasis
- Maintenance of physiological variables (temperature, pH, glucose) within set ranges
- Negative feedback loops as the primary regulatory mechanism
- Walter Cannon's original formulation and its modern extensions

### Allostatic Regulation
- Anticipatory regulation that adjusts setpoints based on predicted demands
- "Stability through change" — proactive rather than purely reactive control
- Detailed in [[allostatic_regulation|allostatic regulation]]

### Homeostasis and the FEP
- Homeostatic regulation as free energy minimization over physiological states
- Interoceptive inference: the brain's model of bodily states
- Active inference accounts of autonomic regulation
- Deep treatment in [[knowledge_base/free_energy_principle/biology/homeostasis|FEP Homeostasis]]

### Multi-Scale Homeostasis
- Cellular homeostasis: ion channels, membrane potential
- Organ-level regulation: cardiovascular, endocrine systems
- Organismic homeostasis: thermoregulation, energy balance
- Social homeostasis: group-level regulation through collective behavior
