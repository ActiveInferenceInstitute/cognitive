# Adaptive Capacity

## Overview
Adaptive capacity is the ability of a system to adjust to change, moderate potential damage, take advantage of opportunities and cope with consequences. The term is used in ecology, in the study of social-ecological systems and in climate-change research, where the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change defines it in similar terms. In organisms, closely related notions are phenotypic plasticity and evolvability. The concept is distinct from adaptation itself, which is the actual adjustment made, since capacity describes the potential to adjust.

## Components
Studies of social-ecological systems (Walker et al., 2004; Folke, 2006) describe adaptive capacity as depending on features such as:
- **Diversity.** Biological, functional and response diversity provide alternative ways of maintaining function when conditions shift.
- **Learning and knowledge.** Systems that monitor change and incorporate experience can revise their responses, as in adaptive management ([[adaptive_management]]).
- **Flexibility and resources.** Capacity depends on the ability to reorganise and the availability of material, social or genetic resources.
- **Connectivity and governance.** Linked components allow recovery and redistribution but, if too tightly coupled, can propagate shocks.

## Relation to resilience and vulnerability
In resilience thinking, adaptive capacity is part of resilience: Walker and colleagues distinguish resilience, the capacity to absorb disturbance and retain function, from adaptability, the capacity of actors to manage resilience, and from transformability, the capacity to create a fundamentally new system when the existing one is untenable (see [[resilience]], [[resilience_thinking]]). In the climate-change literature, Smit and Wandel (2006) treat adaptive capacity as a determinant of vulnerability along with exposure and sensitivity. Gunderson and Holling (2002) place adaptive capacity within the adaptive cycle of panarchy. Measuring it is difficult, and indicators are often context-specific and contested.

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is an interpretive analogy. In the free energy framework, a system that persists maintains itself within viable states by updating its model and acting on its environment, so a system with greater capacity to revise its model and expand its repertoire of actions can be viewed as better able to keep its states within bounds under novel perturbations. Applications to regional systems, such as the state-space treatment in [[bioregional_state_space]], use this idea as a modelling stance and do not offer an established empirical measure.
