# BDNF

## Overview
Brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) is a small secreted protein of the neurotrophin family, which also includes nerve growth factor, NT-3 and NT-4. It was purified from pig brain by Barde, Edgar and Thoenen (1982). It is widely expressed in the central nervous system, with high levels in the hippocampus and cortex, and it affects neuronal survival, growth and the formation and strength of synapses.

## Signalling
BDNF is synthesised as a precursor, proBDNF, that can be cleaved to the mature form. Mature BDNF binds the receptor tyrosine kinase TrkB and activates intracellular pathways that include MAPK/ERK, PI3K/Akt and PLC-gamma, leading to changes in gene expression and protein synthesis. ProBDNF preferentially engages the p75 neurotrophin receptor, whose effects can oppose those of TrkB signalling. Release is regulated by neuronal activity, so BDNF links patterns of activity to structural and functional change (Park & Poo, 2013).

## Role in plasticity and learning
BDNF promotes the late phase of long-term potentiation in the hippocampus and influences dendritic spine growth (see [[ltp]], [[synaptic_plasticity]]). Its expression rises after neural activity and in rodents after physical exercise, and it contributes to adult hippocampal neurogenesis (see [[neurogenesis]]). A common human variant, Val66Met, reduces activity-dependent secretion and was associated with poorer episodic memory and altered hippocampal function in an early study (Egan et al., 2003). Later work on its behavioural effect sizes has produced mixed results.

## Stress and disease
Chronic stress lowers BDNF expression in parts of the rodent hippocampus and prefrontal cortex, and antidepressant treatment tends to raise it. These observations motivated a neurotrophic hypothesis of depression, which remains debated, and measurements of BDNF in human blood are hard to interpret because platelets carry much of it and peripheral levels may not reflect brain levels.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is indirect. Active Inference treats learning as updating of model parameters, which at the neural level is generally associated with synaptic plasticity. BDNF is one of the molecular regulators of that plasticity, but the framework does not specify molecular detail, and no formal model assigns BDNF a role in parameter updates.
