# Biomechanics

## Overview
Biomechanics studies the structure and function of living things using the methods of mechanics. It asks how forces act on and within organisms, how tissues deform, how bodies move through air, water and over surfaces, and how these properties influence form and behaviour. It spans scales from molecular motors and cells to whole animals and plants.

## Principal topics
- **Solid mechanics of tissues**: bone, tendon, cartilage and skin are characterised by stress, strain, stiffness and strength. Many biological materials are viscoelastic, so their response depends on loading rate.
- **Muscle mechanics**: muscle generates force that depends on its length and shortening velocity. A. V. Hill's characteristic relation between force and shortening speed is a foundation of the field.
- **Fluid mechanics**: swimming, flying and blood flow depend on viscous and inertial forces, and the Reynolds number indicates which dominate. Insect flight at small size involves unsteady flow effects.
- **Locomotion and energetics**: gaits, elastic energy storage and mechanical efficiency are analysed with models ranging from inverted pendulums to full musculoskeletal simulations.
- **Scaling**: many mechanical properties change systematically with body size, which constrains design.

Measurements combine motion capture, force plates, imaging, flow visualisation and computational modelling.

## Relation to Active Inference
The body's mechanics set the dynamics that a nervous system must control and predict. Embodied and enactive views stress that part of the computation is carried out by morphology, so that a well-matched body simplifies control. In Active Inference, movement is described as the fulfilment of proprioceptive predictions through reflex arcs, with the body and environment forming the generative process the agent's model approximates. This framing is an active research area in motor control, though most biomechanical modelling uses conventional mechanics and optimal control rather than free energy methods.
