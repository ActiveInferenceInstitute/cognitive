# Magnetic Sense

## Overview
Magnetoreception is the detection of the Earth's magnetic field by an organism, which may use it as a compass, as a source of positional information, or both. Evidence for it comes from behavioural experiments in many animals. Migratory birds have been studied most, beginning with the work of Wiltschko and Wiltschko showing that European robins orient using the magnetic field. Their compass is an inclination compass, which senses the angle of the field lines relative to gravity, rather than distinguishing polarity.

## Proposed mechanisms
Two main hypotheses are considered. The radical-pair hypothesis proposes that light-dependent chemical reactions in photoreceptor proteins, such as cryptochromes, produce pairs of molecules with correlated electron spins whose reaction products are influenced by the field's direction, so that field direction modulates a chemical signal (Ritz, Adem and Schulten, 2000). The magnetite hypothesis proposes that small iron-containing particles in cells act as tiny magnets that transmit forces or torques to receptors. Johnsen and Lohmann review how these and other proposals fit the physics and neurobiology; neither is fully established, and different species may use different mechanisms. The sensory cells and neural pathways remain incompletely characterised.

## Insects
In social insects, behavioural evidence suggests that honeybees and some ants and termites respond to magnetic fields, for example in orientation of the comb or navigation to a nest, but results vary and some are difficult to replicate (Wajnberg et al., 2010). Magnetic information is probably one cue among others, such as the sun compass, polarised light and landmarks, that are combined during navigation (see [[path_integration]]). Its importance relative to other cues in natural conditions is still being worked out.

## Relation to Active Inference
Magnetic sense is a sensory modality, and sensory biology does not depend on a theory of inference. In a predictive framework, a magnetic compass reading would be one cue integrated with other cues into a belief about heading, with weights set by its reliability. This is a general principle of cue integration in Bayesian models of perception; no dedicated Active Inference model of magnetoreception is established.
