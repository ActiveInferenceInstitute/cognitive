# Neural Circuits

## Overview
A neural circuit is a set of neurons connected by synapses in a pattern that implements a function, such as detecting motion, generating a rhythm, or selecting an action. Circuits are the level of description between single cells ([[single_unit]]) and whole-brain systems, and they are where connectivity is linked to computation ([[neural_computation]]).

## Motifs and examples
A small number of motifs recur across brain regions. Feedforward excitation propagates a signal to the next stage. Feedforward inhibition sharpens timing by letting inhibition arrive shortly after excitation. Feedback (recurrent) inhibition limits activity and underlies gain control. Lateral inhibition, in which active neurons suppress their neighbours, enhances contrast and implements competition. Recurrent excitation supports persistent activity and attractor dynamics. Disinhibition, in which an interneuron class inhibits another inhibitory class, lets signals gate others; in cortex, VIP interneurons inhibiting somatostatin interneurons is a well-studied example.

Specific circuits include central pattern generators in spinal cord ([[locomotion]]), the hippocampal trisynaptic loop (dentate gyrus to CA3 to CA1), cortico-basal ganglia loops involved in action selection ([[action_selection]]), and cerebellar circuits for timing and error correction. The complete wiring diagram of the 302-neuron nervous system of *C. elegans* was mapped by White and colleagues in 1986, yet knowing the connectivity did not by itself explain behavior, since synaptic strengths, neuromodulators and cell-intrinsic properties also matter.

Mammalian neocortex has a layered structure with recurring cell types, and the idea of a canonical cortical microcircuit, developed by Douglas and Martin, proposes that a stereotyped local circuit is repeated across areas. Tools for studying circuits include paired recordings ([[electrophysiology]]), optogenetic manipulation, and connectomics from electron microscopy.

## Role in Active Inference
Active Inference makes a concrete proposal for cortical circuitry. Bastos and colleagues mapped the message passing of [[predictive_coding]] onto the canonical microcircuit: superficial pyramidal cells encode prediction errors and send them forward, deep pyramidal cells encode expectations and send predictions backward, and inhibitory interneurons mediate precision-weighting ([[precision_weighting]]). The proposal is consistent with some laminar and frequency-specific data, but its details remain under test and are contested.
