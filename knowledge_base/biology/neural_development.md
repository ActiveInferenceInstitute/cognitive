# Neural Development

## Overview
Neural development is the process by which a nervous system is built from the embryonic ectoderm to a mature organ with billions of connected cells. It combines genetically specified programmes with activity-dependent refinement, and it provides the biological substrate for the stages described in [[cognitive_development]].

## Stages
Neurulation forms the neural tube, which in humans closes within the first month after conception. Cells of the tube proliferate and generate neurons and glia (neurogenesis and gliogenesis); regional identity is set by gradients of signalling molecules, which act by switching on region-specific [[gene_expression]]. Newly born neurons migrate to their final positions, in cortex building the six layers largely in an inside-out order. Axons then grow toward targets guided by attractive and repulsive cues at the growth cone, and synapses form by contact (synaptogenesis).

Initial wiring is imprecise and over-produced. Connections are then refined by activity: spontaneous retinal waves and later visual experience sharpen the maps of the visual system, and the principle that correlated activity stabilizes synapses is Hebbian ([[hebbian_learning]]). Synapse number in human cortex rises rapidly in early childhood and then declines through [[pruning]], with timing that differs by region. Many neurons also die by programmed cell death, which matches neuron number to target size. [[myelination]] continues for years after.

Sensitivity to experience is not constant. [[critical_periods]] are windows in which input strongly shapes particular circuits, for example ocular dominance in the visual cortex; their closing is associated with maturation of inhibition and extracellular matrix. Adult neurogenesis exists in restricted regions in rodents; its extent in adult humans is disputed. Disruption of these processes is studied in neurodevelopmental conditions, where research is ongoing and mechanisms heterogeneous.

## Role in Active Inference
Active Inference frames development as structure learning: the brain's generative model grows and is simplified as evidence accumulates. Bayesian model reduction has been proposed as a formal analogue of pruning, removing parameters that do not improve model evidence ([[friston_2017_curiosity]], [[structure_learning]]). This is an analogy between a Bayesian procedure and anatomy, and does not establish that biological pruning computes model evidence. Developmental timing and critical periods are also modelled as changes in prior precision.
