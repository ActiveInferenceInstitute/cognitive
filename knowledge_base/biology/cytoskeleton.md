# Cytoskeleton

## Overview
The cytoskeleton is a dynamic system of protein polymers that spans the cytoplasm of eukaryotic cells. It is not a fixed scaffold: its filaments continually assemble and disassemble, and this turnover lets cells change shape, crawl, divide and rearrange their contents in response to signals. Bacteria have related filament systems, but the term usually refers to the eukaryotic arrangement.

## Components
- **Actin filaments** (microfilaments), about 7 nm in diameter, are polymers of the protein actin. They concentrate beneath the plasma membrane, drive cell crawling and shape changes, and with myosin generate force in muscle contraction and in the constriction that divides a cell.
- **Intermediate filaments**, about 10 nm in diameter, are built from a family of tissue-specific proteins including keratins and lamins. They are rope-like and bear mechanical stress, and they lack polarity and associated motors.
- **Microtubules**, about 25 nm in diameter, are hollow tubes of tubulin that radiate from organising centres. They form the mitotic spindle and act as tracks for transport.

Microtubules show dynamic instability, in which individual polymers switch stochastically between growth and rapid shrinkage (Mitchison & Kirschner, 1984). Motor proteins use the energy of ATP hydrolysis to move along filaments: kinesins and dyneins on microtubules, and myosins on actin (see [[molecular_motors]]). In neurons, microtubule-based transport carries vesicles and organelles along axons that may be very long relative to the cell body.

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is indirect. The cytoskeleton is part of the physical machinery that maintains cell form and boundaries, and work on cellular models in the free energy framework treats cell shape and pattern as maintained by self-organising dynamics (see [[cellular_intelligence]]). Claims that cytoskeletal polymers carry out quantum computation relevant to consciousness are speculative and contested, and are not part of the Active Inference framework.
