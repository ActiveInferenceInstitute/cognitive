# Age Polyethism

## Overview
Age polyethism, or temporal polyethism, is a form of division of labour in which individual workers perform different tasks at different ages. In many social insect colonies, young workers stay within the nest and care for brood or the queen, middle-aged workers handle food and nest maintenance, and the oldest workers leave to forage or defend the nest. Wilson (1971) treated this as one of the main organising principles of insect societies.

## Patterns and mechanisms
- **Honeybees.** A worker typically begins with cell cleaning, then brood feeding as a nurse, then food storage and nest tasks, and finally foraging in the last weeks of her life. Seeley (1982) argued that the schedule is adaptive because the riskiest task, foraging, is left to the oldest workers, whose remaining expected lifetime is shortest.
- **Ants.** Many species show a similar centrifugal pattern, with younger workers close to the brood and older ones working at the periphery and outside, though the details differ widely among species.
- **Physiological control.** In honeybees, the transition to foraging is associated with rising juvenile hormone titres, and social interactions modulate its timing (Robinson, 1992).
- **Flexibility.** Age is a statistical tendency and is not a rigid programme. Experiments that change the age composition of a colony show that workers can speed up, delay or reverse their development, so that precocious foragers appear when older ones are scarce (Huang & Robinson, 1996). Task choice depends on individual thresholds and local cues as well (see [[response_thresholds]]).
- **Relation to caste.** Age-based roles are distinct from morphological castes, in which workers differ in size or shape.

## Relation to Active Inference
The link is an active modelling interest and not a result. Colonies can be described as distributed inference systems in which workers respond to local cues about colony needs, and age-linked tendencies would then be a structured prior over which tasks an individual samples. This framing, discussed in [[active_inference_social_insects]], is a hypothesis for model building, and the empirical biology of age polyethism does not depend on it.
