# Design Science

## Overview

"Design science" has more than one meaning, and the phrase "comprehensive anticipatory design science" belongs to Buckminster Fuller (1895-1983). Fuller used it for the practice of identifying large-scale trends in resources, technology and population, and designing artifacts and systems that serve more people with fewer resources, in advance of crises. The other common senses are Herbert Simon's "science of design" in *The Sciences of the Artificial* (1969), which treats design as the study of how to devise artifacts to attain goals, and design science research in information systems, a methodology for building and evaluating artifacts. This page concerns Fuller's usage unless stated.

## Fuller's programme

Fuller's argument, developed in books such as *Operating Manual for Spaceship Earth* (1969) and *Critical Path* (1981), was that the planet is a closed system with finite material resources and an effectively open energy supply from the sun, and that technological design could raise the share of humanity served by those resources. He called the trend toward doing more with less *ephemeralization*. Several concrete projects belong to the programme: the geodesic dome, the Dymaxion house, car and map (see [[knowledge_base/research/dymaxion_systems]]), and tensegrity structures (see [[knowledge_base/research/tensegrity_systems]]). The World Design Science Decade (1965-1975) was a programme Fuller proposed to the International Union of Architects in the early 1960s, and the World Game was a resource-planning exercise he proposed for exploring global allocation strategies. His geometry, *synergetics*, is covered in [[knowledge_base/systems/synergetics]].

Fuller's claims were often broad and stated without conventional proof. His engineering designs were built and some were widely adopted, as with geodesic domes, while his larger predictions about resource sufficiency and design-led social change were not borne out in the form he envisaged. A balanced reading treats the programme as a source of design heuristics and a framing of global problems, not a validated theory.

## Connection to Active Inference

Fuller wrote decades before the free energy principle, and no historical link exists. The connection is an analogy, which this repository explores. "Anticipatory" corresponds loosely to acting on a generative model that predicts future states, and in Active Inference planning selects actions by expected free energy, so design under uncertainty has an explicit form. "Comprehensive" corresponds to drawing system boundaries wide enough to include the relevant feedbacks, which is a modelling decision comparable to choosing the boundary in a systems model. The pairing of resource efficiency with adaptive stability echoes the trade-off between complexity (cost) and accuracy in variational free energy, though that formal correspondence is suggestive and no more. Treating the analogy as a derivation would overstate it.
