# Scale-Free Networks

## Overview
A scale-free network is one whose degree distribution, the fraction of nodes with $k$ connections, follows a power law, $P(k)\sim k^{-\gamma}$. Such a distribution has no characteristic scale: a few hubs have very many links while most nodes have few. Reported exponents for empirical networks are often in the range $2<\gamma<3$, where the variance of the degree diverges in the large-network limit. This contrasts with random graphs of the Erdos-Renyi type, whose degrees are concentrated around the mean.

## Generative mechanism
Barabasi and Albert proposed that scale-free structure emerges from growth combined with preferential attachment. New nodes arrive one at a time and link to existing nodes with probability proportional to their current degree, $\Pi(k_i)\propto k_i$, giving $\gamma=3$ in the basic model. The mechanism is a rich-get-richer process, and variants with nonlinear attachment, node fitness or rewiring change the exponent and can remove the power law.

## Consequences
Hubs make scale-free networks tolerant of random node failure, because most nodes are peripheral, and vulnerable to targeted removal of the highest-degree nodes. Hubs also shorten paths and affect spreading processes, in some models lowering the epidemic threshold. Whether real networks actually are scale-free is contested. Broido and Clauset analysed several hundred empirical networks with rigorous statistical fitting and concluded that strictly scale-free structure is rare, though heavy-tailed degrees are common. Distinguishing power laws from log-normal or other heavy-tailed forms is difficult with limited data.

## Relation to Active Inference
The connection is indirect. Active Inference does not predict scale-free structure. Heavy-tailed connectivity, hubs and modular organisation are reported in brain networks, and these properties interact with the message passing that implements inference on a hierarchical model. Analyses such as [[bayesian_renormalization]] consider how structure repeats across scales, which resembles scale-free reasoning, though the topic there is different. Treat any claim that the free energy principle implies scale-free networks as speculative.
