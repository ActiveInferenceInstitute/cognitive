# Organizational Closure

## Overview
Organizational closure is the property of a system whose constituent processes are organised as a network in which each process is enabled by others in the network, and the network as a whole produces and sustains the components required to carry out those processes. Closure here concerns the organisation of relations, not the exchange of matter and energy: a living cell is thermodynamically open, but its metabolism is closed in the sense that it regenerates the molecular machinery that performs the metabolism. The concept is central to theories of autonomy and life in the tradition of Maturana and Varela.

## Origins and variants
Maturana and Varela defined an autopoietic system as a network of processes of production of components that, through their interactions and transformations, continuously regenerate and realise the network that produced them, and that constitute it as a unity in the space in which it exists. Varela later generalised the idea to operational closure for other kinds of autonomous systems, including the immune and nervous systems, where the network is one of interdependent processes. Related proposals include Robert Rosen's closure to efficient causation in (M,R)-systems and the closure of constraints developed by Moreno and Mossio.

## Implications
- **Autonomy.** A closed organisation defines its own identity and so gives a non-arbitrary sense of what counts as self and environment.
- **Norms and viability.** Because the system must maintain itself, its interactions can be evaluated as good or bad for it, a basis for biological value.
- **Control.** The system's own organisation specifies how it responds to perturbations, which links closure to structural coupling.

Critics ask whether closure can be formalised and measured in real organisms.

## Relation to Active Inference
The relation is contested. Some authors argue that a system with a Markov blanket that maintains itself through active states shows a probabilistic counterpart of autopoietic closure, and that free energy minimisation describes self-maintenance. Others dispute that statistical separation by a blanket captures organisational closure, since closure is about causal production of components and the blanket formalism does not by itself specify this. Treat any identification as an interpretation open to debate.
