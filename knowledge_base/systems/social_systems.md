# Social Systems

## Overview

Social systems are complex adaptive systems composed of interacting agents who coordinate behavior through communication, shared norms, and collective inference. Under the Free Energy Principle, social systems can be understood as ensembles of active inference agents that minimize free energy collectively — maintaining shared generative models (cultural norms, institutions) through mutual prediction and coordinated action.

## Key Concepts

### Shared Generative Models
- Cultural norms as collectively maintained prior beliefs
- Language as a shared generative model for communication
- Institutions as structures for coordinating expectations

### Multi-Agent Active Inference
- Agents modeling each other's beliefs and intentions
- Theory of mind as hierarchical inference about others' generative models
- Related to [[knowledge_base/cognitive/multi_agent_active_inference|multi-agent active inference]]

### Social Niche Construction
- How agents collectively shape their social environment
- Co-evolution of cognitive and social structures
- Connection to [[knowledge_base/biology/niche_construction|niche construction]]

### Collective Intelligence
- Emergent problem-solving capabilities of groups
- Swarm intelligence and distributed cognition
- Related to [[knowledge_base/cognitive/collective_behavior|collective behavior]]

## Applications

- Organizational dynamics and institutional design
- Cultural evolution and transmission
- Economic systems and market dynamics
- Political systems and governance
